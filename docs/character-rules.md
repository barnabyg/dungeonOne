# 5e rules

Dungeon One uses the 2024 fifth-edition rules in SRD 5.2 ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-start-afresh-on-5e-and-suspend-compatibility.md)). This is the game's rules document: character creation, combat, exploration and items, checks, traps and talk, adventure modules and the balance gate, and the house rules and position rules that depart from SRD 5.2.

This work includes material from the System Reference Document 5.2 ("SRD 5.2") by Wizards of the Coast LLC, available at https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd. The SRD 5.2 is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License, available at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.

## In scope

Ability modifiers, proficiency bonus, saving throws, skills, advantage and disadvantage, action, bonus action and reaction, the 5e XP table and SRD 5.2 stat blocks. Feats beyond the level-4 Ability Score Improvement, multiclassing, species and backgrounds' features other than the ability increase are not used.

## Where class rules live (#300)

A class is data. `src/class-5e.ts` holds what every class shares (the abilities, levels, the skill list, the tools, the Fighting Style feats, Weapon Mastery, the Ability Score Improvement) and the shape of a class definition. Each class's definition lives in its own module (the Fighter's in `src/fighter-5e.ts`, the Rogue's in `src/rogue-5e.ts`, #306, the Cleric's in `src/cleric-5e.ts`, #339, the Wizard's in `src/wizard-5e.ts`, #340) and gives:

- its hit die: the die's maximum + the Constitution modifier at level 1, then half the die + 1 + the Constitution modifier for each level after (the Fighter's d10 gives 10, then 6);
- its saving throw proficiencies, and the skills and how many it chooses proficiency in;
- its armour training, weapon proficiencies and tool proficiencies: an attack adds the proficiency bonus only with a weapon the class is proficient with, a whole category (the catalogue marks each weapon simple or martial) or the weapons of a category with a named property (the Rogue's martial weapons with Finesse or Light). The Fighter is trained with all armour and shields and proficient with simple and martial weapons; the Rogue is trained with light armour and proficient with simple weapons, martial weapons with Finesse or Light, and thieves' tools. Armour a class isn't trained with costs it, as SRD 5.2 says (see Weapons and armour). Tool proficiency is recorded and shown on the sheet, and adds the proficiency bonus to a check made with the tool (see Thieves' tools, #309);
- how many kinds of weapon it masters at each level, chosen from the catalogue's weapons whose mastery the game uses that it is proficient with;
- its features by level, each with its uses by level, how spent uses recover, and the effect the engine applies (Second Wind, Action Surge, a critical range, attacks per Attack action, advantage on a skill's checks, the Fighting Style, Weapon Mastery, an Ability Score Improvement, since #306 Sneak Attack and Expertise, and since #315 advantage on initiative and Tactical Mind). A feature may have several effects (Remarkable Athlete has two); a feature with no effect is described on the sheet only;
- its subclasses and their features. The first is taken until there is a choice of subclass;
- its ability priority (how a fresh creation places the rolls, and how the balance harness spends an Ability Score Improvement), its default creation choices and its starting kits;
- for a class that casts (#336), its spellcasting: the ability it casts with, how many cantrips it knows and spells it prepares at each level, its spell slots by level and the spells it may choose from (see Spellcasting);
- for the Cleric (#339), its Divine Orders, one chosen at creation, each adding training, cantrips or a bonus to some skills' checks; and, for a class whose later features are still to come (the Wizard, #340), the highest level it reaches yet (`maxLevel`), below level 5: XP above that level's is kept, and the level waits. The Cleric reaches level 5 from #342, and the Wizard level 3 from #343 and level 5 from #344, so no playable class has one now;
- for the Wizard (#340), how many levelled spells its spellbook holds at creation, and how many each level after 1st adds (#343): it prepares only spells in it.

A class may be **test-only** (#336): creation never offers it and the gate never plays it. The one test-only class, the Test Caster (`src/test-caster-class-5e.ts`), exercises the casting engine until a playable class casts.

`src/character-5e.ts` builds, validates and derives every sheet from its class's definition and never asks which class it is. A sheet names its class by id (`"class": "fighter"`, `"rogue"`, `"cleric"` or `"wizard"`); it has a `fightingStyle` exactly when its class chooses one and an `expertise` list exactly when its class has Expertise (library format 14, #306; a caster's `spells`, library format 16, #336; a Cleric's `divineOrder`, library format 17, #339; a Wizard's `spellbook`, library format 18, #340; a Cleric up to level 3 owing a new level's spells, library format 19, #341; a Cleric up to level 5 owing a new level's cantrip, library format 20, #342; a Wizard up to level 3 owing a new level's spellbook spells or Scholar's Expertise, library format 21, #343; a Wizard up to level 5, library format 22, #344). The browser's creation screen and sheet, the runtime's offered actions and the AI DM's tool descriptions, ability checks, the balance harness, the gate and the career simulation all read the definition. Creation offers the Fighter, the Rogue, the Cleric (#339) or the Wizard (#340); the gate judges every class creation offers (`GATE_CLASSES` is `OFFERED_CLASS_IDS`: the Fighter and the Rogue from #310, the Cleric and the Wizard from #348; see Balance gate), and the career simulation plays any of them. No class list outside the class registry names them.

## Creating a character

Every character is created at level 1 with 0 XP, as a Fighter, a Rogue (#306), a Cleric (#339) or a Wizard (#340), with a starting kit and its class's weapon masteries chosen at creation (below). The creation screen's first choice is the class; choosing one starts its creation afresh from that class's default placement and choices (the dice stay), and the screen then offers that class's skills, kits and masteries, the Fighter's Fighting Style or the Rogue's Expertise. The numbers below are the Fighter's; the Rogue's differences are in The Rogue, level 1. The library refuses any new sheet at another level, with XP, below full health, or not made from its pending dice.

**Ability scores.** The engine rolls 4d6 six times from a seeded stream of its own: SHA-256 of the browser's startup seed and the library's creation number and drops the lowest die of each roll. The six rolls are saved in the character library as a pending creation before the player sees them, and every die, including each dropped one, is shown. While the creation is pending, reloading the page, restarting the server (with any seed) or leaving the creation screen shows the same dice; there are no rerolls and no minimum set. The player places the six results on the six abilities in any order. A fresh creation starts with a default placement: the highest kept total on Strength, then the rest, highest first, in the Fighter priority order Strength, Constitution, Dexterity, Wisdom, Charisma, Intelligence (Constitution for hit points, Dexterity for AC in leather and initiative, Wisdom for Perception and Wisdom saves). Tied totals keep their roll order: the earlier roll takes the higher-priority ability. The default background increase is +2 Strength and +1 Constitution. Saving a character spends its dice; the next creation rolls a new set. Deleting a character and starting again is allowed. A character sheet's **Delete character** control opens a confirmation that says deletion is permanent; the final Delete button is enabled only once the player types the character's name exactly, case and spaces included. Cancel, Escape and closing the confirmation change nothing. There is no undo, archive or recycle bin. Deleting removes only that character: a pending creation keeps its dice, so deletion is never a reroll, and the only way to new dice is still to save a character and start another creation.

**Background increase.** After placement the player adds +2 to one ability and +1 to another, or +1 to three. No score can exceed 20 (4d6 keeps at most 18, so +2 reaches 20 at most). The creation screen places the rolls and the increase in one table, one row per ability. Each row's Roll column shows the four dice of the roll placed there, the dropped one marked, so each roll appears once. Its score, modifier and whether it has reached 20 come from the server, which projects them for the current choices, as it does the two-skill limit; the page computes none of them. The player first picks the increase's shape, then its abilities: with +2 and +1 a bonus moves by swapping with the ability that held it, and with +1 to three no fourth ability can be ticked, so an impossible increase cannot be entered. The server still validates every choice. Backgrounds' other features are not used. Placement and the increase can change until the character is saved; the dice cannot.

**Modifiers.** (score − 10) / 2, rounded down: 3 is −4, 8–9 are −1, 10–11 are +0, 12–13 are +1, 18–19 are +4, 20 is +5.

**Starting kit (#207).** Creation offers each class's named kits of common-tier gear, and no starting coin. The owner chose to start nearly empty-handed (6 October 2026): a character finds better gear in its first dungeons. One class's kits are of equal value within 3 gp (SRD 5.2 prices allow no three exactly equal loadouts); kits of different classes need not be (#306). The Fighter's three:

| Kit                      | Gear                         | Value      | With it                                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mace and leather         | Leather armour, mace         | 15 gp      | One 1d6 bludgeoning attack; Sap when mastered                                         |
| Two daggers and leather  | Leather armour, two daggers  | 14 gp      | A 1d4 piercing attack (finesse), then the light-weapon extra attack, Nick if mastered |
| Club, dagger and leather | Leather armour, club, dagger | 12 gp 1 sp | A 1d4 bludgeoning attack, then the extra attack with the dagger, Nick if mastered     |

The creation screen shows each kit's items, value, AC, attack and damage for the current scores, Fighting Style and masteries before saving; the server projects them. Creation starts with the mace kit. The sheet keeps the equipment as a list, armour first and then the weapon attacked with; any legal loadout is a valid sheet, so gear found later (#209) needs no new format.

**Weapon Mastery (#207).** The 2024 Fighter masters three kinds of weapon, chosen at creation from the catalogue's weapons whose mastery the game uses: dagger (Nick), mace (Sap), shortsword (Vex), longsword (Sap), greatsword (Graze) and shortbow (Vex, #230). The club's, light crossbow's and longbow's Slow needs positions and is not offered. Creation starts with the dagger, mace and shortsword, which covers every kit weapon. Once three are ticked the rest are disabled. A mastery applies only while the character wields that weapon.

## The Rogue, level 1 (#306)

The SRD 5.2 Rogue at level 1; its level 2 and 3 features follow below (#307), and its level 4 and 5 features after them (#308).

| Hit die                   | Saving throws           | Skills (choose 4)                                                                                                        | Armour | Weapons                                           | Tools          | Weapon masteries |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------ | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- |
| d8: 8 + Con, then 5 + Con | Dexterity, Intelligence | Acrobatics, Athletics, Deception, Insight, Intimidation, Investigation, Perception, Persuasion, Sleight of Hand, Stealth | Light  | Simple, and martial weapons with Finesse or Light | Thieves' tools | 2                |

- **Creation.** A fresh Rogue creation places the rolls in the priority Dexterity, Constitution, Wisdom, Charisma, Intelligence, Strength (Dexterity for its Finesse attacks, AC, initiative and Stealth; Wisdom for Perception; Charisma for reaction rolls and talking) and starts with +2 Dexterity and +1 Constitution; Athletics, Perception, Persuasion and Stealth; Expertise in Perception and Stealth; the shortsword and dagger kit; and mastery of the shortsword (Vex) and the dagger (Nick). It chooses no Fighting Style.
- **Expertise.** Two of its skill proficiencies, chosen at creation, double their proficiency bonus: a check with either adds twice the bonus, and Expertise in Perception doubles the bonus in passive Perception. The creation screen offers a tick for each skill proficiency chosen; unticking a skill drops its Expertise. The check card says "+ 4 proficiency (Expertise)", and the sheet marks each such skill "(Expertise)".
- **Sneak Attack.** Once per turn, when the Rogue hits with an attack roll that uses a Finesse or ranged weapon and has advantage on the roll, it deals an extra 1d6 damage of the weapon's type. Advantage comes only from the engine's rules: Vex after a hit, a prone or paralysed target, and the like; advantage and disadvantage together cancel, so no Sneak Attack. The dice follow the weapon's, are doubled by a critical hit, and count in the damage any resistance halves; a miss (Graze included) deals none, and the turn's Sneak Attack is then still to come. It may come from the Light extra attack. The engine applies it whenever its rules are met; the player never asks for it, and the AI DM has no tool or argument for it. The attack card shows it ("Damage 4 + 3 + Sneak Attack 5 = 12 piercing"), and its roll chip marks the Sneak Attack die. SRD 5.2 lets a ranged weapon qualify as well as a Finesse one, and so does the engine. The ally-adjacent clause (an ally within 5 feet of the target instead of advantage) is omitted until companions (increment 17); see Rules that need positions.
- **Weapon Mastery.** Two kinds of weapon, chosen at creation from those whose mastery the game uses that the Rogue is proficient with: dagger (Nick), mace (Sap), shortsword (Vex) and shortbow (Vex).
- **Thieves' Cant.** Flavour only: the Rogue knows the secret cant, and it changes nothing in play.
- **Thieves' tools.** Proficiency is recorded and shown on the sheet ("Tools: Thieves' tools."), and adds the proficiency bonus when the Rogue picks a lock or disarms a trap with them (see Thieves' tools under Checks, traps and talk, #309). Both of the Rogue's kits pack a set.
- **Starting kits.** Both leather armour and a shortsword, the owner's choice (8 October 2026). They are of equal value with each other, and worth more than the Fighter's by design:

| Kit                            | Gear                                               | Value | With it                                                                           |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shortsword, dagger and leather | Leather armour, shortsword, dagger; thieves' tools | 47 gp | A 1d6 piercing attack (Finesse), then the dagger's extra attack, Nick if mastered |
| Shortsword and leather         | Leather armour, shortsword; thieves' tools         | 45 gp | One 1d6 piercing attack (Finesse); Vex when mastered                              |

- **Armour training.** The Rogue is trained with light armour only. A Rogue that finds or buys medium or heavy armour, or a shield, can wear it, at SRD 5.2's cost (see Weapons and armour): disadvantage on Strength and Dexterity rolls in the armour, and no AC from the shield (owner decision, 8 October 2026). Its starting kits are leather, so a fresh Rogue pays nothing.

## The Rogue, levels 2 and 3 (#307)

| Level | XP  | Proficiency bonus | Hit points         | Sneak Attack | Features                                         |
| ----- | --- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 2     | 300 | +2                | + 5 + Con modifier | 1d6          | Cunning Action (Hide, Disengage)                 |
| 3     | 900 | +2                | + 5 + Con modifier | 2d6          | Steady Aim; Thief: Fast Hands, Second-Story Work |

The level-up card lists each new feature (and Sneak Attack's dice when they grow, "Sneak Attack 1d6 → 2d6"). Each feature's name is a disclosure of what it does, the same text as the sheet (#319), open from the start so it reads without a click; the player can still fold it (#387).

- **Cunning Action (level 2): Hide and Disengage.** A bonus action. The engine rolls a Stealth check (Dexterity, with the Rogue's proficiency and Expertise) against the best passive Perception among the opponents still in the fight (the first of them in initiative order on a tie; 10 for an opponent without one). On a success the Rogue is **hidden**: its next attack roll has advantage ("Hidden"), which sets up Sneak Attack. Hiding lasts until the Rogue attacks or the fight ends, so a Rogue may attack and then hide for the next turn's first attack; a hidden Rogue can't hide again. On a failure the bonus action is spent and nothing else happens. Untrained armour and the poisoned condition give the check disadvantage. Each Hide is its own action, so its check is rolled each time it is taken, never rerolled by asking again. **Abstracted:** SRD 5.2's Hide needs cover or heavy obscurement and a DC 15 check, and gives the Invisible condition; without positions or lighting there is no cover to find, so the check is against the foes' passive Perception instead, and hiding gives only the advantage on the next attack: it doesn't give attackers disadvantage, and foes still attack the Rogue as usual. **Dash is omitted:** with no movement it would do nothing, so it is not offered. **Disengage** (#396) is a bonus action too: once taken, fleeing the fight later that turn draws no attacks (see Fleeing a fight). It does nothing else, so it is offered only while the action that a flight takes is left and some way out of the room is open to flee by; with none (no exit, or each behind a shut door or a closed way), it is refused ("No way out": "No way out of here is open, so you can't flee: disengaging would do nothing."), and the AI DM is offered no `disengage` tool.
- **Steady Aim (level 3).** A bonus action, offered while the Rogue still has an attack to make this turn (its action, or the Nick extra attack that follows one); it gives advantage on the Rogue's next attack roll this turn ("Steady Aim"), and the advantage ends with that attack or the turn. SRD 5.2 allows it only if the Rogue hasn't moved this turn, and sets its speed to 0. With no positions the Rogue never moves, so the condition always holds and it can be used every turn (owner decision, 8 October 2026). Steady Aim and Hide each take the bonus action, so a turn has at most one of them; a Rogue already hidden may still take Steady Aim, and one attack then spends both.
- **Sneak Attack 2d6 (level 3).** Still once per turn, however many attacks have advantage.
- **Thief (level 3).** The Rogue's only subclass so far, so a Rogue reaching level 3 is a Thief; its features are named "Thief: …" on the sheet.
  - **Fast Hands.** SRD 5.2 lets the bonus action make a Sleight of Hand check, use thieves' tools, or take the Utilize action. In a fight the engine applies the last: once the turn's object interaction is spent, the Thief can draw, stow or swap a weapon again with its bonus action ("using your bonus action (Fast Hands)"). Sleight of Hand checks and thieves' tools are used only outside fights, where nothing is timed by actions, so there Fast Hands changes nothing.
  - **Second-Story Work.** SRD 5.2 gives a climb speed and jumps measured by Dexterity. Without positions there is no speed or distance; instead a module marks a Strength check (Athletics or plain Strength) that climbs or jumps with `"movement": "climb"` or `"jump"` (format 26), and a Thief makes it with Dexterity in place of Strength, keeping its Athletics proficiency ("Athletics check (Dexterity, Second-Story Work)"). Every other character, and the Thief on an unmarked check such as forcing a door, uses Strength. Wolfstone Hillfort's rope bridge (Athletics) and the evaluation Obstacle Yard's wall (Athletics) are marked as climbs.
- **The AI DM** has a `hide` and a `steady_aim` tool, each offered only while the engine would accept it ("I hide behind the crates" calls `hide`). The engine alone rolls Hide and decides whether the Rogue is hidden; the DM can't declare it hidden or give it advantage, and a second bonus action in a turn is refused ("You have already used your bonus action this turn."). The fight's action bar shows Hide and Steady Aim in its Rogue features row (#392), and the initiative list marks a hidden combatant "(hidden)".
- **Balance harness.** The harness plays a Rogue's bonus action by policy: Steady Aim before an attack when offered, otherwise Hide before an attack, and Hide after its attacks when the bonus action is still free.

## The Rogue, levels 4 and 5 (#308)

| Level | XP    | Proficiency bonus | Hit points         | Sneak Attack | Features                      |
| ----- | ----- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------ | ----------------------------- |
| 4     | 2,700 | +2                | + 5 + Con modifier | 2d6          | Ability Score Improvement     |
| 5     | 6,500 | +3                | + 5 + Con modifier | 3d6          | Cunning Strike; Uncanny Dodge |

- **Ability Score Improvement (level 4).** As the Fighter's (#286): +2 to one ability or +1 to two, none above 20, chosen on the sheet's level-up card after settling. The Rogue masters two kinds of weapon at every level, so unlike the Fighter's its level 4 brings no new mastery: the card asks for the improvement alone ("Level 4: choose an Ability Score Improvement"), and a choice that names a mastery is refused. Until it is made the character owes it, saved in the library, and can't start another adventure ("Vex must choose the level 4 Ability Score Improvement on the character sheet before starting another adventure.").
- **Cunning Strike (level 5).** When the Rogue deals Sneak Attack, it may give up Sneak Attack dice for one effect; the dice go before the damage is rolled (doubled, like the rest, on a critical hit), and the effect follows the damage. Its saving throws are against 8 + the Rogue's Dexterity modifier + its proficiency bonus.
  - **Poison** (1d6): the target makes a Constitution saving throw or is poisoned (disadvantage on its attack rolls and checks) for up to 10 of its turns, SRD 5.2's minute, repeating the save at the end of each.
  - **Trip** (1d6): a target that is Large or smaller makes a Dexterity saving throw or is knocked prone. As any prone combatant, it gets up at the end of its next turn.
  - **Withdraw** moves the Rogue, and there are no positions: it is omitted.
  - **When it is chosen.** The effect is named with the attack, and offered only while the attack would deal Sneak Attack if it hits: a Finesse or ranged weapon, advantage without disadvantage, and no Sneak Attack dealt yet this turn. A miss spends nothing, and Sneak Attack is still to come that turn. One effect per attack, so one per Sneak Attack. It is not offered against a target immune to the condition (an undead can't be poisoned), and Trip not against a Huge or Gargantuan one. SRD 5.2's Poison needs a Poisoner's Kit, which the game doesn't have, so it is free (agent choice, #308).
  - **The browser.** While some attack would deal Sneak Attack, a Cunning Strike choice ("None") heads the fight's Attack row (#392), offering Poison (−1d6) and Trip (−1d6). The effect chosen goes with the next attack clicked; an attack it can't go with (another target, an immune one) is disabled with "Not with Trip". The attack's line names it ("Sneak Attack 5 + 6 (Cunning Strike: Trip, 1 Sneak Attack die forgone)"), then the save and the condition ("knocked prone by Vex's Cunning Strike (Trip)").
- **Uncanny Dodge (level 5), the reaction.** When an opponent's attack roll hits the Rogue, the fight waits before the damage is rolled: the Rogue may use its reaction to halve the attack's damage, or take the hit. As in SRD 5.2 the attack's total damage is halved once, rounding down; when it deals two types (a rider's poison as well as a weapon's slashing, say), each type's part is halved rounding down and any point that loses goes to the larger part (the weapon's on a tie), so 7 slashing + 3 poison becomes 4 slashing + 1 poison, 5 in all. Nothing else can be done until it answers; then the opponent's turn and the fight go on. The reaction comes back at the start of the Rogue's turn, so it halves at most one hit a round; taking a hit keeps it for the next one that round. A paralysed Rogue can't react, so its hits land at once. With no positions, darkness or invisible foes, every attacker counts as one the Rogue can see.
  - **The browser** offers it at the moment it applies: the status strip says "Goblin Warrior hits you.", the attack's line gives its roll ("… Hit. Before its damage is rolled, Vex can use Uncanny Dodge to halve it, or take the hit."), and the action bar holds only **Uncanny Dodge** and **Take the hit**. The answer's line gives the damage ("Vex uses Uncanny Dodge from Goblin Warrior's Scimitar. Damage 5 + 2 = 7 slashing, halved to 3 by Uncanny Dodge"), and its roll chip says "halved".
- **The AI DM.** The `attack` and `light_attack` tools take `cunning_strike` (`poison`, `trip` or null) exactly while some target offers it, listing the effects each target offers; the DM gives one only when the player asks for it. While a hit waits, `uncanny_dodge` and `take_hit` are the only action tools, and the scene says what hit the character; neither is offered at any other time (prompt v20). The engine refuses an effect it didn't offer ("Cunning Strike needs Sneak Attack, and this attack has no advantage."), an effect the game doesn't have, any other action while a hit waits ("Goblin Warrior's scimitar has hit Vex: first use Uncanny Dodge to halve its damage, or take the hit.") and an answer with nothing to answer ("Uncanny Dodge answers an attacker's hit, and nothing has hit you just now.").
- **Balance harness.** The harness halves every hit it can with Uncanny Dodge, and adds Cunning Strike to an attack whenever it is offered: Poison on a target not yet poisoned, or else Trip on one not prone. The gate makes a Rogue's level-4 choice by its policy (see Balance harness), so it and the career simulation build and play Rogues up to level 5; since #310 both qualify every shipped module for the Rogue as well as the Fighter (see Balance gate). The Fighter's dice stream is unchanged: no hit waits for a character without Uncanny Dodge.
- **Formats.** The save (34) and trace (28) formats bump: a fight can wait on a hit, and an attack can carry Cunning Strike. The library (14) and module (26) formats are unchanged.

## The Cleric, level 1 (#339)

The SRD 5.2 Cleric at level 1. Its levels 2 and 3 (#341) and 4 and 5 (#342) are below.

| Hit die                   | Saving throws    | Skills (choose 2)                                | Armour                        | Weapons | Weapon masteries |
| ------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | ------- | ---------------- |
| d8: 8 + Con, then 5 + Con | Wisdom, Charisma | History, Insight, Medicine, Persuasion, Religion | Light and medium, and shields | Simple  | None             |

- **Creation.** A fresh Cleric creation places the rolls in the priority Wisdom, Constitution, Strength, Dexterity, Charisma, Intelligence (Wisdom for its spells; Constitution for hit points and concentration; Strength for its mace or club; Dexterity for AC in light armour) and starts with +2 Wisdom and +1 Constitution; Insight and Persuasion; the Protector order; Sacred Flame, Guidance and Resistance, with Bless, Cure Wounds, Guiding Bolt and Healing Word prepared; and the mace kit. It masters no weapon, so the creation screen shows no Weapon Mastery.
- **Divine Order**, chosen at creation (the sheet's `divineOrder`):
  - **Protector**: training with heavy armour and martial weapons, beside the class's own.
  - **Thaumaturge**: one more cantrip known (four at level 1), and its Wisdom modifier, at least +1, added to its Intelligence (Arcana) and Intelligence (Religion) checks. The sheet's skills count it, and the check card names it ("+ 3 (Thaumaturge)"). Arcana, Medicine and Religion joined the skill list with the Cleric.
- **Spellcasting.** Wisdom: spell attack bonus = proficiency + Wisdom modifier, save DC = 8 + both. At level 1 a Cleric knows 3 cantrips (a Thaumaturge 4), prepares 4 spells and has two 1st-level slots (SRD 5.2). The owner's list (D3, 9 October 2026):
  - Cantrips: Sacred Flame, Guidance, Resistance and Thaumaturgy. A Thaumaturge knows all four.
  - 1st level: Bless, Cure Wounds, Guiding Bolt, Healing Word, Inflict Wounds and Shield of Faith.
  - What each does is under Spellcasting.
- **Preparing spells (D8).** The cantrips are chosen once, at creation. The prepared spells are chosen at creation and changed only between adventures, on the character's sheet in the library: a tick for each 1st-level spell on the list and **Prepare spells**, which saves exactly four. During an adventure the sheet says they change only between adventures, and the library refuses a change ("Mira is on an adventure: prepared spells change only between adventures."). The AI DM has no tool for it.
- **Starting kits** (owner's choice, 9 October 2026), of equal value within 3 gp:

| Kit                           | Gear                                               | Value      | With it                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mace, two daggers and leather | Leather armour, mace; two daggers carried (stowed) | 19 gp      | One 1d6 bludgeoning attack; the daggers can be wielded instead |
| Club, shield and leather      | Leather armour, shield, club                       | 20 gp 1 sp | One 1d4 bludgeoning attack, and +2 AC from the shield          |

- **The browser.** Creation offers the Fighter, the Rogue and the Cleric. For the Cleric it offers the Divine Order and a tick for each cantrip and 1st-level spell, with what each does; the count follows the order ("Cantrips 3 of 4 chosen"), and saving waits until both are complete. The sheet shows the spell attack bonus, save DC and slots ("Spell attack +5 · Spell save DC 13 · Wisdom · Spell slots: 2 1st-level."), then the cantrips and prepared spells, each with what it does. The fight and exploring bars offer the prepared spells and cantrips that can be cast there (see Spellcasting).
- **The AI DM.** The prompt says what Guidance, Resistance and Guiding Bolt do, that Thaumaturgy is flavour only (described in words, never cast), and that prepared spells change only between adventures; the cast tool describes each spell it offers (prompt v27).
- **Balance harness.** The harness builds a level-1 Cleric of any percentile from its defaults (levels 2 and 3 from #341, 4 and 5 from #342), and plays it: it heals with its healing spells before a potion (see Balance harness). The gate reports the Cleric, never judging it (see Balance gate).
- **Formats.** The library (17), save (42) and trace (36) formats bump: a sheet may be a Cleric with its Divine Order, a cast may name Resistance's damage type, and effects and fights carry Resistance's turn and Guiding Bolt's mark.

  - **Turn Undead**: each undead opponent still in the fight and not already turned makes a Wisdom saving throw; one that fails is **turned**, Frightened and Incapacitated for the fight's minute (D9), so it can't act. A creature immune to both Frightened and Incapacitated isn't turned. Refused when no undead foe is left to turn ("Turn Undead turns undead, and no undead opponent here is left to turn."). The bestiary's Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul are undead: every combatant now carries its stat block's creature type (`creatureType`: "Undead" is undead, "Fey (Goblinoid)" is fey), and every character is humanoid. | 4     | 2,700 | +2                | + 6 + Con modifier | 3                | 4                | Ability Score Improvement                       |
| 5     | 6,500 | +3                | + 6 + Con modifier | 3                | 4                | Extra Attack (Tactical Shift omitted)           |

Level 5 is the highest supported level (#287); XP above 6,500 is kept. The maximum HP is always derived from the current Constitution modifier, so a Constitution increase raises it for every level, as the 2024 rules say.

**Level 4 choices (#286).** Settling the adventure that takes a character to 2,700 XP credits level 4 at once: its hit points, its third Second Wind use and the healing of 1d10 + 4. The ending's level-up card names these and says two choices wait on the sheet. Until both are made the character owes them: the sheet leads with a level-up card that lists every change the level brings and asks for them, and its adventure list is replaced by a notice; the server refuses to start an adventure for it. The owed choice is part of the sheet in the character library (a level-4 sheet with no Ability Score Improvement and three masteries), so it survives a reload or a restart.

- **Ability Score Improvement:** +2 to one ability score, or +1 to two different ones; no score can rise above 20. The player picks the shape (+2 to one ability, or +1 to two) and then the abilities; an ability the improvement would take past 20 is disabled, and the server refuses such an improvement whatever the page sends ("Strength is 20: an Ability Score Improvement can't raise a score above 20."). Other feats are not used.
- **Fourth weapon mastery:** one more kind of weapon from those whose mastery the game uses (see Weapon Mastery), not already mastered.
- **What changes.** The server projects each score and modifier as the choice is made, and, once both are chosen, lists every number that changes: scores and modifiers, hit points, AC, initiative, each attack's bonus, damage and mastery, saving throws, skills, carrying capacity, and the new mastery with what it does. Confirming makes both choices in one library write; the sheet then shows the new scores, the Ability Score Improvement feature with the increase chosen, and four masteries. The AI DM has no tool for these choices.

**Earning XP and treasure (#133).** XP is credited only when an adventure ends with the character alive, in a victory or an escape. It comes from each encounter won (the sum of the stat-block XP of the opponents defeated in it, plus half, rounded down, of each that fled or surrendered after exchanging blows with the character, and any XP the module awards for sparing each that surrendered; one that fled or surrendered without ever attacking or being attacked gives none but that, #237, #238) and from any XP the ending awards. A fight's award names its opponents as the fight log does, with no article, whether it names one or several (#323): "Defeated Kennel Mastiff; drove off Hesk's Hound", "Defeated Baiting Bear", "Defeated Goblin 1 and Goblin 3; spared Goblin 2". Every other card and refusal names opponents, and creatures to talk to, the same way (#428): "You attack Sergeant Larrow.", "leaving Old Sullage behind", "You already asked Hesk about the burrow.", "Another try at asking Hesk about the burrow (costs 1d4 bludgeoning damage).", "Hesk deals 2 = 2 bludgeoning"; doors, traps, features and rooms keep "the" ("Another try at the Sheer Cliff"). Treasure is an item found by examining a feature, or by searching an opponent's body once its fight is won, or offered by a surrendered opponent (#238), and carried out alive. An opponent that fled leaves no body: what it carried leaves with it. One that surrendered leaves none either, and keeps what it carried unless its module has it hand something over. Each XP award and each treasure is earned once per character: the sheet keeps a ledger of the treasure found and the XP awards credited, apart from what the character holds, and treasure in the ledger is not there to find on a later visit, even once the character no longer holds it. The level follows the XP at once, and the level-up card shows the new hit points and lists the new features, each showing what it does (#319, #387). A defeat or an abandoned adventure credits nothing.

**Coin (#208).** Coin is SRD 5.2 copper, silver and gold pieces (1 sp = 10 cp, 1 gp = 100 cp). A character's purse is held in copper and shown in mixed denominations, largest first ("3 gp 4 sp"); a new character has none. A module authors coin as an item of kind `coin` with its gold, silver and copper pieces, hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, like treasure; the engine, not the AI DM, decides how much it holds. Taking it empties it into the purse at once, and the ledger records it as a find like a treasure, so it is not there to find again. Carrying coin found in the adventure out counts as escaping with loot, even once it is spent. Coin buys gear from merchants inside adventures (#210); there is no market between adventures.

**Settling replaces possessions (#206).** An adventure starts holding the character's possessions: its equipment, the treasure it holds and its purse. Settling a victory or an escape replaces the possessions on the sheet with what the character holds at the end (what it brought and still holds, the treasure it carried out and the coin in its purse), adds its new finds and XP awards to the ledger, and rests it to full health. A defeat or an abandonment leaves the possessions, the ledger and the XP exactly as at the start. Settling happens once, in the same library write that frees the character, so an interruption between the session save and the library write is settled on the next request without duplicating or losing anything.

**Rest between adventures.** A rest between adventures restores every hit point, every feature use and every hit die, so each adventure starts at full health with all uses and hit dice.

- **Armour class:** from the equipment (see Weapons and armour): in a starting kit's leather, 11 + Dexterity modifier; +1 with the Defense style.
- **Initiative:** Dexterity modifier.
- **Attack:** with the first weapon held, the ability modifier + proficiency bonus to hit (+2 more with Archery and a ranged weapon), and the weapon's damage + the ability modifier. A critical hit needs a 20, or 19–20 from level 3.
- **Saving throws:** proficient in Strength and Constitution; the others use the ability modifier alone.
- **Skills:** two of Acrobatics, Animal Handling, Athletics, History, Insight, Intimidation, Perception, Persuasion and Survival. Creation starts with Athletics and Perception. Once two are ticked the rest are disabled and the screen shows "2 of 2 chosen"; unticking one enables them again.
- **Fighting Style:** one of four SRD 5.2 Fighting Style feats, each applying only with the gear it needs (#144, #225):
  - _Archery:_ +2 to attack rolls with a ranged weapon (shortbow, light crossbow, longbow), not to its damage. Melee attacks get nothing, a dagger's included: without positions nothing is thrown. No starting kit holds a ranged weapon, so creation always tags Archery "No effect with this kit"; it applies once a bow or crossbow is the weapon held.
  - _Defense:_ +1 AC while wearing body armour.
  - _Great Weapon Fighting:_ with a two-handed weapon, or a versatile one held in two hands (nothing else in hand, no shield), each 1 or 2 on a damage die counts as 3, critical-hit dice included. A versatile weapon held beside a shield, and every one-handed or light weapon, gets nothing. No starting kit holds such a weapon; it comes from found gear, such as the longsword or greatsword.
  - _Two-Weapon Fighting:_ the light-weapon extra attack adds the ability modifier to its damage, as the first attack does. It changes nothing else.

  The creation screen tags each style "Applies with this kit" or "No effect with this kit" for the kit chosen, with a note saying why; the sheet says, near the top, whether the character's style applies with what it has equipped now, and the Fighting Style feature repeats the note.

- **Second Wind:** a bonus action to regain 1d10 + Fighter level HP, never above maximum HP; 2 uses, 3 from level 4.
- **Weapon Mastery:** three kinds of weapon, chosen at creation (see Starting kit), and a fourth chosen at level 4. Of the SRD masteries, Graze, Nick, Sap, Topple and Vex work without positions; Cleave, Push and Slow are omitted, and no catalogue weapon has Cleave or Push yet. The quarterstaff's Topple (#340) is not used yet: no class here masters a quarterstaff, so it is not offered as a mastery.
- **Action Surge (level 2):** one additional action on your turn, except Magic; 1 use.
- **Tactical Mind (level 2, #315):** when you fail an ability check, you can expend a use of Second Wind to add 1d10 to it instead of healing; the use is kept if the check still fails. See **Tactical Mind** under Exploration.
- **Champion (level 3):** Improved Critical (critical hits on 19–20) and Remarkable Athlete (advantage on initiative and Strength (Athletics) checks; its movement after a critical hit is omitted).
- **Ability Score Improvement (level 4):** see Level 4 choices.
- **Extra Attack (level 5, #287):** the Attack action makes two attacks instead of one, each at any living opponent, so the second may go to another target or follow the first after that target falls. Each attack is its own click or AI DM `attack` call; the first spends the action and the second must follow in the same turn, or it is lost when the turn ends. Action Surge gives a second Attack action with its own two attacks (four in all), and the light-weapon extra attack still follows an attack (three in all, or five with Action Surge). A third attack in one Attack action is refused ("You have already made every attack your Attack actions allow this turn."). A Loading weapon (the light crossbow, #291) fires once an action, whatever the number of attacks the action allows: a shot with it ends the action's attacks, so Extra Attack gives it no second shot and Action Surge one more ("The light crossbow fires once an action (Loading): you have already shot with this action."). Settling at level 5 asks for no choice. A character that jumps from level 3 to 5 in one adventure still owes its level-4 choice.
- **Tactical Shift (level 5): omitted.** It moves the character when it uses Second Wind, and there are no positions.

- **Strength requirements:** heavy armour below its Strength score costs 10 feet of speed in SRD 5.2; without positions the profile records the shortfall and nothing else changes. A heavy weapon attacks with disadvantage below Strength 13 if it is a melee weapon, and below Dexterity 13 if it is a ranged one, the longbow (SRD 5.2; owner decision on #230, 7 October 2026).

## Carrying capacity (#224)

SRD 5.2 sets a Small or Medium creature's carrying capacity at its Strength score × 15 lb. Its only stated consequence is a Speed of 5 feet while dragging, lifting or pushing more, which does nothing without positions, so the owner chose (6 October 2026) that the engine **refuses** whatever would take a character over capacity.

- **What weighs.** Every catalogue item weighs its SRD 5.2 weight (the tables above), equipped or stowed. Arrows weigh 1 lb and bolts 1.5 lb for 20 (SRD 5.2), counted one by one, so 7 arrows weigh 0.35 lb (#230). Coin weighs 50 coins to the pound (SRD 5.2); the purse is held in copper and carried as the fewest coins worth that much, largest first, as it is shown, so 1 gp 2 sp 3 cp is 6 coins. Module items weigh a default by kind, so modules author no weights: a Potion of Healing ½ lb (SRD 5.2), a key nothing (SRD 5.2 weighs none; one comes with its lock), and treasure 1 lb each (SRD 5.2 weighs none), including the treasure a character has kept. Coin and gear found become purse and gear as they are taken.
- **Refused.** Taking gear (ammunition included), coin, treasure or any item, taking back dropped gear, and buying (#210) are each refused when the weight carried afterwards would exceed capacity, with the engine's reply naming the item's weight, the weight carried and the capacity ("The Chain Mail weighs 55 lb, and you carry 23 lb of the 75 lb your Strength allows: drop something first."). The button reads "Too heavy", and the AI DM's `take` tool lists only what fits; asked anyway, it reads the same reply. Dropping, equipping and selling are never refused for weight: a sale's coin always weighs less than the gear sold.
- **Shown.** The sheet and the creation preview show "Carrying: 14 of 135 lb"; during an adventure "You carry" ends with the weight carried and the capacity, which change at once with each take, drop, purchase or sale. `get_character_status` gives the AI DM the same.
- **Sheets.** No count limits stowed gear any more: #209's 20-item stopgap is gone. A sheet over its capacity, such as a weak character that bought chain mail under #209, stays valid so its library still loads; it shows the excess and can take nothing more until it drops or sells enough. Every starting kit weighs at most 13 lb, under the 45 lb of the lowest possible Strength, 3.

## Changing gear (#209)

A character carries its equipment and its **stowed** gear: catalogue weapons, armour and shields it carries but has not equipped. The sheet keeps both. Its AC and attacks always come from what it has equipped, so every change below updates them at once: the result card ends with the new AC and attacks, and the browser's status strip shows them in and out of fights.

- **Finding gear.** A module places gear as an item of kind `gear` naming a catalogue item. Like treasure and coin it is hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, so it is found only by examining. **Take** stows it; a bundle of arrows or bolts (`"gear": "arrows"`) adds 20 to the count instead (#230). Each piece is found once per character: the ledger records it, kept or not. Gear is equipment, not loot, so carrying it out does not make an escape _with loot_.
- **Equip** (outside a fight): body armour, doffing any armour worn, which is stowed; a shield; or a second light weapon beside a light weapon held, for the light-weapon extra attack. Any other weapon is swapped in with **Wield**. Refused: what isn't carried, a shield with a two-handed weapon, a shield or second weapon without a free hand ("Hands full"), and a second weapon when either is not light.
- **Donning time (SRD 5.2).** Light armour takes 1 minute to don and 1 to doff, medium 5 and 1, heavy 10 and 5; a shield takes the Utilize action. The result says how long the change took. There are no deadline clocks in the 5e game yet; when they return they will count this time.
- **Unequip** (outside a fight): armour, a shield or a second weapon, which is stowed. The last weapon held stays in hand; **Wield** another instead. Unarmed strikes are not modelled, so a character always holds a weapon.
- **Wield**: stows the weapons held and wields a stowed weapon. A two-handed weapon is refused while a shield is worn.
- **Drop** (outside a fight): stowed gear only; equipped gear is unequipped first. Dropped gear lies in the room as an item, and **Take** picks it back up.
- **In a fight.** Drawing a second light weapon (Equip) or swapping the weapon held (Wield) uses the turn's one object interaction and no action; swapping from two weapons to one is one interaction. The bar and the AI DM offer only these two in a fight, so the fight's options stay short. A second weapon change in the same turn is refused ("Interaction used"). SRD 5.2 also lets a weapon be equipped or unequipped as part of each attack, and a second object interaction take the Utilize action; both are abstracted into this one interaction per turn. A Thief's Fast Hands (#307) makes a second weapon change in the turn with its bonus action. Armour and shields can't be changed and nothing can be dropped until the fight is over.
- **Strength.** Equipping heavy armour below its Strength requirement is allowed; the result and the status strip say the speed drops by 10 feet, which has no effect without positions.
- **Carrying.** What a character carries is limited by weight, not by count; see [Carrying capacity](#carrying-capacity-224).
- **"You carry".** Outside a fight the character's gear is acted on from its "You carry" list, like carried items (#198), not from the action bar. The list starts with what it wears ("Worn") and the weapons in hand ("In hand") under Worn and wielded; its arrows and bolts ("Arrows (17)", #230) are with the consumables and its stowed gear ("Stowed") under Other, each kind once with a count: Unequip on armour, a shield and a second weapon; Wield on a stowed weapon; Equip on stowed armour, a shield or a light weapon; Drop on stowed gear. In a fight the action bar offers only Wield and Equip on stowed weapons, in its Weapons row (#392), worded for what they do: "Swap Mace for Dagger" and "Hold Dagger in your other hand". It leaves out a change that can't work with what is held (a second weapon beside one that isn't light, or with a shield or two weapons in hand; a two-handed weapon with a shield) (owner decision, 12 October 2026), and a swap for another weapon of the kind already held, which would change nothing. The AI DM's `equip`, `unequip`, `swap_weapon` and `drop` tools are offered from the same projection, and the engine writes each refusal.
- **Keeping it.** A victory or an escape replaces the character's possessions, stowed gear included, with what it holds at the end, so gear dropped or left behind is gone and gear found and carried out is kept. A defeat or abandonment leaves the character exactly as it started.

## Magic items (#345)

The catalogue (`src/equipment-5e.ts`) holds SRD 5.2's **+1 Weapon**, **+1 Armor** and **+1 Shield**, uncommon magic items: a +1 version of every weapon, every body armour and the shield, with ids such as `shortsword-plus-1`, `chain-shirt-plus-1` and `shield-plus-1`, named "+1 Shortsword", "+1 Chain shirt" and "+1 Shield". None needs attunement.

- **The bonus.** A +1 weapon adds 1 to its attack rolls and its damage rolls, the Light extra attack's damage included (its ability modifier is still left out). +1 armour adds 1 to the AC its mundane armour gives, and a +1 shield adds 1 to the shield's +2, only with shield training (an untrained shield adds no AC at all). Graze still deals only the ability modifier on a miss.
- **Otherwise its mundane item.** A +1 item has its mundane item's damage, properties, mastery, weight, armour category, Dexterity cap, Strength requirement and stealth disadvantage. A character who has mastered a kind of weapon (the shortsword) uses its mastery with the +1 version; masteries are chosen from the mundane weapons only. A +1 shield is a shield: one at a time, and equipping a +1 shield stows the shield carried, as new body armour stows the old.
- **Tier and worth.** A +1 item's tier is uncommon, or its mundane item's when that is rarer (a +1 plate armour is rare, so no module hides one yet). It is worth SRD 5.2's 400 gp for an uncommon magic item, which counts toward nothing: no merchant sells or buys one, and it is outside every treasure budget.
- **Placement (D10, owner decision, 9 October 2026).** A module hides at most one magic item (`MAGIC_ITEM_LIMITS` in `src/treasure-5e.ts`, a parameter table), only in a module whose maximum recommended level is 3 or more, outside its gp treasure budget. A Wand of Magic Missiles is one (#346). It is found like other gear: in a feature, or on an inline opponent's body (a bestiary opponent's treasure type gives no gear). Scrolls and potions are priced inside the budget. No item that needs attunement ships in this increment (D11), and the validator refuses one. One shipped module hides a magic item: _The Hollow Chantry_ (#350) hides a +1 Mace in its founder's tomb, behind an optional Ghoul.
- **Merchants.** No merchant stocks a magic item (the validator refuses one), and none buys one: a merchant's room offers no Sell on it and lists no price for it, and the engine refuses the AI DM's offer with "No merchant here deals in magic items: you keep the +1 dagger." ("Magic item").
- **Shown.** Under "You carry", a magic item's entry says where it is ("+1 Shortsword In hand") and opens to explain its bonus ("Uncommon magic weapon: +1 to attack and damage rolls."); its name heads the gear panel's attack or worn armour. The sheet lists each magic item held under **Magic items**, its bonus explained, and its attack line includes the bonus.
- **The balance gate.** The one-hit-kill measure tries each weapon a module places, a +1 weapon among them, as it does every other placed weapon.
- **Formats.** The module (29), library (23), save (48) and trace (42) formats bump: a module may hide a magic item, and a sheet, a save or a trace may hold one.

## Spell scrolls and wands (#346)

`src/magic-items-5e.ts` holds SRD 5.2's **Spell Scroll** and **Wand of Magic Missiles**. A module places a scroll as an item of kind `scroll` naming its `spell`, and the wand as kind `wand` with `"wand": "wand-of-magic-missiles"`; each must be hidden in a feature or carried by an inline opponent, and is found once per character, like gear. Taking one puts it with the character's magic items, ready to cast from at once; neither weighs anything.

- **Which spells.** A scroll holds one levelled spell from the game's curated list (`SPELLS`), cast on its reader's turn: no cantrip, and no reaction spell such as Shield (owner-approved, 11 October 2026). The validator refuses others ("is a spell scroll, but Fire Bolt is a cantrip").
- **Reading a scroll.** A character whose class has the spell on its list reads the scroll and casts the spell with no spell slot, at the scroll's own save DC and attack bonus (SRD 5.2: DC 13 and +5 for a 1st- or 2nd-level spell, DC 15 and +7 for a 3rd-level one, `SCROLL_CASTING`) and its own spellcasting modifier for healing; the scroll is used up. A lasting effect that rolls again on later turns (Spirit Guardians' strikes, Slow's repeat saves) uses the reader's own save DC, a simplification. Reading takes the spell's normal casting time (SRD 5.2; owner decision, 11 October 2026: a scroll doesn't speed casting up, nor slow it): Healing Word from a scroll takes the bonus action, Cure Wounds the action, and Prayer of Healing its minutes, outside a fight. It spends none of the turn's one slot, so a spell with a slot may follow in the same turn if the action allows. The spell is cast at its own level (no upcasting), and Disciple of Life, which needs a slot, adds nothing. Its effect, concentration and duration are the spell's own (D9).
- **Who can read one.** A Fighter or Rogue reads none, and a Cleric can't read a Wizard-only scroll such as Magic Missile: the engine refuses with "Mage Armor isn't on your class's spell list, so you can't read the scroll." ("Not on your list"). The bar still lists the scroll, disabled with that reason, so the player sees why.
- **A spell above its reader's slots (SRD 5.2).** Reading a scroll of a spell of a higher level than any spell slot its reader has needs an ability check with its spellcasting ability: d20 + the modifier against DC 10 + the spell's level. On a failure the scroll is used up with no effect ("Failure: the Spell Scroll (Scorching Ray) crumbles with no effect."). The engine rolls it; no proficiency is added.
- **The Wand of Magic Missiles (SRD 5.2).** An uncommon wand with 7 charges, needing no attunement; anyone may use it, a Fighter or Rogue too. Spending 1 charge casts Magic Missile at 1st level, and each charge more raises the spell's level by one, up to 3rd (the highest spell level in the game, a simplification: SRD 5.2 allows up to 7 charges). Using it takes the action. It needs no attack bonus or save DC, so a character that casts no spells uses it as well as one that does. Spending its last charge rolls a d20: on a 1 it crumbles to ash and is gone.
- **Recharging (D9).** A long rest at a rest site gives the wand back 1d6 + 1 charges, up to 7 (SRD 5.2's "dawn"; the long rest, #335). A wand short of charges is something a long rest restores, so the long rest is offered for it. Between adventures the wand regains every charge, as everything else refreshes (D2).
- **Outside a fight.** A scroll's spell is read from its entry under "You carry", with **Read** ("Read Spell Scroll (Mage Armor)"), disabled with the engine's reason when it would refuse; only spells that may be cast outside a fight are offered there (healing, and buffs that outlast a fight), as for spells cast from slots. The wand has no target outside a fight, so it offers nothing there.
- **In a fight.** The fight's Cast choice lists each scroll and the wand beside the spells the character knows: "Magic Missile — Wand of Magic Missiles, 2nd level for 2 charges (5 left)" or "Thunderwave — Spell Scroll (Thunderwave)", with the target choice as for any spell.
- **Kept or lost.** A scroll and a wand are kept like gear: on a surviving end the character keeps exactly the magic items it holds then (a read scroll is gone, an unread one kept; a wand with its charges, then recharged between adventures); a defeat or an abandoned adventure leaves it with exactly those it started with. The sheet lists each under **Magic items**, a wand with its charges.
- **Copying into a spellbook (SRD 5.2).** Between adventures, a Wizard may copy a scroll's spell into its spellbook from the scroll's entry on the sheet: **Copy into spellbook (50 gp)**, at 50 gp a spell level (`COPY_COST_PER_LEVEL`). The spell must be on the Wizard's list, of a level it has slots for, and not in the spellbook already, and the purse must hold the cost; otherwise the button is disabled with why ("Magic Missile is in the spellbook already."). Copying uses up the scroll (an agent's choice: SRD 5.2 doesn't say, and keeping both the scroll and the copy would count its value twice). The copied spell is kept in the sheet's `copiedSpells`, apart from the spells each level writes, so it doesn't count toward a level's owed spells; the Wizard prepares it like any other spellbook spell, and the spellbook lists it. Copying takes no time: there is no clock between adventures. The library refuses it during an adventure.
- **Treasure.** A scroll's value counts toward the module's treasure budget (D10): a 1st-level scroll is common and worth 50 gp, a 2nd- or 3rd-level one uncommon and worth 200 gp, half its rarity's magic item value as for potions (`SCROLL_VALUES`, `SCROLL_TIERS`). Its tier follows the tier rules, so a 2nd- or 3rd-level scroll is hidden only in a module for level 3 and up. The wand is outside the budget, as a magic item. No merchant sells or buys either.
- **The AI DM.** The cast tool takes `item`, the id of the scroll or wand the player reads or uses (null otherwise), while one can be cast from, and describes each with its spell, its levels and charges and its targets; the prompt (v33) says when to use it and that the engine checks the class list, rolls the scroll's check and spends the charges.
- **The balance gate.** The harness never reads a scroll or uses a wand. _The Hollow Chantry_ (#350) hides two: a Spell Scroll (Cure Wounds) in its vestry and a Spell Scroll (Magic Missile) on its priests' stair.
- **Formats.** The module (30), library (24), save (49) and trace (43) formats bump: a module may place a scroll or a wand, a sheet holds `magicItems` and may hold `copiedSpells`, and a cast may name its item.

## Monster loot (#240)

Every bestiary monster has a treasure type (`TREASURE_TYPES` in `src/treasure-5e.ts`, a house rule the owner chose on 7 October 2026), which says what it carries:

| Treasure type        | Carries                                                   | Monsters                                               |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `none`               | nothing                                                   | Zombie, Skeleton, Ghoul, Giant Spider, Wolf, Giant Rat |
| `copper`             | 3d6 cp                                                    | Goblin Minion, Goblin Warrior, Kobold                  |
| `silver`             | 3d6 sp                                                    | Bandit, Hobgoblin Warrior, Gnoll Ravager               |
| `silver-and-trinket` | 3d6 sp and one 10 gp gem or 25 gp art object (a d2 picks) | Goblin Boss, Bugbear Warrior                           |
| `gold`               | 2d6 gp                                                    | Ogre                                                   |

- **Rolled at authoring time.** `npm.cmd run loot -- <module.json> --seed <n>` (`src/loot-5e.ts`) rolls each bestiary opponent's treasure type and writes the result into the module as ordinary items it carries (`hiddenIn` the opponent): a pouch of coin (`<opponent>-coins`, "Goblin Warrior's Coins") and any trinket (`<opponent>-trinket`, "Goblin Boss's Rough Gem"). The same file and seed always roll the same loot, and the balance gate and the budget see exactly its value. It skips an opponent that already carries loot (a key is not loot), one with type `none`, an inline opponent, and one whose fight ends the adventure, since its body is never searched; it refuses a roll that would put the module over its budget. An author may lower or remove what was rolled. Rolling again leaves an opponent that still carries some loot alone, but rolls afresh for one whose loot was all removed.
- **Validated.** Everything but a key that a bestiary opponent carries must be loot its treasure type could produce: coin only in its type's coin, together no more than its dice can roll, and at most one of its trinkets. No type gives potions or gear. The message names the module, the room, the opponent, what it carries and what its type gives. An inline opponent has no treasure type, so what it carries is the author's. Carried loot counts toward the budget like any find.
- **Found on the body.** As since #133, loot appears only once the opponent's fight is won and its body is searched; one that flees takes it with it (#237), and one that surrenders keeps it unless asked (#238).
- **Shipped modules.** Loot the types couldn't produce moved to a feature of the same room (owner decision): the Delve's guard purse (18 sp, under the guard post's overturned table, as the Zombie carries nothing) and jewelled goblet (in the vault's iron chest), the Goblin Warren's Potion of Healing (under the guard tunnel's gnawed bones) and the Tinker's Toll's 12 gp purse (in the toll tower's strongbox, so the Toll's shortsword stays affordable). The Robbers' Barrow's goblin carried 10 cp rolled with seed 240 in place of 2 gp 5 sp; its Grave Robber, a Bandit since #252, carries 14 sp rolled with seed 252. The Goblin Warren's boss's 25 gp silver chain fits its type. The Delve and Toll release runs examine the features instead of the bodies. The Silvervein Mine (#241) was rolled with seed 241: its kobolds carry 9 cp and 8 cp, and its bugbear 12 sp and a 10 gp rough gem. The #275 modules were rolled with seed 275: the Shepherd's Bothy's Moor Bandit, the Gravedigger's Lodge's False Gravedigger and the Ravager's Tower's Gnoll Ravager each carry 11 sp. The Wolfstone Hillfort (#289) was rolled with seed 289: the Reaver carries 6 gp.

## Trading with merchants (#210)

A module may make a creature a **merchant**, with a stock of catalogue gear and the minutes each trade takes. Merchants trade only inside adventures: the owner dropped the market between adventures (6 October 2026). Outside a fight, in the merchant's room:

- **Prices.** A merchant sells each item it stocks, any number of times, at its catalogue price (the tables above). It buys any catalogue gear the character carries for **half its price, rounded down to the copper** (a 1 sp club sells for 5 cp). Prices and stock are the engine's: the AI DM's `trade` tool names only an offer the engine lists (`buy:` an item stocked, `sell:` stowed gear or a bundle of arrows or bolts, or `sell-treasure:` a carried gem or art object), with no price. A merchant buys **gems and art objects at their full value** (SRD 5.2 trade goods, #239), whether found in this adventure or brought in from an earlier one; it buys no potions.
- **Ammunition (#230).** Arrows and bolts are bought and sold by the bundle of 20, at 1 gp to buy and 5 sp to sell. Buying adds 20 to the count; **Sell** on the "You carry" entry sells 20, and with fewer than 20 it is refused with "Fewer than 20" on the button and the engine's reply ("You carry only 7 arrows, and a merchant buys them by the 20.").
- **Stock tiers.** A merchant never stocks a magic item (#345). It stocks common gear always and uncommon gear (such as the longbow) only in a module whose lowest recommended level is 3 or more; no merchant sells rare gear in increment 12. The module validator enforces this. No shipped module stocks or places ranged weapons or ammunition yet: there is no archer starting kit, so bows reach a character only this way (owner decision on #230).
- **Buying.** The purse pays and the item is stowed. Too little coin is refused with the engine's reply naming the price and the purse ("Too little coin" on the button), and so is a purchase that would take the character over its carrying capacity once the coin is paid ("Too heavy", #224).
- **Selling.** Stowed gear sells at once. No merchant buys a magic item (#345, see Magic items). Equipped gear sells only once the player confirms it in the panel: the engine refuses an unconfirmed sale of gear that is only equipped, and the AI DM has no offer for it. Body armour sold is doffed first (its doffing minutes are reported); the last weapon held is never sold. The result ends with the new AC and attacks.
- **Time.** Each purchase or sale takes the merchant's authored minutes (1–60), which the result reports. There are no deadline clocks in the 5e game yet; when they return they will count this time, as they will donning time.
- **Coin found here.** Coin found earlier in the same adventure is in the purse at once and can be spent there. Spending it does not make it findable again: the ledger records the find, not the coin.
- **Treasure sold here.** A gem or art object found in the adventure and sold there stays in the ledger as found, so it is never there to find again, and escaping after selling it is escaping with loot (its coin goes out in the purse).
- **Keeping it.** Trades follow the adventure rollback contract: a victory or an escape keeps what the character holds at the end (gear bought, the change), and a defeat or abandonment leaves its coin and gear exactly as they were at the start.
- **The balance gate** plays no trades, but its one-hit-kill measure also tries each weapon a merchant sells, as it does each weapon a module places.

## Combat

Combat (#128–#130) uses the numbers above. An encounter has two sides, the party and its opponents, and each side may hold several combatants; for now the party is one Fighter, and a module may set several opponents against it. Every die comes from the adventure session's own seeded stream and is recorded with the action that drew it.

- **Initiative.** When a fight begins, each combatant rolls d20 + its initiative bonus (Dexterity modifier). A level-3 Champion rolls it with advantage (Remarkable Athlete, #315): two d20s, the higher kept. A surprised Champion's advantage and disadvantage cancel, as SRD 5.2 says, so it rolls one d20; the card says "advantage (Remarkable Athlete) and disadvantage (surprised) cancel", and the initiative breakdown names both. Higher totals act first. A tie goes to the higher Dexterity score; combatants still tied each roll a d20 roll-off, repeated among any still tied, and the higher roll acts first. The browser's initiative table shows the order, each total, hit points and armour class, and highlights and tags the combatant whose turn it is; each combatant's roll and roll-off are under **How initiative was rolled**. Once the fight is over the table folds away behind an **Initiative order** button.
- **Turns.** Combatants act in initiative order, round after round, skipping the defeated. A defeated opponent drops out: it takes no more turns and cannot be targeted, and the fight goes on while any opponent stands.
- **Turn economy.** Each turn has one action, one bonus action and one reaction. On the player's turn an attack on one living opponent of their choice takes the action, the light-weapon extra attack takes the bonus action (unless Nick makes it part of the Attack action), Second Wind takes the bonus action, drinking a potion takes the bonus action (as in SRD 5.2), and Action Surge adds one more action. From level 5 the Attack action makes two attacks (Extra Attack): the bar keeps an attack button for each living opponent after the first, labelled "Second attack on _name_ with _weapon_", and the status strip says a second attack is left to make. The player uses them by the action bar's Attack, Extra attack, Drink, Second Wind, Action Surge and End turn buttons or by telling the AI DM. The bar always shows the whole toolkit; an action the engine would refuse now is disabled with the engine's reason, shortened to "Action used", "Bonus action used", "Attack first", "Extra attack used", "Full HP" or "No uses left". Action Surge is in the bar from level 2. The turn lasts until the player ends it or nothing is left that the engine would accept, so a player at full health without Action Surge ends the turn by attacking, as before. The fight's first turn says so ("It is your turn. Your turn ends by itself once nothing is left to do."), and the browser's End turn names what it would leave unused ("Left unused: Action Surge", #392). The browser's status strip shows what is left this turn and each feature's uses. Only the Rogue's Uncanny Dodge (#308) and reaction spells such as Shield (#337) use a reaction: opportunity attacks need positions, and no Fighter feature at levels 1–5 takes one; fleeing (#396) draws an attack from each foe in their place, which takes no reaction. Opponents only attack.
- **Second Wind and Action Surge in combat.** Second Wind rolls 1d10 + Fighter level and restores that much, up to maximum HP. It is refused at full health, when the bonus action is spent and when no use is left. Action Surge is refused below level 2 and when its use is spent. A refusal changes nothing and spends nothing.
- **Light-weapon extra attack (Light, SRD 5.2).** A character holding two light weapons attacks with the first, and after that attack may make one extra attack that turn with the second against any living opponent. Its damage adds no ability modifier unless the modifier is negative, or the character has Two-Weapon Fighting. It takes the bonus action, so it competes with Second Wind and a potion, unless the second weapon has Nick and is mastered, which makes it part of the Attack action. It is offered once per turn, Action Surge included. The action bar shows Extra attack for each living opponent only to a character holding two light weapons, disabled with "Attack first" before an attack this turn, "Extra attack used" once made, and "Bonus action used" without Nick once the bonus action is spent. The AI DM's `light_attack` tool is offered exactly while it is enabled; called otherwise, the engine refuses it.
- **Weapon masteries.** Each applies only to a mastered weapon being wielded.
  - _Sap:_ when the attack hits a creature that survives, the creature is sapped: it has disadvantage on its next attack roll, which spends the effect. The effect also ends at the start of the player's next turn. The browser marks a sapped combatant in the initiative table.
  - _Vex:_ when the attack hits and deals damage to a creature that survives, the attacker has advantage on its next attack roll against that creature, with any weapon, which spends it. It lapses at the end of the attacker's next turn.
  - _Graze:_ when the attack misses, natural 1s included, the target still takes damage equal to the attack's damage modifier, if above 0, of the weapon's type.
  - _Nick:_ see the light-weapon extra attack. Nick acts only on the extra attack: the SRD lets the extra attack be part of the Attack action, and here that holds when the second weapon has Nick.
- **Heavy weapons.** A heavy melee weapon wielded with Strength below 13, or a heavy ranged weapon with Dexterity below 13, attacks with disadvantage (SRD 5.2).
- **Ranged weapons (#230).** A ranged attack uses Dexterity, takes the action like any attack, and spends one arrow or bolt of the kind its weapon needs; the attack's line says how many are left ("2 arrows left."). With none it is refused before any die is rolled ("You have no arrows left for the shortbow."), and the action bar shows Attack disabled with "No arrows" or "No bolts"; the AI DM is not offered `attack`. Switching weapons uses the turn's one object interaction (**Wield**), with no free swap. SRD 5.2 gives a ranged attack disadvantage within 5 feet of a hostile creature that can see you; without positions every opponent closes in, so the owner chose (6 October 2026) **no disadvantage in a fight's first round** (an opening volley while foes close) and **disadvantage from round 2 on**, named "Close combat" on the result card. When a fight is won, half the ammunition spent in it, rounded down, is recovered at once ("You recover 1 arrow from the fight. You have 2 arrows."); in a fight that is lost, or an adventure abandoned, the ammunition rolls back with the rest of the possessions. Opponents' ranged attacks (a Bandit's light crossbow, a Goblin's shortbow) stay out of scope.
- **Advantage and disadvantage.** With advantage the engine rolls two d20s and keeps the higher; with disadvantage, the lower. With sources of both it rolls one d20. The kept die decides natural 1s, natural 20s and critical hits. The result card names each source and shows both dice, for example "at disadvantage (Sap): 18 and 6, keeping 6". Advantage and disadvantage come only from the engine's rules: for now, Sap, a heavy weapon below its ability 13, a ranged attack from round 2 (Close combat) and the attacker's poisoned or prone condition give disadvantage, and Vex, Pack Tactics and a prone or paralysed target give advantage. Neither the player nor the AI DM can grant them.
- **Conditions (#232).** The engine owns every condition: it records on the combatant which condition it is, what gave it (the opponent and its attack), how many of the combatant's turns it lasts and the save that ends it, if any. Only poisoned, prone, paralysed (#234), and Sleep's incapacitated and unconscious (#340) are in play; frightened and restrained wait for a monster that needs them, and 0 HP is still instant defeat. The balance harness plays riders through the real engine, and each fight's record counts the conditions put on the character, by kind (#270).
  - _Poisoned_ (SRD 5.2): disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks. No ability check is rolled in a fight, and conditions end with the fight, so only the attack rolls are affected yet. Source: the Giant Spider's Bite (DC 11 Constitution save; up to 10 turns, a minute, repeating the save at the end of each of its turns).
  - _Prone_ (SRD 5.2): disadvantage on its own attack rolls; attack rolls against it have advantage. Without positions every attacker counts as within 5 feet, so every attack against a prone combatant has advantage. Standing up is abstracted: a prone combatant spends its next turn getting up, so it stays prone until the end of that turn, attacking at disadvantage, and then stands. Source: the Wolf's Bite (DC 11 Strength save).
  - _Paralysed_ (#234, SRD 5.2): the combatant can't take actions, bonus actions or reactions, automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws (no die is rolled; the narration says why), attack rolls against it have advantage, and every hit on it is a critical hit, doubling its damage dice. Without positions every attacker counts as within 5 feet. The player's action bar offers only Wait (its End turn), with the reason beside it, and the engine refuses every other fight action, from the bar or the AI DM, with "You are paralysed and can't act until it ends; you can only wait." A paralysed opponent spends its turn doing nothing but any repeat save; no shipped monster can paralyse one yet. A paralysed ally does not count for Pack Tactics. Source: the Ghoul's Claw (SRD 5.2: +4, 1d4 + 2 slashing; DC 10 Constitution save; paralysed until the end of the target's next turn, with no repeat save). The SRD's exemption of Undead and elf targets is not modelled: the Fighter is the only target. A rider may instead give paralysis for up to 10 turns with a repeat save, as poison does.
  - _Frightened_ (#341, SRD 5.2, from Turn Undead): disadvantage on its attack rolls and ability checks. Without positions its source is always in sight, and its bar on moving closer changes nothing. A turned undead is Incapacitated too, so it can't act at all.
  - _Incapacitated_ and _Unconscious_ (#340, SRD 5.2, from Sleep): an incapacitated combatant takes no actions, bonus actions or reactions, and loses its concentration. An unconscious one is incapacitated too, fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws without a roll, and attack rolls against it have advantage and every hit is a critical hit, as against a paralysed one ("Critical hit: Giant Rat is unconscious!"). It drops prone as well, which adds nothing more here. Both last only while Sleep holds the target (see The Wizard's spells).
  - _Riders._ A monster's attack may carry a rider: extra damage of its own type on a hit (the Giant Spider's 1d6 poison), with its dice doubled by a critical hit, and a condition, after a saving throw if the rider names one (d20 + the target's save bonus against the DC; the player's Strength and Constitution saves add its proficiency). A hit that drops the target to 0 HP gives no condition and rolls no save. The same condition again replaces the old one.
  - _Ending._ At the end of each of its turns a combatant rolls the repeat save against each condition that allows one, and a success ends it; then a condition whose turns have run out ends. Every condition ends when the fight does. Each save, condition and ending is an event with its dice, narrated and replayed exactly from the session's seed.
  - _Shown._ The browser tags each combatant's conditions in the initiative table, and the character's on the status strip, each with its source and how it ends for screen readers; the AI DM's scene and character status report them, and no tool can give, remove or change one.
- **Pack Tactics (#232).** A monster with Pack Tactics (the Wolf, the Giant Rat) has advantage on its attack rolls while an ally on its side is alive and able to act. Without positions, the ally need not be beside the target.
- **Damage types (#233).** Every damage roll has one of the 13 SRD 5.2 damage types (acid, bludgeoning, cold, fire, force, lightning, necrotic, piercing, poison, psychic, radiant, slashing, thunder): weapons, monster attacks, riders and traps. A module or bestiary naming any other type is refused.
- **Resistance, vulnerability and immunity (#233).** A stat block may list damage resistances, vulnerabilities and immunities, each type in at most one list. After the damage is rolled (critical dice, Great Weapon Fighting and modifier included), immunity makes it 0, resistance halves it (rounding down) and vulnerability doubles it; a rider's extra damage and Graze's damage on a miss are adjusted by their own type. The attack's event records the damage rolled and which defence applied; the result card says so ("= 7 bludgeoning, doubled to 14 (vulnerable)", "halved to 3 (resistant)", "ignored (immune)") and its damage roll shows the damage taken in bold. The bestiary's Skeleton is vulnerable to bludgeoning; the Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul are immune to poison damage.
- **Condition immunities (#233).** A stat block may list the conditions (poisoned or prone) it cannot be given: a rider's condition then rolls no save and has no effect. The Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul are immune to poisoned; their SRD 5.2 immunity to exhaustion (and the Ghoul's to charmed) is left out, as neither condition is in play.
- **Monster saving throws (#233).** A monster's saving throw is d20 + the ability's modifier, plus its proficiency bonus (from its challenge rating: +2 up to CR 4, +3 up to 8, and so on) in each save its stat block lists in `saveProficiencies`; the Zombie is proficient in Wisdom saves (+0). Like every die, the d20 comes from the session's seeded stream.
- **Undead Fortitude (#233).** When damage reduces a monster with Undead Fortitude (the Zombie) to 0 HP, unless the damage is radiant or from a critical hit, it makes a Constitution saving throw against DC 5 + the damage taken (the attack's damage and its rider's, after any resistance); on a success it drops to 1 HP instead and fights on. The attack's line shows it at 0 HP, and a following line gives the save: "Success: Zombie refuses to fall and has 1/15 HP" or "Failure: Zombie stays down", then its defeat. It may save again each time it is reduced to 0.
- **Feature uses and rests.** Every adventure starts with all Second Wind and Action Surge uses, because a rest between adventures restores them and every hit point; spent uses, like hit points and carried items, last from one fight to the next, and only a short rest restores some, and a long rest (both below) all, during the adventure. The sheet and the creation preview say so once, under the features, for a character with a feature with limited uses (spell slots alone don't count) (#431). The fight and the feature descriptions leave it out: the resource pips show the uses left, and no rest happens mid-fight. Since the AI DM's scene lists the features with their descriptions, dropping the rule from them is prompt v44.
- **Feature recovery (#333).** Each feature with uses records in its class data how many it has at each level and what a **short rest** and a **long rest** each restore: a number of uses, or all of them (SRD 5.2). Second Wind regains one use on a short rest and all on a long rest; Action Surge regains its use on either. The adventure session tracks the uses left of each such feature by its id.
- **Hit dice (#333).** A character has one hit die of its class's size per level (a level-3 Fighter has 3d10, a level-3 Rogue 3d8). The adventure session tracks the hit dice left, all of them at the start; the AI DM's character status reports them ("Hit dice: 3 of 3 d10 left"), and the browser's status strip shows a pip for each outside a fight's turns (they can't be spent in a fight). Only a short rest spends them; a long rest (#335, below) and a rest between adventures regain them all (SRD 5.2, D2).
- **Short rests (#334).** Outside a fight, in a room with no unresolved hostile encounter (none, or one won or ended peacefully), the character may take a short rest, **at most two per adventure** (a house rule; SRD 5.2's limit is the clock, which this game leaves out). The player chooses how many hit dice to spend, up to those left. They are spent one at a time: each rolls the class's hit die and heals the roll + the Constitution modifier, at least 0 per die, up to the maximum, and once the character is at full HP no more are spent (the rest of the number chosen stays in the pool); at full HP a rest, taken only to regain feature uses, spends none. Then each feature regains its short-rest uses, up to its most. A rest must do something: one at full HP with every use, one that spends no hit dice and restores no use, one in a fight or with foes in the room, and a third are refused, as is spending more hit dice than are left. Like everything in the adventure, the rest is undone if the adventure is abandoned or lost. The result card shows each die ("You spend a hit die: d10 7 + 2 = 9; you regain 9 HP and have 16/20 HP") and each feature's uses regained ("Second Wind regains 1 use (2 of 2 left)."). In the browser the rest sits in the character column, under what the character carries, outside a fight: the short rests and hit dice left and, while a rest would restore something, how many hit dice to spend (the most it can, unless the player chooses fewer) and Rest, disabled with its reason when the engine would refuse it (foes in the room, or both short rests taken). The AI DM has a `rest` tool, offered only while the engine would accept a rest, whose `hit_dice` lists the numbers it would accept. The balance harness rests whenever the character is below its style's heal threshold and a rest is offered, before drinking a potion.
- **Long rests (#335).** A module may mark a room as a **rest site** (`restSite: true`). There, outside a fight and with no unresolved hostile encounter in the room, the character may take a long rest, **at most one per adventure** (D1, a house rule; SRD 5.2 limits it to one a day, and there is no clock). It restores every hit point, every hit die (SRD 5.2, D2) and each feature's long-rest uses (Second Wind and Action Surge all of theirs); it leaves the short rests taken as they were. A long rest away from a rest site, after the one an adventure allows, in a fight, with foes in the room, or at full HP with every hit die and use is refused. The card reads "You take a long rest: you regain 6 HP (13/13 HP) and 1 hit die (2 of 2 d10 left). Long rests: 0 of 1 left in this adventure.", then each feature's uses regained. In the browser, at a rest site the character column shows a **Long rest** part under Short rest: the long rests left and, while one would restore something, Long rest, disabled with its reason when the engine would refuse it (foes here, or the long rest taken); the AI DM's character status lists "Long rests: 1 of 1 left" in a module with a rest site. The AI DM has a `long_rest` tool with no arguments, offered only while the engine would accept a long rest. The balance harness takes a long rest at a rest site whenever the character is below its heal threshold, before a short rest or a potion. _The Hollow Chantry_ (#350) is the first shipped module with a rest site (its vestry) and a wandering encounter (a Zombie, on 25 or less); its cautious runs rarely reach the vestry below the threshold, so the gate's runs take almost no long rests there.
- **Wandering encounters and interrupted rests (#335).** A module may author one **wandering encounter** (`wanderingEncounter`: an encounter in no room and a `chance` from 1 to 100). Each short or long rest first rolls a d100 from the session's seeded stream; at the chance or less the rest is **interrupted**: it restores nothing, is not counted against the rests an adventure allows (SRD 5.2: an interrupted rest gives no benefit and may be started again), and the wandering encounter's fight begins in the room where the character rests, as any fight begins. The card shows the d100 ("You keep watch as you rest: d100 12, 25 or less: Prowling Rat comes upon you. Your short rest is interrupted and restores nothing.", or "… d100 63, over 25: nothing disturbs you."). It fires at most once: once it has come, no rest rolls for it again. Its fight is credited like any other, once, with its opponents' XP, and losing it ends the adventure in its defeat ending. Its opponents carry nothing and never surrender. A module without a wandering encounter rolls nothing, so its rests are never interrupted. Since #403 this one encounter is a one-entry wandering encounter table rolled only on rests: it plays and draws its dice as before, and a module has it or region tables, never both.
- **Opponent targeting.** Each opponent attacks a living party combatant on its own turn. With one party combatant there is no choice and no die. With more (companions, later), the opponent rolls a die with one side per living party combatant, from the session's seeded stream, and attacks the one at that position counting in initiative order; the die is recorded with the attack.
- **Attacks.** d20 + attack bonus hits when it meets or beats the target's AC. A natural 20 (19–20 for a Champion) is a critical hit, which always hits and rolls the damage dice twice; a natural 1 always misses. Damage is the dice plus the damage modifier, never below 0. With Great Weapon Fighting and a weapon in two hands, each 1 or 2 on a damage die counts as 3: the result card's text and its damage roll show each die as rolled and, for a 1 or 2, that it counts as 3 (such as "d10 2→3").
- **Defeat.** A combatant at 0 HP is defeated. An opponent at 0 HP drops out of the fight. When every opponent is defeated, has fled or has surrendered (see Morale), the encounter's victory ending follows if it names one; otherwise the fight is over and the character explores on. The player character at 0 HP is defeated at once (no death saving throws): the adventure ends in the module's defeat ending, the character's sheet is saved at 0 HP and marked defeated, and it cannot start another adventure. After a victory the character keeps what it holds, is credited the XP it earned and rests to full health (see Earning XP and treasure).
- **Refused actions.** Acting out of turn, attacking an absent, friendly or defeated target, attacking with the action spent, using a feature it can't use now, or acting after the fight is over is refused with an engine-written reason. A refusal changes nothing and draws no dice.
- **Multiattack (#235).** A stat block's `multiattack` (2–4) is how many attacks the opponent makes on its turn. For each, a seeded die picks the target when more than one party combatant stands, as for any opponent, and a second die picks which of its attacks it makes when it has more than one; a combatant that falls is not picked again, and the turn's attacks stop when the fight ends. The narration says "(attack chosen by a die: 2)" and the result card shows the "attack die". A monster without Multiattack makes one attack, its first, and draws no attack die. Source: SRD 5.2's Multiattack, where a block names the attacks it may combine; here the die chooses among all of them.
- **Monster traits (#235).** The opponent AI uses every trait deterministically: no choice is left to the AI DM, and the only dice are the ones named here.
  - _Rampage_ (house, after the gnoll's trait): when an opponent with it drops a combatant to 0 HP with an attack on its turn, it makes one bonus attack at once, its target and attack chosen by dice as above. It takes the bonus action, so once a turn. The SRD's move of half its speed is omitted. While the Fighter is the only party combatant, a drop ends the fight, so Rampage cannot yet happen in play; the engine applies it with more party combatants.
  - _Nimble Escape_ (SRD 5.2, goblins; and the house Kobold): a bonus-action Disengage or Hide. Without positions there are no opportunity attacks to avoid, and hiding needs cover the game does not model, so the owner decided (#235) that it changes nothing in a fight. Fleeing (#237) did not change that: a fleeing monster leaves without provoking an attack, so a goblin's Disengage has nothing to avoid. The engine and the balance harness carry the trait, and a test checks that a fight with it plays exactly as one without. A stronger abstraction, disadvantage on the next attack against the goblin until its next turn, cost every goblin module about 20 points of the gate's weakest survival and was rejected.
- **Morale (#237, a house rule).** Every stat block gives a morale DC, or `"never"` for undead and mindless monsters, which never check. A side checks morale when its first combatant falls, and again when defeat or flight leaves it at half its starting numbers or fewer; each check is made once per fight, and when both come at once (a pair's first fall) the side checks once. Each combatant on it that is still in the fight, has a morale DC and is not already fleeing makes a Wisdom saving throw against that DC, in initiative order, with d20s from the session's seeded stream: "Goblin 2 checks morale as the first of its side falls: a Wisdom saving throw, 9 − 1 = 8 against DC 8. Success: it stands its ground." One that fails is fleeing: on its next turn it leaves the fight instead of acting ("Goblin 2 flees the fight"), unless it is incapacitated (paralysed). Until then it can be attacked, and one cut down first is defeated. A fled monster can't be attacked, leaves no body and takes what it carried with it (owner decision, 6 October 2026). It gives half its XP, rounded down, if it exchanged blows with the character first (it attacked, or was attacked, hit or miss), and none if it did not (owner decision, 7 October 2026): the award reads "Defeated Goblin 1 and Goblin 3; drove off Goblin 2". Its going can bring its side to half strength. Fleeing provokes no free attack: there are no opportunity attacks without positions, and the owner decided (#237) against adding one. The initiative table tags a monster Fleeing, then Fled, and the AI DM's scene reports it. The player character has no morale DC and never flees. The DCs are house numbers: 8 for goblin minions and warriors, kobolds, bandits and giant rats (a Wisdom 8 goblin fails about 40% of the time); 6 for the Goblin Boss, Wolf, Giant Spider, Bugbear Warrior and Ogre; 5 for the Hobgoblin Warrior and Gnoll Ravager; never for the Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul. The balance harness plays fleeing through the real runtime, so it counts in survival and XP, and each fight's record counts who fled; the one-hit-kill check still counts every ordinary enemy, whether or not it may flee.
- **Surrender (#238).** A module may author a `surrender` for an opponent that checks morale (never for an undead or mindless one): a description, topics as for a creature, and optional XP for sparing it. Such an opponent surrenders instead of fleeing, in the same way: a failed morale saving throw reads "Failure: it will surrender on its turn.", the initiative table tags it Surrendering, and on its next turn, unless it is paralysed, it yields instead of acting ("Goblin 2 throws down its arms and surrenders.") and is tagged Surrendered. Until then it can be attacked, and one cut down first is defeated. A surrendered opponent can't be attacked, its yielding counts toward its side's half strength, and the fight is won when no opponent is left fighting. It leaves no body. Once the fight is won it is a creature in the room, under its own name, with its description and its authored topics; the AI DM can ask it only about those, as with any creature. A topic may `give` items the opponent carries: answering it (after a passed check, when it has one) offers them ("Goblin 2 offers you the Stolen Ring 2."), and they lie in the room to take like a discovered item; a failed check offers nothing, and, like every check, is not tried again. Each is found once per character, as any treasure, coin or gear is, so a character that found it before is offered nothing. If the opponent is cut down instead, its body holds the same items; if it flees, they leave with it. It gives combat XP as a fled opponent does, half its XP if it exchanged blows with the character first and none otherwise, plus the module's XP for sparing it, all in the fight's award ("Defeated Goblin 1 and Goblin 3; spared Goblin 2"), earned once with it. Monsters in the bestiary carry no surrender: it belongs to an adventure. The balance harness plays surrender through the real runtime like fleeing, counts who surrendered in each fight, and never talks; the XP check counts, for an opponent that may surrender, the greater of its XP and half its XP plus its sparing XP.
- **Not used yet.** An opponent uses its stat block's first melee attack and its rider, if any, or with Multiattack (#235) several of its attacks, and its traits and defences; stat-block riders that need advantage (the Goblin Warrior's extra 1d4) and ranged attacks are not used.

**The AI Dungeon Master.** The engine is the only authority over dice, turn order, targets, hits, damage, hit points and endings. The AI DM may only call `look` and `get_character_status` to read the scene, and the action tools `move`, `sneak`, `ambush`, `react`, `examine`, `take`, `use_item`, `force_door`, `pick_lock`, `break_door`, `unlock`, `search`, `disarm`, `talk`, `equip`, `unequip`, `swap_weapon`, `drop`, `attack`, `light_attack`, `second_wind`, `action_surge` and `end_turn`. A check tool is called only when the player explicitly asks for that approach ("force the door"); words that name none ("open the door") get a question listing the offered approaches, and no roll. `talk` lists only the creatures' authored topics, a surrendered opponent's included (#238), and a check already tried is not offered again; calling it anyway gets the engine's refusal, never a reroll. Each action tool is offered only while the engine would accept it, and lists only what is visible and legal; the tools come from the same projection as the browser's action bar, so the AI DM is offered exactly the actions the bar shows enabled (`get_character_status` names each attack with its bonus and damage, and the arrows and bolts held, #230): `move` the room's exits, `examine` its features, visible items and carried items, `take` its visible items, `use_item` carried potions while drinking one would be accepted, `attack` the living opponents on the player's turn, and `light_attack` them while the extra attack is enabled. Looking, searching, reading, inspecting or opening something ("search the chest") is `examine`. "Catch my breath" or "second wind" calls `second_wind` only when it is offered; otherwise the AI says it is not available. One message allows one action. While the character is paralysed (#234) only `end_turn` is offered, and any other action tool gets the engine's refusal. Opponents are listed by id and name; when the player's words fit exactly one, by its name or by an ordinal matching the number in its name ("attack the second goblin" is Goblin Minion 2 when Goblin Minions 1 and 2 are listed), the AI calls `attack` with it; it never counts positions in a list. When they name none or fit several ("attack the goblin" with two goblins) it asks which one and calls nothing. It never guesses a target. No tool takes a roll, damage, advantage, extra-action, discovery, item or outcome argument; any extra argument is refused before the engine is reached. The engine writes the reply to every action it resolves or refuses, so the AI cannot narrate a different roll, discovery, item or result, and a reply without a tool call changes nothing. Taking an item that is not visible (one that does not exist, or one still hidden) is refused by the engine.

## Spellcasting (#336)

The casting engine is class-agnostic: spells are data, and a class's definition gives its spellcasting. The Cleric (#339) is the first playable class that casts; the Wizard brings its own spell list (D3). The test-only Test Caster and its fixed character, Sage (`src/test-caster-5e.ts`: Wis 17, spell attack +5, save DC 13 at level 1), exercise it in the engine tests, the scripted DM cases and trace replay.

- **Spells as data.** `src/spells-5e.ts` holds each spell from SRD 5.2: its level (0 for a cantrip), school, casting time (an action, a bonus action or a reaction), effect, damage type, saving throw, duration, concentration, cantrip growth and upcast rule. The engine reads only this data. The spells so far: Fire Bolt (ranged spell attack, 1d10 fire), Sacred Flame (Dexterity save, 1d8 radiant, none on a success), Shocking Grasp (melee spell attack, 1d8 lightning), Magic Missile (three darts of 1d4 + 1 force that always hit; one more dart per slot level above 1st), Inflict Wounds (Constitution save, 2d10 necrotic, half on a success; +1d10 per slot level above 1st), Cure Wounds (2d8 + the spellcasting modifier; +2d8 per slot level above 1st), Healing Word (a bonus action: 2d4 + the modifier; +2d4 per slot level above 1st), from #338 the area spells Burning Hands (a 15-foot cone: Dexterity save, 3d6 fire, half on a success; +1d6 per slot level above 1st), Shatter (2nd level, a 10-foot sphere: Constitution save, 3d8 thunder, half; +1d8 per slot level above 2nd) and Fireball (3rd level, a 20-foot sphere: Dexterity save, 8d6 fire, half; +1d6 per slot level above 3rd), and from #337 Bless (concentration, up to 1 minute: +1d4 to attack rolls and saving throws), Shield of Faith (a bonus action; concentration, up to 10 minutes: +2 AC), Mage Armor (8 hours: base AC 13 + Dexterity on a target wearing no armour) and Shield (a reaction to being hit: +5 AC until the start of the caster's next turn), from #339 Guidance, Resistance and Thaumaturgy (cantrips) and Guiding Bolt (below), and from #340 Ray of Frost and Chill Touch (cantrips), Chromatic Orb, Thunderwave and Sleep (below).
- **Known and prepared spells.** A caster's sheet records the cantrips it knows and the spells it has prepared (`spells`), its class's counts for its level (fewer only while a new level leaves some to choose, #341), each on the class's list, and each prepared spell of a level it has slots for and not one it always has prepared (the Life Domain's, #341). A class that casts nothing has none. Prepared spells change only between adventures, in the library (D8, #339; see The Cleric, level 1); a Wizard prepares only spells in its spellbook (#340), and a level-5 Wizard's Memorize Spell swaps one on a short rest for the rest of the adventure (#344).
- **Spell attack bonus and save DC.** A spell attack adds the proficiency bonus and the spellcasting ability modifier; a spell's save DC is 8 + both (SRD 5.2).
- **Cantrips.** A cantrip is cast at will, spending no slot. Its damage dice grow at character level 5 (Fire Bolt 2d10, Sacred Flame 2d8, Shocking Grasp 2d8).
- **Spell slots.** A class's data gives its slots of each level at each character level (the Test Caster has the SRD 5.2 full-caster table: 2 1st-level slots at level 1, up to 4, 3 and 2 of 1st, 2nd and 3rd level at level 5). A levelled spell spends one slot of its level or higher; a higher slot makes it stronger by its upcast rule. Slots are tracked as feature uses (`spell-slots-1` and on), so they last from fight to fight, a long rest restores them all, a short rest none, and a rest between adventures restores them all. The AI DM's character status reports them ("Spell slots: 1st 1 of 2 left").
- **Casting in a fight.** A spell whose casting time is an action takes the Magic action; a bonus-action spell takes the bonus action. Only one spell slot may be spent a turn (SRD 5.2): after a levelled spell, by action or bonus action, another is refused, though a cantrip is not. Action Surge's extra action can cast a cantrip, not a second levelled spell. A reaction spell (#337) is cast on an opponent's turn, so its slot isn't the turn's one.
- **Effects.** A _spell attack_ rolls d20 + the spell attack bonus against AC, like a weapon attack: a natural 20 is a critical hit (its dice doubled) and a natural 1 misses; advantage and disadvantage come from the same engine rules (Sap, Vex, Hide, Steady Aim, conditions). A ranged spell attack follows the ranged-weapon rule (D6): no disadvantage in round 1, disadvantage (Close combat) from round 2; a melee one has none. Spell attacks never deal Sneak Attack, which needs a weapon. A _save_ spell makes its target roll its saving throw against the caster's DC (a paralysed target fails a Dexterity save without a roll): a failure takes the damage, a success half of it (rounded down) or none, and no damage dice are rolled when a success takes none. Magic Missile's darts _always hit_, each rolling its own die. A _healing_ spell heals its dice + the spellcasting modifier, up to the maximum. Damage meets resistance, vulnerability and immunity like any damage, and radiant damage, or a critical spell attack, bypasses Undead Fortitude. A target a spell drops is defeated and its side checks morale, as for an attack.
- **The Cleric's spells (#339).**
  - **Guidance** (cantrip, concentration, up to 1 minute): the character's next ability check that the engine grades (a feature's, a door's, a trap's, a topic's or a search's check, or a parley) adds 1d4, rolled after the d20 and named on the card ("d20 9 + 3 + 3 (Guidance) = 15"); then Guidance ends ("Guidance ends on you: its die is spent."). It may be cast outside a fight, where checks are made. SRD 5.2 lets the caster choose one skill and adds the die to every check with it for the minute; without a clock the game gives the die to the next check, whatever its skill (owner decision). Its 1-minute band (D9) still applies: it ends with a fight, at a rest or with the adventure if no check comes first. A Stealth check to sneak or hide is not graded, so it doesn't spend Guidance.
  - **Resistance** (cantrip, concentration, up to 1 minute): cast with a damage type (acid, bludgeoning, cold, fire, lightning, necrotic, piercing, poison, radiant, slashing or thunder, as SRD 5.2 lists). Once a turn, when the target takes damage of that type from an attack, 1d4 comes off it (the weapon's damage first, else the rider's), before Uncanny Dodge's halving; the card says "less 3 (Resistance)". The bar offers it once for each such type the foes' attacks deal ("Resistance against piercing (cantrip)"), and only in a fight, as it lasts no longer than one; any other type, a type for another spell, or none is refused ("Choose the damage type Resistance resists: …"), and so is a type no opponent still in the fight deals with an attack or its rider, from the bar, the AI DM or the API alike ("Resistance resists only damage your opponents' attacks deal: slashing."). Chromatic Orb's types are not limited so.
  - **Thaumaturgy** (cantrip): flavour only. It is known and shown on the sheet ("flavour only: no effect in play"), never offered as a cast, and a cast of it is refused ("Thaumaturgy is flavour only: it has no effect in play."). The AI DM may describe its harmless wonders in words; they never change a check, a roll or an outcome.
  - **Guiding Bolt** (1st level): a ranged spell attack, 4d6 radiant, +1d6 per slot level above 1st. A hit on a target left standing gives the next attack roll against that target advantage ("Guiding Bolt"), whoever makes it, until the end of the caster's next turn; the attack spends it, hit or miss.
- **The Cleric's 2nd-level spells (#341).**
  - **Aid** (8 hours; a Life Domain spell): the character's maximum and current hit points rise by 5 (+5 per slot level above 2nd) until a long rest or the adventure's end; a long rest then restores hit points to the maximum without it. SRD 5.2's three targets are one without companions. Cast in or out of a fight; not again while it lasts.
  - **Lesser Restoration** (a bonus action; a Life Domain spell): ends the character's paralysis or poison, the first it has in that order (blinded and deafened don't exist here). A paralysed character can't cast it on itself, so in practice it cures poison. Refused when there is nothing to cure ("You have none of the conditions Lesser Restoration ends: paralysed, poisoned."). Conditions end with a fight, so it is cast only in one.
  - **Spiritual Weapon** (a bonus action, concentration, up to 1 minute): a melee spell attack as it is cast, 1d8 + the spellcasting modifier force (+1d8 per slot level above 2nd); then, on each later turn while it lasts, a bonus action attacks again with it. Moving the weapon is omitted (no positions).
  - **Hold Person** (concentration, up to 1 minute): only a humanoid ("Hold Person works only on a humanoid, and Zombie is an undead."); a Wisdom save or paralysed while the spell lasts, a save at the end of each of its turns ending it, and damage doesn't end it. A creature immune to paralysis succeeds without a roll. Its extra targets from a higher slot are omitted. Of the shipped bestiary, the Bandit, the Bandit Captain and the Warrior Veteran are humanoids.
  - **Protection from Poison** (1 hour, so until the next rest, D9): ends the character's poison, then gives resistance to poison damage (cancelling a vulnerability) and advantage on saving throws against being poisoned ("advantage: Protection from Poison").
  - **Prayer of Healing** (ten minutes to cast, so only outside a fight: "Prayer of Healing takes ten minutes to cast: only outside a fight."): heals 2d8 (+1d8 per slot level above 2nd) with no modifier, Disciple of Life's 2 + the slot level added, and gives a short rest's recovery of feature uses (one Channel Divinity use), without spending hit dice and without counting as one of the adventure's two short rests. Its target can't benefit from it again until a long rest ("You can't benefit from Prayer of Healing again until a long rest."). Like every healing spell it is refused at full hit points, even when its short rest would give a use back. SRD 5.2's five targets are one.
- **The Cleric's 3rd-level spells (#342).**
  - **Mass Healing Word** (a bonus action; a Life Domain spell from level 5): heals 2d4 + the spellcasting modifier (+1d4 per slot level above 3rd), with Disciple of Life's 2 + the slot level. SRD 5.2's six creatures are one without companions. Cast in or out of a fight.
  - **Spirit Guardians** (concentration, up to 10 minutes): an area spell catching up to three opponents (a 15-foot emanation, D4), chosen at casting. Each makes a Wisdom saving throw against one roll of 3d8 radiant (+1d8 per slot level above 3rd) as it is cast, taking it or half on a success; then, while the caster concentrates, each caught opponent still in the fight saves again at the end of each of its turns against a fresh roll. A creature makes the save only once a turn, which the engine's turns keep. The guardians end with the fight (owner's choice, not D9's next rest: they only strike the foes they caught). Halving the foes' speed is omitted (no positions), and the damage is always radiant (the character's alignment isn't modelled).
  - **Beacon of Hope** (concentration, up to 1 minute, so only in a fight): the character has advantage on Wisdom saving throws ("advantage: Beacon of Hope"), and healing spells, potions and Divine Spark heal their most, with no dice drawn ("4 + 4 (Beacon of Hope: the most)"). Its death saving throws don't exist (ADR 0005). SRD 5.2's any number of creatures is one without companions.
  - **Bestow Curse** (concentration, up to 1 minute): the target makes a Wisdom saving throw or is cursed until the fight ends or the caster's concentration does. The curse is chosen at casting, from two (owner's choice): _attacks_, disadvantage on its attack rolls against the character ("disadvantage: Bestow Curse"); or _necrotic_, 1d8 necrotic more whenever the character damages it with an attack roll (a weapon, a spell attack or Spiritual Weapon; doubled by a critical hit) or a spell (Sacred Flame, Spirit Guardians and other damaging spells), shown after the damage ("plus 5 = 5 necrotic (Bestow Curse)"). Divine Spark and Sear Undead are no spells, so they don't add it, and neither does a Graze mastery's miss. The ability-disadvantage and forced-Dodge curses are omitted. A cast without a curse, or with another, is refused ("Choose the curse Bestow Curse lays: attacks or necrotic."), and so is a curse for any other spell. Its longer durations from 4th-level and higher slots are out of reach.
  - **Protection from Energy** (concentration, up to 1 hour, so until the next rest): resistance to the damage type chosen at casting, acid, cold, fire, lightning or thunder, cancelling a vulnerability to it. The bar offers it once for each type, in a fight or out of one, whatever the foes deal. SRD 5.2's touched creature is the character.
- **Casting outside a fight.** Only a healing spell, a spell whose effect outlasts a fight (Shield of Faith, Mage Armor, #337), or Guidance (#339), on the character, spending its slot; other spells are refused ("Fire Bolt is cast in a fight: outside one, only healing spells and spells that outlast a fight."; "Bless lasts no longer than a fight: cast it in one.").
- **Refusals.** The engine refuses, before any die: a spell that doesn't exist, a cantrip the character doesn't know or a spell it hasn't prepared (by name: "You haven't prepared Inflict Wounds."), a cantrip given a slot, a levelled spell without a slot of its level or higher, a slot level the character has none of or none left of, a second slot in a turn, its action or bonus action already spent, a damaging spell at anyone but a living opponent still fighting, and a healing spell at a foe or at full health.
- **Ongoing effects and durations (#337).** A buff spell puts an effect on its target, the caster or an ally (only the caster until companions): Bless adds a d4 to each attack roll and saving throw it makes, rolled after the d20 and named on the card ("9 + 5 + 2 (Bless) = 16"); Shield of Faith and Shield add to its AC; Mage Armor sets its base AC to 13 + Dexterity while it wears no armour (an unarmoured AC is 10 + Dexterity + a shield, so it adds 3), is refused on a target wearing armour and gives nothing if armour is put on later (SRD 5.2 ends it then). A spell already on its target can't be cast on it again, so no duration is renewed or extended. **Durations without a clock (D9):** up to 1 minute (Bless) ends when the fight ends; 10 minutes to 1 hour (Shield of Faith) ends at the next rest, short or long; 8 hours or "until dawn" (Mage Armor) ends at a long rest or when the adventure ends; Shield's one round ends at the start of the caster's next turn, or with the fight. Only a completed rest ends one: a rest the wandering encounter interrupts ends nothing. An effect that outlasts a fight is carried with the character's hit points and slots into the next fight; like them it lives only in the adventure, so none survives it. A spell lasting no longer than a fight is cast only in one.
- **The Wizard's spells (#340).**

  - **Chill Touch** (cantrip): a melee spell attack, 1d10 necrotic. Its target can't regain hit points until the end of the caster's next turn; no monster regains hit points here, so that has no effect.

The bar offers it once for each type ("Chromatic Orb of fire (1st-level slot)"), and a cast without one, or with another, is refused ("Choose the damage type Chromatic Orb deals: …"). Its leap to a second creature needs positions and is omitted.
  - **Thunderwave** (1st level): a 15-foot cube, so up to 2 opponents (the cube heuristic under Areas of effect): a Constitution save, 2d8 thunder, half on a success, +1d8 per slot level above 1st. Its push needs positions and is omitted.

It makes a Wisdom save against the caster's DC, with advantage (#369, owner's house rule: an alert foe in a fight resists it), so it fails only when both d20s do; on a failure it is incapacitated until the end of its next turn, when it saves again, with advantage too, and on a second failure it is unconscious for as long as Sleep lasts, never running out by turns; a success on either save ends Sleep on it. Damage wakes it: the spell ends on it, with its condition. Sleep ends, and its conditions with it, when the caster's concentration does (another concentration spell, a failed Constitution save, falling) and with the fight. A creature immune to exhaustion (the bestiary's Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul, as SRD 5.2 gives them) succeeds without a roll ("it is immune to exhaustion"). Omitted: an ally can't shake a sleeping target awake, as the character has no allies; and Unconscious lasts until the spell's minute ends, which is the fight (D9), not a count of 10 rounds.
- **The Wizard's spells (#343).**
  - **Ray of Sickness** (1st level): a ranged spell attack, 2d8 poison, +1d8 per slot level above 1st; a hit leaves the target poisoned (disadvantage on its attack rolls) until the end of its next turn, which stands for SRD 5.2's "until the end of your next turn" (the target's next turn comes first). A creature immune to poisoned isn't.

The caster names up to as many different targets as there are rays, and the rays are split among them as evenly as they go, the first named taking any more: one target takes all three, two take two and one. SRD 5.2 lets each ray choose its target, so a split such as three and one of four rays isn't offered. A ray whose target is already down goes at the next target named still in the fight; with none left, the rest are lost. One slot and one action cast them all.
  - **Acid Arrow** (2nd level): a ranged spell attack, 4d4 acid; a hit also leaves acid that deals 2d4 acid at the end of the target's next turn (rolled then, not doubled by a critical), and a miss still splashes half the 4d4. Both grow by 1d4 per slot level above 2nd. The later acid is an effect on the target ("Acid Arrow takes hold on Bandit: 2d4 acid more at the end of its next turn"), gone once it burns or the fight ends.

  - **Shatter** (2nd level): as the Cleric's (#338). **Hold Person** (#341): as the Cleric's; off this list from #348 until #369.
  - **Blur** (2nd level, concentration, up to 1 minute): attack rolls against the character have disadvantage ("disadvantage (Blur)") until its concentration ends or the fight does. No monster has Blindsight or Truesight to see through it.
  - **Mirror Image** (2nd level, 1 minute, no concentration): three illusory duplicates. Each time an attack roll hits the character, the engine rolls a d6 for each duplicate left; if any shows 3 or higher, a duplicate takes the hit instead and is destroyed, and the character takes no damage and nothing else the hit would do. The spell ends with its last duplicate ("Mirror Image ends on you: the last duplicate is gone.") or the fight. It comes after any reaction: a hit Shield turns into a miss rolls no d6s. No monster is blinded or has Blindsight or Truesight.

  - **Fireball** (3rd level): as under Spells as data (#338): a 20-foot sphere, so up to 4 opponents.

While it lasts, on each later turn the caster's action (the Magic action, no slot) makes the attack again, at the same or another foe (`drain-attack`; refused without it: "You have no draining touch to make again: cast Vampiric Touch first."). It ends with the caster's concentration or the fight.

The engine gives it as an Attack the turn's first attack takes outside an Attack action under way, so the action is left for a spell ("hasted" on the attack); Extra Attack's attacks still follow the action's. When Haste ends before the fight does (its concentration broken, another concentration spell cast, or being incapacitated), its holder is incapacitated until the end of its next turn, the rest of the current one too if it is its own: lethargy. Ending with the fight brings none. Its doubled speed and its Dash, Disengage, Hide and Utilize are omitted (no positions, no objects to use in a fight).

Each makes a Wisdom saving throw or is slowed while the caster concentrates, until the fight ends: −2 AC, −2 on Dexterity saving throws (in the save's bonus), and one attack a turn: a Multiattack makes one attack, and Rampage's bonus attack doesn't follow it. At the end of each of its turns a slowed foe saves again, ending it on a success ("Red Bandit makes a Wisdom saving throw against Slow: 2 + 0 = 2 against DC 15. Failure: still slowed."). Its halved speed, its bar on reactions (no monster reacts) and the chance that a slowed spellcaster's spell fails (no monster casts) are omitted.
- **Concentration (#337).** Bless and Shield of Faith need concentration. A caster concentrates on one spell at a time: casting another concentration spell ends the first ("Bless ends on you: you concentrate on another spell."). Damage that leaves it standing, in a fight or out of one (a trap's darts), calls for a Constitution saving throw against the higher of 10 and half the damage, rounded down, at most 30 (SRD 5.2); Bless's d4 counts. A failure ends the spell. Being incapacitated (paralysed) or falling also ends it. Each attack is one source of damage, its rider included, and calls for one save.
- **Area spells (#338).** An area spell catches up to its most opponents (see Rules that need positions), chosen by the caster, each once; more, a repeat, or an ally is refused before any die ("Burning Hands catches at most 2 opponents.", "Burning Hands can't catch the same creature twice."), and a spell without an area has one target ("Fire Bolt has one target."). The damage is rolled once ("Burning Hands: damage 4 + 5 + 6 = 15 fire, and each of its 2 targets saves against it."); then each target, in the order chosen, makes its own saving throw against the caster's DC and takes the full damage on a failure or half (rounded down) on a success, through its own resistance, vulnerability or immunity. A target it drops is defeated and its side checks morale before the next target saves. Unlike a single-target save spell, the damage is rolled even if every target then succeeds.
- **Reaction spells (#337).** Shield goes through the reaction path Uncanny Dodge uses, generalised: when an opponent's attack roll hits a caster that has Shield prepared, its reaction free and a 1st-level slot or higher left, the fight waits before the damage is rolled. The caster casts Shield, or takes the hit (a character with Uncanny Dodge too would be offered both, as one reaction). Shield's +5 AC counts against the attack that triggered it: a total that no longer meets the new AC misses, and a natural 20 still hits. It lasts until the start of the caster's next turn, so it also covers the attacker's other attacks that turn. SRD 5.2's other trigger, being targeted by Magic Missile, never comes: no monster casts.
- **The action bar and the AI DM.** The runtime projects a Cast action for each spell, slot level and target it would take now, disabled with its reason ("No slot", "Slot used this turn", "Full HP", "Already on", "Wearing armour"); a reaction spell only while a hit waits for an answer. In the browser (#337) the action bar's spell choice lists each spell at each slot level ("Magic Missile (1st-level slot)", "Fire Bolt (cantrip)") with a target choice ("Yourself" for a buff or healing spell) and Cast, disabled with the engine's reason; an area spell (#338) shows a box for each foe instead, "Up to 2", with the first ones ticked and the rest disabled once its most are, and Cast waits for at least one; outside a fight it offers what can be cast out of one, and answering a hit only reaction spells, beside Take the hit. The status strip lists each ongoing effect with the spell holding concentration and when it ends ("Bless, concentration, until the fight ends"), and the AI DM's character status and scene list them too. The command-line adapter lists the casts. The AI DM has a `cast` tool with `spell`, `slot_level` (null for a cantrip) and `targets` (a list, #338: one id, or an area spell's chosen opponents), offered only while some spell can be cast, listing the spells, slot levels, targets and each area spell's most the engine would accept; any other spell, slot or target, too many targets or one twice gets the engine's refusal. No tool takes a duration, and recasting an effect or keeping two concentration spells is refused or ends the first. The AI DM's `cast` tool also takes `damage_type` while Resistance is offered (#339), listing the types the engine would take. The balance harness casts only healing spells (#339; see Balance harness).

## Sneaking in, surprise, sneaking past and lurking monsters (#301–#303)

**Stealth** is a Dexterity skill anyone can roll; a class's skill choices give proficiency in it (the Fighter's list does not include it; the Rogue's does, and a fresh Rogue has Expertise in it, #306). Armour whose SRD 5.2 entry gives disadvantage on Stealth (chain mail and plate) gives the check disadvantage.

**Passive Perception.** Every stat block gives its passive Perception: in the bestiary (format 8) the SRD 5.2 value (for example Wolf 15, Giant Rat 12, Goblin Warrior 9, Zombie 8), and for a house stat block, such as the Gnoll Ravager, 10 + its Wisdom modifier. A stat block authored inline in a module (module format 21) must give its own; the validator refuses one without it.

**Sneaking in.** Beside each exit to a room where a fight not yet won waits, the browser offers **Sneak** next to **Go**, and the AI DM a `sneak` tool listing only those rooms; sneaking anywhere else is refused ("No fight waits in the Alcove: there is no one to sneak up on."). Sneaking takes the same way as going in (a door must be open, a trap on the way springs first), then rolls a Stealth check whose DC is the highest passive Perception among the fight's opponents (the first of them on a tie). Meeting it leaves the character **unseen** in the room (#302): the fight has not begun, and the character may spring an ambush or slip past. Missing it starts the fight as usual. The engine alone decides: the AI DM has no tool or parameter that declares surprise or an ambush.

**Ambush and surprise (SRD 5.2).** Unseen, the browser offers **Ambush** and the AI DM an `ambush` tool (called when the player asks to attack or ambush the opponents); it starts the fight with every opponent surprised. A surprised combatant rolls initiative with disadvantage: two d20s, the lower kept. Nothing else changes; the 2024 rules have no surprise round. The sneak card shows the Stealth roll (and any armour disadvantage), the passive Perception it beat or missed and whose it is, and who has not noticed the character; the ambush card names who is surprised, the initiative table tags each surprised opponent, and the initiative breakdown shows both dice.

**Sneaking past (#302).** Unseen, the character may go on through any open way out of the room (a **Go** button, or the AI DM's `move`: "I slip past them to the north door"), back the way it came included, or leave the adventure from an exit room. The fight is then **bypassed**: unresolved and still there, its opponents keep what they carry, and coming back through its room meets it again. While unseen nothing else in the room can be done, as it would give the character away: examining features or items, taking, doors, searching, disarming, talking, gear changes and trading are refused ("Not while you are sneaking past Giant Rat: you would be seen."); only drinking a potion is allowed. After a failed sneak the fight is on, and moving is refused as in any fight.

**Remembered.** The Stealth check is remembered with the fight it was made against and never rerolled: once made, sneaking up on the same fight again is refused before any die is drawn ("going in again starts it"), so coming back to a bypassed fight starts it. Only an encounter with `sneakAgain: true` offers a fresh sneak after the character has slipped past it, with a new Stealth check that replaces the old one.

**XP for slipping past.** A bypassed fight gives no XP by default. An encounter may author `bypassXp`, credited on surviving completion under the encounter's own award (`adventure/encounter/id`) when the fight was slipped past and never won: an encounter is credited once, won or slipped past, so a character that slipped past a fight earns nothing more for it by winning it in a later adventure, and one that slips past and then wins it in the same adventure earns the fight's XP alone. The balance harness's _stealth-first_ style sneaks and slips past, and the gate reports it; the gate's XP limit counts the bypass XP (see Balance harness and Balance gate).

**Lurking monsters (#303).** A module may mark an encounter `lurking: true`: its opponents lie in wait. The first time the character comes into its room (by going in, sneaking in, or starting there), the least stealthy of them (the lowest Stealth bonus, the first of them on a tie) rolls a Stealth check for them all against the character's passive Perception. Meeting it hides them: the character is **surprised** and rolls initiative with disadvantage. Missing it, the character spots them and the fight opens normally. One roll for the group, by its weakest member, mirrors sneaking in, where the character's one roll meets the opponents' best passive Perception. The engine alone decides: the AI DM has no tool or parameter that declares or removes surprise, and the system prompt says so.

- **Stealth bonus.** A stat block may give `stealth`, its Stealth bonus. The bestiary (format 9) gives SRD 5.2's where it lists one: Goblin Minion, Goblin Warrior, Goblin Boss and Bugbear Warrior +6, Giant Spider +7, Wolf and Dire Wolf +4. The house Kobold has +4, its Dexterity modifier (+2) and proficiency bonus (+2), as a Nimble Escape sneak like the goblins. Every other stat block, the house Gnoll Ravager included, rolls its Dexterity modifier. An inline stat block (module format 23) may give its own.
- **Passive Perception.** The character's is 10 + its Wisdom modifier, + its proficiency bonus if it is proficient in Perception (twice with Expertise, #306), + 5 if a rule gives it advantage on Perception checks or − 5 for disadvantage; advantage and disadvantage together cancel, adding nothing (SRD 5.2). No rule in the game gives either yet. A level-1 Fighter with Wisdom 10 and Perception has 12.
- **The card.** "Giant Rat is lying in wait in the Rat-Gnawed Cellar. Giant Rat's Stealth check: d20 10 + 4 = 14 against your passive Perception 12 (10 + 0 Wisdom + 2 proficiency). Success: you did not notice it, and you are surprised and roll initiative with disadvantage." On a failure: "Failure: you spot it, and you are not surprised." The roll's chip shows the lurker's die against the passive Perception, and the initiative table tags the character Surprised, with both initiative dice.
- **Remembered.** The roll is remembered with its fight and never rerolled: coming back into the room (after slipping past the fight, #302) reuses it and draws no die. Hidden lurkers always start the fight, so only a roll the character won is ever reused.
- **Sneaking into a lurking room.** Both checks are made as the character comes in, its Stealth first, then the lurkers'. They combine as each side's awareness of the other: _the character's sneak succeeds and the lurkers are spotted_: the character is unseen (#302) and may ambush or slip past; _the sneak fails and the lurkers are hidden_: the fight begins and only the character is surprised; _both succeed_: neither side has noticed the other, so they stumble on each other and the fight begins with everyone surprised (2024 rules: surprise is per combatant, each caught unaware rolling with disadvantage), and there is no ambushing or slipping past; _both fail_: the fight opens normally. The sneak card says when the lurkers were hidden too.
- **Balance.** The harness and the gate play lurking through the real runtime, so it counts in survival; each run records how many fights surprised the character. The Counting-House's goblin and bugbear and, since #443, the Coldwater Weirgate's Brindle and drowned guildsmen lurk; the fixture _The Lurking Tunnels_ (`tests/fixtures/modules.mjs`) is the rat tunnels with the Giant Rat lurking (Stealth +4), and the gate plays it. Lurking took that fixture's weakest survival from 64.5% to 56.0%; the rat tunnels fail the gate either way.

## Reaction rolls (#304, a house rule)

Reaction rolls are a house rule on top of 5e: SRD 5.2 leaves how a creature greets the characters to the DM.

**Reaction-eligible.** A module marks an encounter reaction-eligible by giving it a `reaction`; the default is off. It may mark single opponents `reacts: true`: then only they react (and the rest follow their lead), and otherwise every opponent does. Mindless opponents never react: an Undead, or any stat block whose morale is `"never"`, the bestiary's mark for undead and mindless monsters. The validator refuses a reaction-eligible encounter with any mindless opponent, even one that would only follow another's lead.

**When it is rolled.** When a reaction-eligible fight would begin with no one surprised: on going into its room (or starting there), after any sneak (#301) or lurking (#303) roll. So it is rolled on going in, after a failed sneak (the opponents noticed the character), when lurkers were spotted, and on coming back to a fight slipped past (#302). It is not rolled while the character is unseen (it has not been noticed), on an ambush (the character chose to attack), when lurkers hid from the character (they are springing their own attack), or when a sneak and hidden lurkers meet and everyone is surprised.

**The roll.** 2d6 + the character's Charisma modifier, looked up in the reaction table (`REACTION_TABLE` in `src/reaction-5e.ts`, the one place to retune it):

| 2d6 + Charisma | Band        |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| 2 or less      | Hostile     |
| 3–5            | Unfriendly  |
| 6–8            | Uncertain   |
| 9–11           | Indifferent |
| 12 or more     | Friendly    |

With Charisma −1 a friendly band is out of reach; with +3 a hostile one is.

**Options.** A hostile band always fights: the fight begins at once. Every other band offers only the options its module authors, each band at least one, from **attack** (the fight begins, no one surprised) and **let pass** (the encounter ends peacefully and the character goes on), and **parley**, **toll** and **trade** (#305, below). Until the character chooses, the fight has not begun and nothing else can be done ("Giant Rat is waiting to see what you do: you may attack or pass peacefully."). An option the band doesn't offer is refused ("That is not offered: you may attack.").

**Let pass.** The encounter is settled: its room is free to explore, no fight waits there on coming back, and sneaking into it is not offered. The opponents keep what they carry: there are no bodies to search. An encounter may author `peacefulXp` (1–10,000) in its reaction, credited on surviving completion under the encounter's own award (`adventure/encounter/id`), so an encounter is credited once, won, slipped past or ended peacefully ("Parted peacefully with Giant Rat").

**Remembered.** The roll is remembered with its fight and never rerolled: refusals and projections draw no die, and should the fight be met again the roll is reused, drawing none.

**The card.** "Giant Rat sees you. Reaction roll: 2d6 (3 + 4) − 1 Charisma = 6: uncertain. The rat freezes, whiskers twitching. You may attack or pass peacefully." The band's authored words follow the roll, and the roll's chip shows the two d6s, the Charisma modifier and the band. The action bar offers one button per option: **Attack** and **Pass peacefully**.

**The AI DM.** The engine alone rolls the reaction and decides its band and options; the AI DM narrates the band from the engine's result. While the opponents react, `react` is its only action tool, listing only the band's options; it picks the one the player's words name ("I attack anyway" is attack). No tool takes a band, any extra argument is refused, and an option not offered gets the engine's refusal. Five AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite reactions`, on _The Wary Cellar_, `adventures/eval/wary-cellar.json`) cover going in, attacking anyway, passing peacefully, an option not offered and a player asking to change the band.

**Balance.** The harness has a reaction policy: _attack_, the default, attacks whenever the band offers it; _peaceful_ takes a peaceful option whenever one is offered. Each takes the other when its own is not offered. The gate judges the _attack_ runs (the worst case for survival) and, for a module with a reaction-eligible encounter, reports both policies beside its checks; its XP limit counts peaceful XP. No shipped module is reaction-eligible yet, so no verdict changed. The fixture _The Wary Tunnels_ (`tests/fixtures/modules.mjs`) authors every band, and the gate plays it: always attacking, the weakest character survives 91.5% of 200 runs; taking the peaceful option, 96.5%.

## Parley, tolls and trade (#305, a house rule)

Parley, tolls and trade extend the reaction-roll house rule: SRD 5.2 has the Persuasion, Deception and Intimidation skills, but how they move a creature's attitude is the DM's call, and here it is the module's. A reaction may author each of them once, and a band offers one only by naming it among its options. Every band must still offer attack or let pass, so the character can always end the reaction.

**Deception.** The skill list gains Deception (Charisma), SRD 5.2's skill for lies and bluffs. No Fighter can choose it: the Fighter's skill list doesn't have it, so a Fighter rolls it with its Charisma modifier alone.

**Parley.** `parley` is `{ "approaches": [...], "bands": {...} }`: one to three approaches, each `{ "skill", "dc" }` with a different one of `persuasion`, `deception` and `intimidation` and a DC from 5 to 30, and the graded check's bands (#281), each with optional `text` and one of a `shift` (−4 to 4, not 0: that many bands up toward friendly or down toward hostile, stopping at either end) or an `outcome`: `let-pass` (the encounter ends peacefully, for its peaceful XP), `fight` (the fight begins, no one surprised) or `surprise-attack` (only on `failure-by-5`: the opponents attack, and the character is surprised and rolls initiative with disadvantage). A failure or success by 5 or more left out counts as plain failure or success; a band left out, or with only words, leaves the reaction where it was. A shift to hostile begins the fight, since hostile always fights. A parley is made once per encounter and remembered with it: the band it leaves the reaction in stands, its options exclude the parley, and asking again is refused ("You have already parleyed: you may …"), never rerolled. Parley approaches have no circumstances (#284) and no retry. The check goes through the same rolls as any other (a d20 from the session's seeded stream, the check card with its band, the band's words), and the balance harness's always-fail and always-succeed check policies (#285) apply to it.

**Toll.** `toll` is `{ "coins": { "gp", "sp", "cp" }, "text"? }`: what the opponents let the character pass for, and their words as it is paid. Paying it takes the price from the purse and ends the encounter peacefully, like let pass, for its peaceful XP. With too little coin it is refused ("The toll is 5 sp, and you have 0 cp.") and its button is disabled ("Too little coin"). The coin is spent inside the adventure, so it rolls back with it: settling a victory or an escape keeps the loss, and a defeat or abandonment restores it. Nothing lowers or waives a toll.

**Trade.** `trade` is a merchant's stock and minutes (#210, with every merchant rule: catalogue gear only, no rare gear, uncommon gear only for level 3 and up, 1–60 minutes). While the band holds and offers trade, the reacting opponents are a merchant: they appear among the room's creatures with their wares and the prices they pay, and buying, selling gear and selling gems and art objects work as with any merchant. Trading ends nothing: the character still answers the reaction, and once it does (or a band without trade holds) the trade is over. Trade is open, not chosen, so it is no button and no `react` option.

**The card.** "Bandit sees you. Reaction roll: 2d6 (1 + 1) + 3 Charisma = 5: unfriendly. "Five silver, or blood." You may attack, parley (Persuasion DC 12, Deception DC 14, Intimidation DC 13) or pay the toll (5 sp)." A parley's card shows its check and band, the band's words, then what it did: "Bandit is now uncertain (was unfriendly). You may attack or pay the toll (5 sp).", "Bandit lets you pass: the encounter ends peacefully.", "Bandit turns hostile and attacks." or "Bandit attacks before you are ready: you are surprised and roll initiative with disadvantage." A toll's card: "You pay the toll of 5 sp. She bites a coin and waves you on. Bandit lets you pass: the encounter ends peacefully. Purse: 1 gp 5 sp." The action bar has one **Parley** button per approach, its skill and DC under it ("Deception DC 14"; the button is named in full, "Parley with Deception DC 14"), and **Pay the toll (5 sp)**; trade's Buy and Sell sit on the wares and on "You carry" as with any merchant.

**The AI DM.** `react` lists only the band's options and takes a parley's approach, listing the offered ones; it picks the approach the player's words describe: an honest appeal is Persuasion, a lie or bluff ("I tell them we're from the guild") Deception, and a threat Intimidation. `trade` is offered while the band offers trade. No tool takes a band, a price or a retry: any extra argument is refused, an option not offered gets the engine's refusal ("That is not offered: you may …"), and the system prompt says nothing lowers or waives a toll. Six AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite parley`, on _The Bandit's Toll_, `adventures/eval/bandit-toll.json`) cover the three approaches, paying the toll, asking for it to be waived and asking to change the band.

**Balance.** The harness's _peaceful_ reaction policy takes let pass, then a toll it can afford (one with too little coin is not offered), then a parley with the character's best offered approach (its skill bonus furthest above, or least below, the approach's DC; the first on a tie), and attacks only when none is offered; _attack_ is unchanged. The harness never trades. No shipped module authors a parley, toll or trade yet, so no verdict changed. The fixture _The Bandit's Toll_ (`tests/fixtures/modules.mjs`) authors all three.

## Exploring

An adventure module is a set of rooms joined by two-way passages; there is no map and no movement speed. The character starts in the module's start room.

- **Moving.** The character goes through an exit to the room beyond. Entering a room whose fight has not been won begins it at once: initiative is rolled, and opponents who win it act first. In the middle of a fight it can't simply go: it may only flee (below), or leave while every foe left is turned (#341, D13). A room whose fight was won stays clear.
- **Fleeing a fight (#396, a house rule).** On its turn, with its action, the character may flee through any exit it could otherwise go through: a shut door, a closed way and a way not yet found are refused ("Door shut", "Way closed", "There is no way from here to there"), without a roll. Before it goes, each opponent still in the fight and able to act (not incapacitated, so not turned, asleep or paralysed) makes one attack against it, in initiative order, standing in for opportunity attacks without positions (D1): with Multiattack, one of its attacks, a die choosing which as on its turn. A hit may wait for Uncanny Dodge or a reaction spell as any other does; the flight goes on once it is answered. 0 HP from one of these attacks is a defeat in the fight's defeat ending, and the character never leaves. A Rogue that has taken Disengage this turn (Cunning Action, a bonus action) draws no attacks. Then it goes through the exit as a move does: a trap on the way springs, and the move counts (#395). The fight stays unresolved in its room, as a turned-away fight does (D13): its fallen stay fallen, its opponents that fled or surrendered stay gone (and count as driven off or spared if it is won later), and those still standing keep their hit points and are met again on coming back, their conditions and the character's effects lasting the fight ended. Foes don't follow (D2): pursuit needs monster movement. A wandering encounter fled (#335) is met again only if a later rest is interrupted, with the same wounds. Fleeing into an exit room offers leaving as usual, which ends the adventure in an escape. The fight bar's **Flee** row, on one line, offers a button for each exit the character could take, named by the room it leads to (its accessible name "Flee to Foot of the Stair"), and after them the attacks a flight would draw, the same for every exit: "Draws an attack from Giant Rat" for one foe, "Draws 2 attacks" for several (naming them in its tooltip), "Draws no attacks" once disengaged, or the reason every flight is refused now ("Action used"). An exit behind a shut door or a closed way gets no button. The AI DM's `flee` tool lists only those exits and says whose attacks the flight draws; a Rogue's `disengage` tool is offered beside it, and only while some exit is open to flee by. The card reads "You turn to flee: Giant Rat and Zombie each get an attack on you as you go.", then each attack, then "You get away from the Rat-Gnawed Cellar into the Foot of the Stair, leaving Giant Rat and Zombie behind. The fight is unresolved: they wait there if you come back, wounds and all, and the fallen stay fallen." A monster's own fleeing is morale (see Combat), not this.
- **Regions and moves (#395).** A module may group its rooms into **regions**, such as an upper and a lower level: a list of rooms with a display name, not a map. In such a module every room belongs to one region. The room panel shows the current room's region above its name, and the AI DM's scene names it; a module without regions shows none. The adventure session counts the character's **moves**: each time it goes through an exit into another room, sneaking in included, the count rises by one; a refused move, or a trap on the way that ends the adventure, adds none. The count is part of the session's state, so a reload replays it, and a trace records it after every turn. A region's wandering encounter table (#403, below) rolls on its count of moves; later increment 17 tickets hang rest limits, the known-rooms panel and the gate's region sweep on regions and the count.
- **Flags (#397).** A module may declare **flags**: named facts about the adventure session, such as "the portcullis is raised", that only ever turn on (D3: a door that opens and later shuts is two flags). The engine alone sets a flag, from what the character does: a feature examined (`sets` on the feature, whatever its check's band), an item taken or given (`sets` on the item), a room first entered (`sets` on the room; the start room's is set as the adventure begins), an encounter's outcome (`sets` on the encounter: `won`, `bypassed` for slipping past it unfought, `peaceful` for ending it peacefully, or `fled` for the character fleeing it), or a check's band (a `set` effect). Setting a flag again changes nothing. A passage may be open only once a flag is set (`openWhen`) or closed once one is (`closeWhen`), anywhere in the module, as a band's open and close effects are in one check: a pulled lever in one room raises a portcullis in another. A way a flag keeps shut or has closed is refused like a band-closed one ("Way closed"), its door and trap gone with it while it is closed; the room panel shows "The way is closed." and, once a flag opens it, "The way is open now.", and the AI DM's scene says "The way is closed." or "This way has opened." beside the exit. When an action sets a flag that opens or closes a way, its card says so, as a band's does ("The way between the Foot of the Stair and the Rat-Gnawed Cellar is open."). The flags set are part of the session's state, in the order set, so a reload replays them from the actions and a trace records them after every turn. No AI DM tool takes a flag: a player asking for one to be set changes nothing. Five AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite flags`, on _The Winch Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/winch-tunnels.json`) cover heaving the winch that raises a portcullis, going through once the way opened, a way still closed, a player asking for the portcullis to be up, and fleeing (#396) a fight in the rat cage, whose gate drops shut behind the character as it comes in: no way is open, so no `flee` tool is offered and the AI DM calls none. The balance harness plays flags through the real runtime: its route planner treats a way a flag opens as shut until the flag is set, and then goes first to the nearest room where something not yet done sets the flag for sure, exactly as the validator counts it (`sureFlagSetters` in `src/adventure-5e.ts`: entering the room, examining a feature, taking an item or ending its fight), even a room it has been through, such as one it slipped through unseen; there it examines the features and takes the items as it does everywhere. A flag only some bands of a check set is never planned for. A way a flag has closed is never planned through. Setting a flag uses actions the harness already plays (move, examine, take and fighting), so the played-actions table gains no kind. The Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped module to declare flags.
- **Encounters that appear, vanish or move with flags (#398).** An encounter may be in its room only once a flag is set (`presentWhile`: guards come once the alarm is raised), only until one is (`presentUntil`: they leave), or in another room once one is (`movesTo`: `{ "roomId", "when" }`, so defenders fall back, gather or take up an ambush); at most one of the three. Only an encounter not yet met goes where the flags place it: once the character has sneaked up on it, been lain in wait for, met its reaction, fought it, slipped past it or fled it, it stays where it was met, whatever flags are set after (the session's state remembers where, `metEncounters`). A moved encounter keeps everything else: its opponents, lurking, reaction, sneaking and XP, and what its opponents carry goes with it, so their bodies are searched where they fell. The room panel, the Sneak control (offered toward the room where a fight now waits) and the AI DM's scene all show the encounter where it is now. When a flag places a fight not yet met in the character's own room, its opponents come in at once ("Someone comes into the Foot of the Stair." and their descriptions) and the fight begins as one the character walked into would, after a reaction roll if it reacts, but with no one lying in wait and no one surprised; nothing comes into a room while a fight is under way there, while the character waits there unseen or while opponents react to it. The encounter whose fight wins the adventure, and the wandering encounter, never appear, vanish or move. No room may hold two encounters, whatever their flags: as one met stays, two could both be there. Reachability counts a fight a flag places only where it is sure to be met: one that moves once its new room is reached and either its flag is set for sure or its own room is reached too, one that appears once its room is reached and its flag is set for sure, and one that vanishes never. So when an essential room or every exit lies behind a flag only such a fight sets, the validator names the encounter ("encounter cellar-rat can cut off every exit: once flag alarm is set it moves to room den, where it may never be met, so flag rat-dead its fight sets is never set for sure."). The balance harness plays it through the real runtime, and its route planner places each fight where the flags set so far put it: a flag that moves a fight onto the required path makes it a fight on the path, which the cautious style must win, and the gate's survival rate counts it. No new action kinds.
- **Alarms (#401).** A module may declare an **alarm** (`alarm`: `{ "flag", "text", "fights"? }`): one of its flags, which only **noise** sets. Noise is a sneak into a fight that fails by 5 or more; a fight in one of the alarm's `fights` regions (each `{ "region", "round" }`, the round 1–10) that goes on past that round, whichever encounter it is (the wandering encounter's included); a check (a feature's, a door's force, pick or break, a trap's disarm, a topic's) that lands in a band marked `"noisy": true` (the dropped tray, the stuck door forced); or an opponent that flees a fight and survives it, counted when the fight is won or the character leaves it. The engine raises the alarm once, at the first noise, and remembers what raised it in the session's state (`alarm`: the sneak's encounter, the fight's encounter, room and round, the check and its band, or the fled opponent), so a reload replays it and a trace records it; later noise changes nothing, and noise made by the action that ends the adventure raises nothing. Nothing else may set the alarm's flag: no `sets`, `set` effect or use. What the alarm changes is authored with flag conditions, as any flag's are: an encounter that moves or appears (defenders gathered into one hall, an ambush laid by a door), a passage it closes (a barred gate), a topic withdrawn (a creature that will no longer talk). Once it is raised, the room panel shows the module's `text` under the room's description, in every room ("A gong booms below, and goblin voices shout. The tunnels are roused."), and the card of the action that raised it names the noise ("The alarm is raised by examining the Stacked Crates (failure by 5 or more). A gong booms below …"; "… by a sneak into the Rat-Gnawed Cellar that failed by 5 or more", "… by the fight in the Rat-Gnawed Cellar going on past round 2", "… by Giant Rat that fled the fight in the Rat-Gnawed Cellar and lived"), followed by any way it opened or closed. The result's `alarm` event is what a rule answering the alarm reads. The AI DM's scene says "The alarm is raised:" and the module's words; no tool raises or lowers it, and the prompt (v39) says the engine alone decides it. Four AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite alarm`, on _The Alarmed Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/alarm-tunnels.json`) cover a player asking to raise the alarm, asking for it to be quiet again, asking what the noise was, and taking the crawl round the grate it closed. Reachability never counts the alarm as set: a way only it opens is never counted on, and a way it closes is a closer, refused when it can cut off an essential room or every exit (a passage the alarm closes can't be the only way). The balance harness meets the alarm through the real runtime: its noise comes from actions the harness already plays, the route planner goes round a way it has closed, and the avoid-optional style, where a flag has closed the way through the required rooms, goes round by any room. No new action kinds. The Coldwater Weirgate's weir bell (#412) is the first shipped alarm.
- **Using an item on a feature (#400).** A feature may accept items the character carries (`uses`: up to four, each `{ "item", "text", "effects", "consumes" }`, one per item): fitting the missing stone snake into a door, pouring oil on a coffin, throwing meat to a chained beast. Each use has the module's words, may use the item up (`"consumes": true`), and has 1–6 effects drawn from a band's: a discovery, an item revealed, damage (which can end the adventure in its defeat), a passage opened (a hidden one) or closed, or a flag set. The browser offers **Use … on …** in the feature's row of the action bar, beside Examine, for each item the feature accepts that the character carries ("Use Stone Snake", named in full "Use Stone Snake on Stone Door"); the AI DM has a `use_on` tool listing only the offered pairs, each `feature:item`. A use takes no roll. Its card gives the words, then "The Stone Snake is used up." when it is, then its effects as a band's card does. Each use is made once and remembered in the session's state (`featureUses`), so its revelations follow from it after a reload; a kept item's use stays in the bar, disabled ("Already used"). The engine refuses, without effect, a use in a fight ("Not while you are fighting."), on a feature not here, of an item the character doesn't carry ("You don't carry that."), of an item the feature doesn't accept ("The Potion of Healing is no use on the Stone Door."), and one already made. The balance harness uses an item on a feature as soon as it carries one the feature accepts, with the room's examinations, before it leaves the room (`use-on`, marked played in the harness's played-actions table); its route planner treats a hidden way only a use opens as shut until a use has opened it, fetches the item first and then goes to the feature, as it fetches a key, so the gate's required path goes by the item's room. Three AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite items`, on _The Snake Door_, `adventures/eval/snake-door.json`) cover the right item, the wrong item and an item not carried; prompt v37.
- **Wandering encounter tables by region (#403).** A region may have a **wandering encounter table** (`wandering`: `{ "chance", "every"?, "entries" }`): a chance from 1 to 100, and 1 to 6 entries, each an **omen** (`{ "omen" }`, words only: a distant chain, wet footsteps) or a wandering encounter (`{ "encounterId", "omen" }`, an encounter in no room, with the words it becomes once it has come). The engine rolls the table, a d100 from the session's seeded stream, in three cases: every `every` moves (1–20) within the region (the session counts the moves that end in each such region, `regionMoves`; without `every`, moves never roll); on each short or long rest in the region, before the rest; and when the alarm (#401) is raised, the table of the region the character is in as it is raised. At the chance or less an entry comes, and the same d100 picks it: the entries share the chance equally in order (chance 30 with three entries: 1–10 the first, 11–20 the second, 21–30 the third), so the chance must be at least the number of entries. A wandering encounter that comes begins its fight where the character is, as the wandering encounter of #335 does (its opponents carry nothing and never surrender; its fight is credited once, and losing it ends the adventure in its defeat ending); it **comes at most once** (the session remembers it, `wanderersCome`, even if the character flees its fight) and from then on is its omen. An omen is said and changes nothing, and may come again and again. On a rest, a fight that comes **interrupts** it: the rest restores nothing and isn't counted, as in #335; an omen, or nothing, lets the rest go on. A move whose roll falls due but that meets a fight (or a foe unseen or reacting, or a Tactical Mind offer) loses that roll; the next falls due `every` moves on. The alarm's roll waits instead: raised in a fight, it is rolled as soon as the character is out of it (won, fled or slipped away), still with the table of the region where it was raised (`wanderingDue`). The card shows the d100 ("You listen as you go: d100 37, 100 or less: Far above, a chain clinks against stone, then is still.", "The alarm carries through the dark: d100 40, 100 or less: Prowling Rat comes upon you.", "… over 10: nothing stirs."). Wandering encounters count against a module's encounters, whose cap rises from 20 to 30 (D5). The AI DM's scene says what last wandered into the room ("An omen, heard here: …", "A wandering encounter came upon you here: Prowling Rat."), until the character moves on; no tool summons, rolls for, delays or keeps away a wandering encounter, and the prompt (v40) says the engine alone rolls for them. Four AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite wandering`, on _The Wandering Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/wandering-tunnels.json`) cover a player trying to draw one out, asking for a promise that none will come, asking to skip a roll, and asking what the omen was. The balance harness meets wandering encounters through the real runtime, as part of the moves, rests and alarms it already plays (no new action kinds): their fights count in survival on seeded and always-fail checks like any other, and their XP in the XP limit; the gate reports, for a module with region tables, how many judged runs met one and fell in one ("Wandering encounters, reported (not judged): …"). The Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped module with region tables.
- **Long modules (#404).** A module with two or more regions may declare itself **long** (`"long": true`). A long module counts rests **per region** rather than per adventure, a house rule for a module too big for two short rests and one long rest: the character may take two short rests and one long rest in each region, the long one only at a rest site, so every region of a long module must have a rest site. The session remembers the rests taken in each region (`regionRests`, by region id), so a reload replays them and a trace records them; it still counts the adventure's rests in all (`shortRests`, `longRests`), which the harness's metrics report. A rest is refused at the region's limit ("You have taken the two short rests each region allows: rest again in another region."), and allowed again in a region where the character hasn't used its rests; going back to a region doesn't give its rests back. The rest cards, the character column's rest parts, the AI DM's character status and its `rest` and `long_rest` tools say the rests left "in this region" ("Short rests: 1 of 2 left in this region."). A long module has twice the treasure budget for its maximum level (see Treasure) and an XP limit one level higher (see the balance gate's XP check), D6. The career simulation places a long module in its order like any other, by its minimum recommended level. The Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped long module.
- **Known rooms (#405).** The session remembers the rooms the character has been in (`visitedRoomIds`, the start room first, each once) and the fights it has fled (`fledEncounterIds`), so a reload replays them and a trace records them. The engine projects the **known rooms**: every room visited, and every room seen through a visited room's exits (a hidden way only once opened), grouped by region in the module's order (one group in a module without regions), the current room marked. A visited room has a record, as the engine sees it now: the discoveries made there (features and bodies examined), the creatures talked to and what each said, the doors on its open ways (open or shut) and the traps found or sprung on them, and its fight, once met: under way, won, ended peacefully, slipped past, fled from or still waiting (left with its foes turned, or placed there by a flag after the character met it). A room only seen has its name alone. The browser's **Known rooms** panel, after the room and the fight, lists them as small buttons in a row per region, after the region's name, each marked "(you are here)", "(visited)" or "(seen)" (a visited room went unmarked until the owner asked for the mark, 14 October 2026, as a room left behind looked forgotten); choosing one shows its record below (choosing it again hides it) and never moves the character. No record shows until a room is chosen, since the room panel already shows the current room, and none is chosen after a reload. The browser applies no rule of its own. The AI DM sees them in its scene (#406).
- **Known rooms in the AI DM's scene (#406).** The AI DM's scene has a line for every known room but the character's own (which is the scene's room), in the known rooms' order: a room only seen is "Smugglers' Den (Lower Tunnels): seen, not yet visited."; a visited one gives its ways on as they stand now, each marked "(closed)", "(opened)" for a hidden way a check or a use opened, or "(open now)" for a way a flag opened, then its record: each discovery ("Discovered at the Rusted Lantern: …"), what each creature said ("Old Hermit said of the den: …"), its doors ("Doors: Iron Door to the Strongroom, shut."), the traps found ("Traps: Dart Trap on the way to the Offering Room, armed.") and its fight ("Fight: fled from, against Giant Rat."). The flags the character knows of are those it has seen work, so the summary shows them as the ways they opened or closed, and the alarm as the scene's alarm (#401); a flag's id is never shown, as a flag that moves an unmet fight or changes a topic elsewhere is nothing the character could know. The summary is capped at 4,096 bytes as JSON (`KNOWN_ROOMS_SCENE_BYTES`): over it, the rooms visited first lose their records first and keep their ways, then the rooms only seen are left out, then the rooms visited first. On the fixture long module with every room visited the whole scene stays under 8,192 bytes. The summary decides nothing: no tool takes a known room, `move` still goes only through the scene's exits and the engine refuses any other, and the prompt (v41) tells the AI DM to answer what the character remembers from it, to move one room toward a known room that is not an exit by the ways it gives, and to add nothing it doesn't hold. Three AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite memory`, on _The Remembered Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/memory-tunnels.json`) cover recalling a discovery made in another room, what a creature said, and heading back toward a known room through the right exit; a case may name words its reply must use (`recalls`), without which its narration check fails.

- **Fight actions: using the room in a fight (#402).** A feature may offer a **fight action** (`fightAction`: `{ "once", "check", "bands" }`), the single-player stand-in for teamwork against a big monster: toppling a statue onto an opponent, cutting the chain of a hanging cage, pushing a foe into a chasm. On the character's turn, with its action, it makes the action's check (one approach, or 2–4 as `{ "approaches" }`, each a skill or an ability and a DC only) against one opponent still in the fight, and the band's effects land on that opponent: `{ "type": "damage", "dice", "sides", "modifier", "damageType" }`, dealt as a spell's damage is (its resistances, Undead Fortitude, its fall and its side's morale check); `{ "type": "condition", "condition", "turns" }`, prone, poisoned or paralysed for that many of its turns with no saving throw, through the engine's own condition rules (prone always 1: it gets up on its next turn); or `{ "type": "remove" }`, which takes it out of the fight. A band has words, 1–2 effects of different types, or both; a removal stands alone; a band left out, or with only words, does nothing to the opponent, and an unauthored failure or success by 5 or more counts as plain failure or success. A removed opponent counts as defeated for the fight's XP ("Defeated Scarred Rat"), checks morale as a fall does, and leaves no body, so anything it carried is lost with it (D4; the issue's wording, over the proposal's "carrying its loot with it"), which is why the validator never lets a removal reach an opponent carrying a door's key or an item a use needs. Each fight action is used once per fight (`"once": "fight"`: once in each encounter fought in its room, such as the room's own and a wandering one at a rest site; fleeing and coming back is the same fight) or once per adventure (`"once": "adventure"`), and one that can remove an opponent must be once per adventure (D4: once per feature). Its check is rolled once from the seeded stream, never rerolled, and remembered with its band in the session's state (`fightActions`, with the removed opponents in `removedOpponents`), so a reload replays it; the character's poison (or fear) gives it disadvantage, as conditions give ability checks, and Tactical Mind and Guidance don't apply. The engine refuses, without a roll, a fight action outside a fight, on a feature not here or with none, one already used ("You already used the Leaning Statue in this adventure."), one with several approaches and none chosen, against an opponent not in the fight (fallen, fled, surrendered, or never there), and one when it isn't the character's turn or its action is spent. The browser shows a **Use the room** row in the Fight section with a button for each feature, opponent and approach ("Use Leaning Statue against Scarred Rat with Athletics", the approach under it), disabled with "Already used" once spent; the AI DM has a `use_feature` tool, offered only in a fight while the engine would accept it, listing each `feature:opponent` pair and the approaches each offers (prompt v38). Its card gives "You use the Leaning Statue against Scarred Rat.", the check, the band's words, then what it did ("The Leaning Statue hits Scarred Rat. Damage …", "Scarred Rat is knocked prone …", "Scarred Rat is out of the fight, defeated, and whatever it carried is lost with it."). The balance harness's judged styles never use fight actions, so the gate stays a lower bound; the reported _use-room_ style does (see Use the room, reported), and the gate's one-hit-kill measure, which weighs one attack, never counts them. Three AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite room`, on _The Statue Cellar_, `adventures/eval/statue-cellar.json`) cover toppling the statue onto an opponent, a target not in the fight, and a second use.
- **Features and topics that change with flags (#399).** A feature may have another description while a flag is set (`changed`: `{ "when", "description" }`): the altar after the ritual, the hall after the alarm. Its discovery and check are unchanged, so a discovery made before reads the same after. The room panel, the examine card and the AI DM's scene give the description as it is now. A creature's topic may be offered only once a flag is set (`offeredWhile`: the hermit talks about the crypt once the character has seen it) or only until one is (`offeredUntil`), or both. A topic not offered is in neither the action bar nor the AI DM's scene, and asking about it is refused ("There is no one here to ask about that."). A topic already asked and since withdrawn stays in the room panel with what the creature said, but can't be asked again or retried. A surrendered opponent hands over what it carries only through a topic's `gives` (#238), so such a topic must stay there to ask: it can't be withdrawn, and may wait only on a flag set for sure. As topics' checks may open hidden ways, reachability counts a topic's check only once it is surely offered: never one that can be withdrawn, and one that waits on a flag only once that flag is set for sure. All of it follows from the flags in the session's state, so a reload shows the same.
- **Examining.** Examining a feature (looking at, searching, reading, inspecting or opening it) shows its description and makes its authored discovery, if it has one. A discovery is made once and stays on the room panel; examining again repeats it and finds nothing new. An item hidden in a feature becomes visible when that feature is first examined. Visible and carried items can be examined too. Examining draws no dice, except that a feature may have a check (#281), made the first time it is examined: then its bands, not the examination, make its discovery and reveal what is hidden in it. Examining it again repeats what the band revealed and rolls nothing.
- **Ability checks.** A check is d20 + the ability's modifier, + the proficiency bonus when the character is proficient in the check's skill (twice with Expertise, #306), against the module's DC; it succeeds when the total meets the DC. A natural 20 or 1 is just its number. The Champion's Remarkable Athlete (level 3) gives advantage on Athletics checks. Each check is rolled once and its outcome remembered: trying again is refused, never rerolled, and the action stays in the bar, disabled, with the reason ("Already tried"). Every check site (forcing, picking or breaking a door, searching a room, disarming a trap, asking about a topic) goes through one check path (#280): one d20 from the session's seeded stream, a check event and its card, then the site's own event, and the outcome remembered under the site's id.
- **Graded checks (#281).** An authored check (a feature's, a door's force, pick or break, a trap's disarm, a topic's) grades its total into a band: **failure by 5 or more** (DC − 5 or less), **failure**, **success**, or **success by 5 or more** (DC + 5 or more). Each band the module authors may have words and effects; a failure or success by 5 or more that the module leaves out counts as plain failure or success, and a plain band it leaves out does only what the site always does (a door opens on a success, a trap is disarmed, a topic gets its reply or failure words). The effects are a **discovery** (a feature's authored discovery is made and shows on the room panel), an **item** (an item hidden in a feature is revealed, there to take; like other finds, treasure, coin and gear are found once per character, and a band never awards an item, only reveals it), **damage** (dice of an SRD 5.2 damage type, with no saving throw; at 0 HP the adventure ends in the effect's defeat ending), and a route **opened** or **closed** (#282): a hidden passage is no exit at all until a band opens it, and then it is an exit like any other, from both sides; a closed passage stays closed for the rest of the adventure, its door and trap are gone with it, and going through is refused ("Way closed"). The room panel marks a way a band opened ("A way you found.") and one it closed ("The way is closed."), and the AI DM's room description says which ways a check opened and which are closed. The band is remembered with the check, so what it revealed stays revealed after a reload. The card shows the roll, the band (the compact line shortens a band by 5 or more to "Failure by 5+" or "Success by 5+"), the band's words and each effect, a damage effect with its dice. The AI DM's tools return the band and its effects; its prompt says it narrates only those, and a tool call with any argument the tool doesn't list (such as a band or an effect of its own) is refused without a roll. A room search is one Perception roll for the whole room, so a trap's find DC is never graded.
- **Alternative approaches (#283).** An authored check may offer two to four approaches to one obstacle, each a different skill or ability with its own DC, such as Athletics (DC 12) or Acrobatics (DC 14) to get over a wall, or Persuasion or Intimidation to get past a guard. The bands and their effects belong to the obstacle, whichever approach is rolled. An Athletics or Strength approach may be marked `"movement": "climb"` or `"jump"` (#307), so a Thief makes it with Dexterity (Second-Story Work); marking any other approach is refused. Until one is rolled, the action bar offers one button per approach, its skill shown under it (the button is named in full, such as "Examine Crumbling Wall with Athletics"), and the AI DM's tool for that action takes the approach, listing the ones offered (and null for any other target). An action that names no approach is refused with the choices ("Choose how"), and one naming an approach that isn't offered is refused ("Not offered"), without a roll. Once one approach is rolled, the obstacle's outcome is remembered: the others are withdrawn, one plain button is left (Examine rolls nothing more; Force, Talk and the rest say "Already tried" or "Already asked"), and asking again by any approach is refused, unless the module authors a retry (#284), which offers every approach again.
- **Retries (#284).** A check is never tried again unless its module authors a **retry**: **after a cost**, damage (dice of an SRD 5.2 damage type, with no saving throw) or a carried tool the module names, used up; or **after a changed circumstance**, once the character holds a named item, has made a named discovery or has won a named encounter. A retry is offered only after a failure (or failure by 5 or more), only while its condition holds: a cost retry after every failure while the cost can be paid, and a circumstance retry once for each change, so it is not offered when the circumstance already held at the last try. Its cost is paid before the roll; damage that drops the character to 0 HP ends the adventure in the cost's defeat ending, and the check is not made. The retry rolls the check again from the session's seeded stream, and its band applies on top of what earlier tries revealed (a discovery, item or opened way stays; a closed way stays closed). Once a check's success could only open ways that have all been closed, it offers no other try (#289): a bridge that fell is not there to cross again. A success that would also make a discovery or reveal an item keeps its retry. The site's other actions still refuse a check already made ("Already tried", "Already asked", and Examine rolls nothing): only the retry, its own button named "Try again" with its cost or circumstance under it (such as "Costs 1d4 bludgeoning damage", "Uses up the Iron Spike" or "Knotted Rope"), rolls again; a check with several approaches offers one retry per approach. The AI DM's tool for that check takes `retry` only while some target offers one, listing each and why; `retry: true` where none is offered is refused without a roll ("No other try"), so asking for another try changes nothing.
- **Circumstances (#284).** Each approach to a check may name circumstances that give it **advantage** or **disadvantage**: the character holds a named item, has made a named discovery or has won a named encounter, or a named flag is set (#397), or, with `not`, hasn't (fighting nearby gives disadvantage to listen). Each has an authored name, which the check's card gives as the source ("at advantage (Knotted Rope)"). They combine with Remarkable Athlete as SRD 5.2 says: any advantage and any disadvantage cancel, and several sources are still one advantage. They are judged when the check is rolled, after a retry's cost is paid. The AI DM cannot grant either: a tool call that adds an `advantage` argument is refused without a roll. A trap's find has none, as a room's search is one roll.
- **Tools (#284).** A module item of kind `tool`, such as a rope or an iron spike, does nothing by itself and weighs nothing: a circumstance or a retry's cost names it. It is not kept after the adventure.
- **Tactical Mind (#315).** Right after a level-2+ Fighter fails an ability check at any check site (forcing, picking or breaking a door, searching a room, disarming a trap, asking about a topic, examining a feature), while it has a use of Second Wind left, the browser offers **Tactical Mind: add 1d10** beside the other actions and the AI DM a `tactical_mind` tool. The engine rolls only the d10 and adds it to the check's total; it never rerolls the d20. The check is graded again on the new total, and the new band is remembered in place of the old one, so asking again is still refused. The site's outcome follows the new total (the door opens, the search finds the traps whose DC it meets, the trap is disarmed, the creature answers). When the band changes, the new band's words and effects apply; what the failure already did, such as its damage, stands (a lesser failure deals no damage again), and anything the old band revealed or closed follows the new band instead. The use of Second Wind is spent only if the check now succeeds. The offer lasts until the next action: any other action, accepted, takes it away. It is not offered after a saving throw, a sneak, a lurk, a parley or Hide in a fight, which act on their result at once. The card reads "Tactical Mind: you add 1d10 to the Athletics check. 8 + 4 = 12 against DC 12. Success: a use of Second Wind is spent (1 of 2 left)." The balance harness never uses it: it keeps every use of Second Wind for healing, so the gate stays a lower bound.
- **Saving throws.** A saving throw is d20 + the ability's modifier, + the proficiency bonus in the class's saves (the Fighter's Strength and Constitution, the Rogue's Dexterity and Intelligence), against the DC. Only traps call for one so far.
- **Doors.** A passage may have a door. A stuck door opens with **Force**, a Strength (Athletics) check. A locked door opens with **Pick**, a Dexterity check with thieves' tools (see Thieves' tools), **Break**, the check the module names, or **Unlock**, offered while the character carries the door's key, with no roll. Each approach is tried once; an opened door stays open from both sides. Going through a shut door is refused ("Door shut").
- **Traps.** A passage may have a hidden trap. In a module with traps, every room offers **Search**, one check per room: Wisdom (Perception) to spot a trap or Intelligence (Investigation) to work out where one must be, whichever the character is better at (Perception on a tie, #309). It is one button, not two approaches: the engine chooses the better skill, as no player would choose the worse one. Every search shows the same DC, the lowest find DC of any trap in the module, so the DC never gives a trap away; the search finds each hidden trap on the room's exits whose own find DC the total meets. A found trap shows on its exit and offers **Disarm**, a check tried once; a failed disarm leaves it armed. A module may author a disarm with thieves' tools (see Thieves' tools). Going through an armed trap, found or not, springs it once: a saving throw, then its damage, halved (rounding down) on a success. A trap that drops the character to 0 HP ends the adventure in the trap's defeat ending.
- **Thieves' tools (#309).** SRD 5.2's thieves' tools (25 gp) pick a lock or disarm a trap. **Pick** is a Dexterity check with them, and a module may author a trap's disarm the same way, alone or as one of its approaches; the proficiency bonus is added only when the character's class is proficient with them (the Rogue). The check is offered only while the character carries a set: without one the Pick button and the `pick_lock` tool are not offered, a disarm offers only its other approaches, and asking anyway is refused with the ways still open named: "You carry no thieves' tools, so you can't pick the Iron Door's lock. You could break it open or find its key." A tools check is remembered like any other. The shipped traps (the Abandoned Delve's loose step, the Warden's Crypt's dart trap and the Thornwood Lodge's man-trap) offer thieves' tools or a bare-handed Dexterity check at the same DC (agent-made choice, #309), so a Fighter disarms them as before and a Rogue adds its proficiency.
- **Talking.** A room may hold creatures to talk to, each with authored topics. **Talk** asks about one topic: the creature answers with its authored words, or, for a topic with a check (such as Charisma (Persuasion)), its success or failure words. Each topic is asked once, and the room panel keeps what the creature said.
- **Taking.** A visible item is taken and carried. Taking is refused for anything not visible.
- **Potions of Healing.** Drinking a Potion of Healing restores 2d4 + 2 HP, and a Potion of Greater Healing (#239) 4d4 + 4, never above maximum HP, and uses it up. Outside a fight it is drunk at once; in a fight it takes the bonus action, so it competes with Second Wind. Administering it to another creature is not used, as the party is one character.
- **Not during a fight.** Moving, examining, taking, opening doors, searching, disarming and talking are refused until the fight is won. In a fight a character can only drink a potion, take the combat actions or flee.

The browser's status strip shows the character's HP, an HP bar and its health: healthy, bloodied at half HP or fewer (the SRD 5.2 Bloodied), critical at a quarter or fewer, and defeated at 0. The engine projects the health; the browser applies no rule of its own. In a fight the strip adds the round, whose turn it is, and a pip for each Action (two after Action Surge), the Bonus action and the Reaction; in and out of fights it shows a pip for each Second Wind, Action Surge and Channel Divinity use, and a line with the character's AC (naming the armour and shield worn) and attacks from its gear as it stands. The room panel shows the room (under its region's name, in a module with regions, #395), the exits, the features with any discovery made, and the visible items, leaving out each empty list (a room with no exits, features or items says so in one line); in a fight the panel stays open, below the Fight section and its initiative table. The room panel also shows each exit's door (shut or open) and a found or sprung trap, and each creature with what it has said. The action bar shows the name of each exit, feature, item, carried potion, door, room, found trap and topic with a **Go**, **Examine**, **Take**, **Drink**, **Force**, **Pick**, **Break**, **Unlock**, **Search**, **Disarm** or **Talk** button, whose accessible name is the full action, such as "Examine Rusted Lantern" or "Force Swollen Door". The history shows each check and saving throw as its own roll group: the die, the ability modifier, the proficiency bonus and the DC, with a Success or Failure tag. The server projects whether the engine would accept each one now; one it would refuse stays in place, disabled, with the reason as visible text linked to the button (a potion at full HP says "Full HP"). After an action, focus stays on the clicked button while it is still enabled, and otherwise moves to the newest conversation-history entry, never to an unrelated action.

**Layout (#432).** The adventure screen has three columns from 1500 px wide, up to 1880 px in all: the character (the status strip, "You carry" and the rests) on the left (owner decision, 14 October 2026), the scene (the room, the fight and the known rooms) and the action bar with the history. Tab order follows character, scene, then actions and history. Narrower, it keeps two, up to 1240 px: the scene with the character's lists under it and the status strip held at the top, and the action bar with the history; there "You carry" and the rests come after the scene on screen but before it in tab order. "You carry" groups what the character carries under Worn and wielded, Consumables (potions, spell scrolls, wands, arrows and bolts), Treasure (gems and art objects) and Other (stowed gear, keys and tools), a line per entry with its icon, name and state ("Worn", "In hand", "Stowed") and its verbs on the right, a disabled verb's reason beside it. An entry's description opens from its name, and shows on hover. Treasure starts collapsed when it holds more than four entries; what the player opens or closes stays so until the next adventure.

## Adventure modules

A 5e adventure module is a JSON file in format version 42 (`src/adventure-5e.ts`). It declares its recommended levels (1–5) and a difficulty (easy, medium or hard); its rooms, each with features (each with an optional discovery and an optional check, #281), items (the SRD 5.2 Potion of Healing or Potion of Greater Healing, or a key, optionally hidden in one of the room's features or carried by an opponent of its fight; or treasure, coin, gear, a spell scroll or a wand, which must be, with treasure's catalogue gem or art object, coin's gold, silver and copper pieces, gear's catalogue item, a scroll's `spell` and a wand's catalogue `wand`, #346), creatures to talk to (each with topics, a topic optionally with a check and failure words, and optionally a merchant's stock of catalogue gear and minutes per trade) and an optional encounter; the two-way passages between rooms, each with an optional door (stuck, with a force check; or locked, with any of a key, a pick check and a break check) and an optional trap (its find, `{ "dc" }` only, as the search chooses Perception or Investigation (#309); its disarm check, trigger text, saving throw, damage of an SRD 5.2 type and defeat ending); a check is a Fighter skill or an ability with a DC from 5 to 30, and a Dexterity check may name `"tool": "thieves-tools"` (#309), which only a character carrying thieves' tools can make; a door's pick must be such a check, and only a pick or a trap's disarm may name the tool; a check may instead offer `approaches` (#283): 2–4 of those, each with a different skill, ability or tool; and any check but a trap's find may have `bands` (#281): any of `failure-by-5`, `failure`, `success` and `success-by-5`, each with optional `text` and up to six `effects`, each `{ "type": "discovery", "feature" }`, `{ "type": "item", "item" }` or `{ "type": "damage", "dice", "sides", "modifier", "damageType", "defeatEndingId" }`; each encounter with each opponent's `monster`, the id of a bestiary monster, with an optional `name` and `description` replacing the monster's own, or else its name, description and a one-off SRD 5.2 stat block inline (size, type, AC, average hit points and formula, ability scores, challenge rating, XP, melee attacks, each with an optional rider, its morale, and the optional lists a bestiary stat block may have), an optional `icon` (#377: the id of an icon in `src/icons-5e.ts`, shown beside its name in place of its monster's, or of crossed swords for an inline stat block), an optional `boss: true`, which exempts it from the balance gate's one-hit-kill cap, and an optional `surrender` (#238: a description, topics, each optionally with `gives`, the ids of items the opponent carries, and optional `xp`); its defeat ending and an optional victory ending, an optional `bypassXp` (#302: 1–10,000 XP for slipping past it unfought) an optional `sneakAgain: true` (#302: a fresh sneak after slipping past), an optional `lurking: true` (#303: its opponents lie in wait) and an optional `reaction` (#304: `bands`, the `options` and optional `text` of each of `unfriendly`, `uncertain`, `indifferent` and `friendly`, and an optional `peacefulXp`; #305: an optional `parley`, `toll` and `trade`, see Parley, tolls and trade), and each opponent may be marked `reacts: true`; and its endings. A passage may be `hidden: true` (#282), and a band's effects may also be `{ "type": "open", "passage" }` or `{ "type": "close", "passage" }`. A room may be a `restSite: true` (#335), where a long rest may be taken, and the module may have one `wanderingEncounter` (#335): `{ "encounterId", "chance" }`, an encounter placed in no room and the chance, 1–100 on a d100, that it interrupts each rest (see Wandering encounters and interrupted rests). A module may declare `regions` (#395): 1–10 parts of it, such as an upper and a lower level, each `{ "id", "name" }`; then every room names its `region` (see Regions and moves). A module may declare `flags` (#397): 1–30 ids; a room, a feature and an item may name the flag it `sets`, an encounter may have `sets`, `{ "won", "bypassed", "peaceful", "fled" }`, at least one naming a flag, a band's effects may also be `{ "type": "set", "flag" }`, and a passage may have `openWhen` and `closeWhen`, each a flag (see Flags); an encounter may have one of `presentWhile`, `presentUntil`, each a flag, and `movesTo`, `{ "roomId", "when" }` (#398, see Encounters that appear, vanish or move with flags); a feature may have `changed`, `{ "when", "description" }`, and a topic `offeredWhile` and `offeredUntil`, each a flag (#399, see Features and topics that change with flags); and a feature may have `uses` (#400): up to four, each `{ "item", "text", "effects" }` with an optional `"consumes": true` (see Using an item on a feature); and a feature may have a `fightAction` (#402): `{ "once", "check", "bands" }` (see Fight actions); and a module may declare an `alarm` (#401): `{ "flag", "text" }` with optional `fights`, 1–10 `{ "region", "round" }`, and a check's band may be `"noisy": true` (see Alarms); and a module may declare `routes` (#407): 1–6, each `{ "id", "name", "rooms" }`, its rooms 1–20 of the module's, the rooms the route must pass in order (see Balance gate); and a long module's room may be `avoidable: true` (#408), left out of its region's sweep (see Balance gate). An item may be a `tool` (#284). Any approach but a trap's find may have `advantage` and `disadvantage` (#284), each 1–4 circumstances: `{ "type": "holds", "item", "name" }`, `{ "type": "discovered", "feature", "name" }`, `{ "type": "won", "encounter", "name" }` or `{ "type": "flag", "flag", "name" }` (#397), each with optional `"not": true`; and a check may have a `retry`: `{ "cost": { "type": "damage", "dice", "sides", "modifier", "damageType", "defeatEndingId" } }`, `{ "cost": { "type": "item", "item" } }` or `{ "after": <circumstance> }`. The validator rejects:

- unknown room, encounter, feature and ending references, and an unknown item kind;
- an opponent naming a monster that is not in the bestiary (the message names the module, the opponent and the id), and an opponent with both a bestiary monster and an inline stat block;
- a room that cannot be reached from the start room, and an ending that no encounter, trap or check's damage names;
- a check's band that names an unknown feature as a discovery, a feature without a discovery, an unknown item, an item that is not hidden in a feature, or an ending that is not a defeat; a band no character of the module's highest level can roll at its DC (its total is a natural 1 at −4 up to a natural 20 at +5, plus the proficiency bonus for a skill), naming the check, the band, the total it needs and the range; `bands` with no band, and a band with neither words nor effects; fewer than two or more than four `approaches`, two with the same skill or ability, and `approaches` beside a skill, ability or DC; and an item hidden in a feature with a check that no check's item effect reveals;
- a check, trap or hidden passage that guards the only route to an essential room (one whose fight ends the adventure in victory): such a room must be reachable without a check or a trap, where a locked door counts as open when its key can itself be reached that way (and is not hidden in a feature with a check), or through a check every band of which leaves a way forward (#282: a door's success opens it, and any band's `open` effects open hidden passages; a band no character can roll doesn't count). A passage some band can close is never counted on, and a close that can cut off an essential room or every exit is rejected, naming the check, the passage and the room. A check only thieves' tools can make is never counted on, as the character may carry none (#309): a door only they open is named as such, and a route only they make sure is rejected as needing thieves' tools. The same holds for reaching some exit;
- a circumstance naming an unknown item, feature or encounter, an item that is coin or gear (never held), or a feature without a discovery; a circumstance without a name or of an unknown type; a retry with both or neither of `cost` and `after`, an `after` with `not` (it could never change back), a cost naming an unknown item or one that isn't a tool, or a tool the check's own advantage needs held (it is used up before the roll), and a damage cost whose ending is not a defeat (a cost's ending counts as reached);
- an `open` or `close` naming an unknown passage, an `open` of a passage that isn't hidden, and a hidden passage that no band opens;
- a stuck door with anything but a force check, a locked door with a force check or with neither key, pick nor break, a pick that is not a Dexterity check with thieves' tools, thieves' tools on any check but a pick or a disarm, a key that is not an item of kind key, an unknown skill or ability, and a topic with a check but no failure words (or the reverse);
- an encounter whose victory or defeat ending names the wrong kind, an encounter in no room (but a wandering encounter) or in several, and a module without a victory ending; an encounter (#398) with more than one of `presentWhile`, `presentUntil` and `movesTo`, moving to an unknown room or its own, naming an unknown flag, or ending the adventure in victory; a wandering encounter with any of them; and a room that two encounters may hold;
- a `restSite` that isn't `true`; a `wanderingEncounter` with other keys, naming an unknown encounter, with a chance that isn't a whole number from 1 to 100, or naming an encounter placed in a room, one with a victory ending, `bypassXp`, `sneakAgain`, `lurking` or a reaction, or one with an opponent that can surrender (#335);
- more than 30 encounters, wandering ones included (D5, #403); a region's wandering table (#403) with keys other than `chance`, `every` and `entries`, a chance that isn't a whole number from 1 to 100 or is less than its number of entries, an `every` outside 1–20, no entries or more than 6, an entry without an `omen` or with keys other than `omen` and `encounterId`, or an encounter that couldn't be the `wanderingEncounter` (above); an encounter in two entries; and a module with both a `wanderingEncounter` and a region table;
- a `long` that isn't `true`, and a long module (#404) with fewer than two regions or a region without a rest site;
- routes (#407) other than a list of 1–6, a route with keys other than `id`, `name` and `rooms`, two routes with the same id, a route with no rooms or more than 20, and a route naming an unknown room or one room twice;
- an `avoidable` (#408) that isn't `true`, an avoidable room in a module that isn't long (only a long module is swept), one a declared route names (D8: an avoidable room must lie off every declared route, so the character can leave it unvisited) and one whose fight wins the module's victory; and a long module with a room from which no exit can be reached by passages nothing can close (D9: every region has an exit the character can reach from each of its rooms, so a hurt character can leave with what it found). A passage a flag's `closeWhen`, a band's or a use's close can shut is never counted on, even if the character came in by it; any other passage counts, as one the character has come through stays open. A long module with no exit at all is refused the same way;
- a region with keys other than `id`, `name` and `wandering`, two regions with the same id, a room naming an unknown region, a declared region no room names, and a module where some rooms name a region and others don't (#395);
- flags (#397) given twice; a flag named anywhere (a `sets`, a `set` effect, an `openWhen` or `closeWhen`, a circumstance) that the module doesn't declare; a declared flag nothing sets, and one nothing uses (no passage, circumstance, feature's `changed` or topic's offer names it), as a declared region no room names is refused; an encounter's `sets` naming no outcome or one but `won`, `bypassed`, `peaceful` and `fled`; a hidden passage with `openWhen` (a band opens a hidden passage, a flag one there to see), and a passage that opens and closes on the same flag; a topic offered while and until the same flag, and one that `gives` (#399) but can be withdrawn (`offeredUntil`) or waits on a flag not set for sure; a use (#400) naming an unknown item, an item the character never carries to use (coin, gear, a scroll or a wand), treasure used up, the same item twice on one feature, no effects, or an effect as a band's would be refused. A hidden passage must be opened by a band or a use. A way a use opens counts toward reaching an essential room or an exit only once the feature's room and the item are reached for sure: an item in plain sight or hidden in a feature with no check (not one a band may never reveal, nor one an opponent carries), which nothing else may use up first. A use that closes a way is a closer like a band's, refused when it can cut off an essential room or every exit, naming the use. Reachability extends to flags: as flags only turn on, the rooms reached and the flags set for sure are grown together until neither changes, and the rules above are checked over them. A flag is set for sure by entering a room reached, examining its features, taking its items (but those hidden in a feature with a check or carried by an opponent, whose body may never be searched) and ending its fight, and by a check reached for sure every band of which sets it. A fight's flag is sure only when every final outcome the fight can reach sets it: its `won` flag, and its `peaceful` one too when it is reaction-eligible (#304), as it may end peacefully and never be won; slipping past it (#302) and fleeing it (#396) leave it unresolved, met again on coming back, so neither counts, and neither of their flags is ever counted on. So an essential room, or every exit, reachable only through a way a flag opens that only some bands of a check set is rejected, naming the passage and its flag, and a passage a flag closes is never counted on: one that can cut off an essential room or every exit is rejected, naming the flag;
- a fight action (#402) whose `once` is not `fight` or `adventure`, whose check's approaches name a tool, a movement or circumstances, whose bands author no effect, a band with more than two effects, two of one type or a removal beside another effect, a condition other than prone, poisoned or paralysed, prone for other than 1 turn, damage outside a band's damage ranges; a fight action in a room where no fight is ever fought (no encounter of its own, none flags move there, and no wandering encounter at a rest site); and one that can remove an opponent but is used once per fight, or in a room where a boss (`boss: true`) may be fought (D4: a boss is never removed), or where an opponent may be fought that carries a door's key or an item a use (#400) needs (removed, it would be lost, stranding the character as a lost key does, #331; its other loot may be lost);
- an alarm (#401) naming a flag the module doesn't declare, a region it doesn't declare or one twice, or a round outside 1–10; anything else (a `sets`, a `set` effect, a use) setting the alarm's flag; and a band marked `noisy` in a module without an alarm, or `noisy` other than true.
- an item hidden in a feature with neither a discovery nor a check to reveal it;
- a reaction (#304) with a band missing or with no authored option, a hostile band (it always fights), an option that is not `attack`, `let-pass`, `parley`, `toll` or `trade` or is given twice, a band offering neither `attack` nor `let-pass` (#305: the character could never end the reaction), a band offering a parley, toll or trade its reaction doesn't author and one authored that no band offers, a parley with no approach, more than three, one that isn't Persuasion, Deception or Intimidation or one given twice, a parley band with neither text, shift nor outcome, with both a shift and an outcome, a shift of 0 or beyond ±4, an unknown outcome or `surprise-attack` on any band but `failure-by-5`, a toll without at least one coin, and trade stock that breaks a merchant's rules (below), a reaction-eligible encounter whose fight ends the adventure, a mindless opponent anywhere in a reaction-eligible encounter (an Undead, or a stat block whose morale is `"never"`), and an opponent marked `reacts` in an encounter with no reaction;
- a surrender on an undead or mindless opponent (its morale is `"never"`), on an opponent whose fight ends the adventure, a topic that gives an item that doesn't exist or that the opponent doesn't carry, an item given by two topics, and `gives` on a topic of anything but a surrender;
- treasure, coin or gear that is not hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, coin without an amount of at least one piece, an amount on anything but coin, gear that names no catalogue item, a catalogue item on anything but gear, treasure that names no catalogue gem or art object, and a gem or art object on anything but treasure;
- loot carried by a bestiary opponent that its treasure type couldn't produce (see Monster loot);
- findable treasure worth more than the budget for the module's maximum recommended level, naming the module, the total and the excess, and an item whose tier is not allowed at that level, naming the module, the item and its tier (see Treasure);

## Balance harness

`src/balance-5e.ts` plays any 5e module through the real runtime and measures how dangerous it is. `npm.cmd run balance` reports on every built-in module for each class, or on the module files named after it, then gives the balance gate's verdict on each for every class over the same seeds, and exits with code 1 if any module fails with a named reason or does not qualify; `--seeds <count>`, `--percentiles <p,p>`, `--styles <style,style>`, `--checks <policy>`, `--classes <class,class>` (the report's classes, any of `fighter`, `rogue`, `cleric` and `wizard`; every one by default) and `--json` change the run. The gate always plays every check policy and every class, whatever `--checks` and `--classes` say.

- **Characters.** Each class is played (#310): the Fighter and the Rogue, from #339 a level-1 Cleric from its defaults (the Protector, its default spells and either kit), and from #340 a level-1 Wizard from its defaults (its default spellbook and spells, and either kit). 10,000 creations are rolled with 4d6-drop-lowest from a fixed seed and placed as a fresh creation of the class starts, by its own priority. A Fighter gets `defaultPlacement`, +2 Strength and +1 Constitution, Athletics and Perception, Defense, and mastery of the dagger, mace and shortsword (`FIGHTER_DEFAULT_CHOICES`); a Rogue gets Dexterity first, +2 Dexterity and +1 Constitution, Athletics, Perception, Persuasion and Stealth with Expertise in Perception and Stealth, and mastery of the shortsword and dagger (its creation defaults). The report plays the class's default kit (the Fighter's mace, the Rogue's shortsword and dagger); the gate plays every kit of the class. A Rogue's Dexterity-first build is its usual one. Both play the default masteries, so another choice of masteries is not gated. At level 4 each character makes the gate's level choice (#286, `gateLevelChoice`): the Ability Score Improvement's two points go one at a time to the first ability below 20 in the order attack ability (the class's first primary ability, Strength for the Fighter and Dexterity for the Rogue, #308; or Dexterity for the Dexterity-first build), then the class's ability priority (the Fighter's Strength, Constitution, Dexterity, Wisdom, Charisma, Intelligence), so it is +2 to the attack ability unless that passes 20 (Strength 19 gives +1 Strength and +1 Constitution, Strength 20 gives +2 Constitution); the Fighter's fourth mastery is the weapon the gate is trying when a module places or sells one it can master, and otherwise the first not yet mastered in catalogue order, the longsword; the Rogue's level 4 brings no mastery; a Cleric's or Wizard's +2 goes on its spellcasting ability, Wisdom or Intelligence, and each new level's cantrips, spells and spellbook spells are chosen in its list's order (`withOwedChoices`). Each class ranks the sample by the sum of the six ability modifiers its own creation gives (its background increase may land on an odd or an even score, so the classes' 5th percentile can be different rolls); the weak and strong characters are the 5th and 95th percentiles. Each plays at every recommended level, at full health with that level's XP.
- **Objective and required path.** A module's objective is its victory if it has one, or else getting out with loot (treasure or coin). The required path is the rooms on the route to it through the fewest fights, then the fewest moves: to the victory fight, or to the nearest loot and out by the nearest exit. A key a key-only door needs is fetched on the way. No route goes through a hidden passage that only a topic's check opens (#297): no style talks, so the gate judges the route a player who never talks must take. The nearest loot is the nearest such a player can reach, and a module whose objective only talking reaches fails as `unreachable-objective`. Every other room is optional, and so is its fight. On a declared route (#407) the required path first passes the route's rooms in order, each reached by the fewest fights from the last, then goes on to the objective; the harness plays it with the route's next room as its goal until it has passed them all, and then the objective, so a fight on the route is a fight on its path.
- **Styles.** Each picks only actions `projectActions` offers as available, as the action bar does. _direct_ goes into every room and fights every fight, and heals below a quarter of its hit points; _cautious_ skips optional fights but looks into optional rooms with no fight, searches each room for traps and disarms those it finds, and heals below half; _avoid-optional_ enters only the required rooms, and heals below a quarter, but goes round by any room where a flag has closed the way through them (#401); _stealth-first_ (#302) plays as _cautious_, but sneaks into every fight it would enter and, unseen, slips past it unless its goal (the victory fight, loot or a key) is in that room or the way on is shut, when it springs an ambush; a fight it slipped past is met again if its route comes back; _retreat_ (#396) plays as _cautious_, but in a fight, below its heal threshold with no heal left to take, it flees toward the nearest exit room, through a way out whose room has no fight and from which some way to an exit room passes no fight, after Disengage when it has it; once it has fled it heads out that way and leaves. It flees each fight once (met again, it fights on), and with no such way out it fights on. Only _retreat_ flees. _use-room_ (#402) plays as _cautious_, but in a fight, with no heal to take, uses the room's fight action whenever one is offered, against the opponent with the most hit points left, by its first approach; in a module without fight actions it plays exactly as _cautious_. Only _use-room_ uses fight actions. All of them examine every feature and fallen body, take every item, attack the opponent with the fewest hit points and follow with the light-weapon extra attack on it, use Action Surge once the action is spent, try a shut door's key, then force, pick and break, and plan around a door that holds. A style takes another try (#284) a module offers, but never one whose damage could leave it below its healing threshold, so failing checks can't wear a run down. No style talks: no topic changes the outcome. Each answers a reaction roll (#304) by the run's reaction policy: _attack_ by default, or _peaceful_ (see Reaction rolls).
- **Casting policies** (#348). A caster plays every style by a casting policy: _conserve_, the default, or _spend-early_ (`CASTING_POLICIES`). Both heal below the style's heal threshold as before (a healing spell on itself, the lowest slot first, before a potion), cast Mage Armor once before the first fight and Shield when it turns a hit into a miss, and keep a Spiritual Weapon's bonus-action attack and a Vampiric Touch's repeat going on the weakest foe once cast. Otherwise a turn's action is a damaging cantrip (Fire Bolt, Ray of Frost, Shocking Grasp, Chill Touch or Sacred Flame, whose save the policies cast since #348) on the weakest foe, ahead of a weapon. _conserve_ casts a levelled spell only below the heal threshold with no heal left to take: "cantrips until below the heal threshold". _spend-early_ casts one on every turn it can: with two or more foes left untouched, a control spell (Sleep, Hold Person) on the one with the most hit points, never one immune to it; otherwise the damage spell worth most (`castValue`: its mean damage on each foe it catches, no more than each one's hit points, Spirit Guardians, Spiritual Weapon and Vampiric Touch counting a second round), the higher slot on a tie. While concentrating, neither casts another concentration spell. The policies cast no other buff, curse, Slow or restoration spell. A foe asleep or turned is left alone while others stand (damage would end it); a held one is still attacked. Every run records the spells cast.
- **Check policies** (#285). _seeded_, the default, rolls every check as a player meets it. _always-fail_ and _always-succeed_ make every check (a feature's, a door's force, pick or break, a trap's disarm, a topic's and a room's search for traps, each approach of one) total the lowest or the highest any character of the module's maximum recommended level could make: a natural 1 at the lowest ability modifier (−4), or a natural 20 at the highest (+5) plus the proficiency bonus for a skill (`extremeTotals`). Each check so lands in its worst or best band the validator counts reachable, and an unauthored band by 5 falls back to plain failure or success as usual; a search finds no trap, or every trap it could. The d20 is still drawn, so the rest of the dice (attacks, damage, saving throws) are the seeded ones. Trap saving throws are saves, not checks, and stay seeded. A run that leaves loot a failed check left hidden heads for the exits without it. Loot an opponent carried is lost the same way once the opponent leaves no body to search: it fled, it surrendered (no style talks, so it never hands the loot over), or its encounter ended peacefully (#324); the run heads for the next loot or out. A key lost either way counts as absent (#331): the run plans around its door, and a run left with no way on or out is stranded, naming the lost key and why it was lost, which fails the gate (owner decision): the module must give another way, such as another route or a door that can be picked, forced or broken.
- **Metrics,** per level, percentile and style: survival and completion rates, fights slipped past (shown for _stealth-first_), each ending's count, for each fight its HP lost, rounds and defeat rate, Second Winds, potions, short and long rests, rests interrupted (#335) and HP healed, trap damage, XP and treasure earned, and each opponent's chance of dying to one attack from full HP (hit chance and critical hits counted, from the character's own attack, with a heavy weapon's disadvantage and Graze's damage on a miss, the enemy's resistance, vulnerability or immunity to the attack's damage type, and Undead Fortitude's save, which a critical or radiant hit skips; Sap and Vex affect only a later attack, and Defense only AC, so none of them changes it).
- **Failures.** A module the harness can't play fails with a named reason instead of passing: `unsupported-action` (the runtime offers an action no style knows), `step-limit` (a run takes more than 2,000 actions), `stranded` (a run is left alive with no action that leads on or out; a module that needs something no style does, such as talking, ends this way; the reason names the run's check policy and every check it made, with its band and any passage that band closed, so a check that fails into a dead end is named, and every lost key to a door only a key opens with why it was lost, #331), `unreachable-objective` (no route reaches the objective; when only talking would, the reason says so and names the route talking opens, #297) or `unsimulated-trait` (#235: an opponent lists a trait the encounter engine does not apply, named with the opponent; every trait a stat block may list today is applied, so only an unvalidated module meets it). Multiattack, Rampage and the other traits are played by the real engine, so the harness simulates them as a player meets them.
- **Budget.** `npm.cmd test` gates all seventeen built-in modules for every class once, the Coldwater Weirgate among them as the gate-time canary (#410, #412, below), and its other checks of the shipped modules read those results. The gate has the time budget: the test fails above 350 seconds of reference CPU time (#440; see below). With two classes it took about 61 seconds against a cap of 110 (since the test-suite speed-up, which also stopped `npm.cmd test` replaying the default run, seeds 0–199 with every style and both percentiles, beside the gate). Before then the budget timed the default run: since #321 it took about 58 seconds of reference CPU time for both classes, against about 109 before it, measured back to back on one machine while other work was running (about 90 on the idle machine the budget was first set on), under a cap of 90 seconds of reference CPU time. It was 150 for both classes from #310, 45 for the Fighter alone, and 30 until #289, when the twelfth module took a CI runner just over it. Most of the time is the engine trying each offered action to project the action bar at each step. Two things keep that down (#321): the harness asks the runtime to project only the kinds of action a style may play, so gear changes, trade and talk are never tried, and a character's profile is reused while its sheet's fields are unchanged. With four classes coming to the gate, #347 cut it further without changing any report: the harness projects only the spells a style may cast (each policy's kind of spell is named in `CAST_SPELLS` in `src/balance-5e.ts`; a spell a new policy casts must be named there, or the harness never sees it offered), projects healing actions and spells only below its heal threshold, and while exploring projects what it looks at first (a reaction's answers, Mage Armor, examining something not yet examined, taking) before the rest; a cautious run whose reaction policy decided nothing is reused as the peaceful run; and the 10,000 sample creations are ranked by their ability scores alone, without building each character. Measured back to back on one machine in raw CPU seconds after #343, the gate (two classes judged, two reported) went from about 97 to 57, and each class's full gate over the fourteen modules from 40 to 29 for the Fighter, 24 to 18 for the Rogue, 115 to 29 for the Cleric and 23 to 12 for the Wizard (at the modules it reaches, up to level 3), about 210 to 93 in all. The browser server gated every module for both classes before it served the page, so `npm.cmd run browser` took about 24 seconds to start in that back-to-back measurement, down from 52 before #321. It now reads the verdicts recorded in `adventures/5e/gate-verdicts.json` (`src/gate-verdicts-5e.ts`), each keyed by a SHA-256 hash of the loaded module, and gates only a module changed since they were recorded, so it starts at once. ## Balance gate

A module is offered in the browser only if it qualifies at its declared difficulty for every class creation offers (`gateModule` in `src/balance-5e.ts`; `GATE_CLASSES` is `OFFERED_CLASS_IDS`: the Fighter and the Rogue from #310, the Cleric and the Wizard from #348; `gateAdventure` judges one class), and `npm.cmd run verify` fails if a built-in module doesn't qualify for any one of them. The gate uses the harness's characters of each class and seeds 0–199, plays checks on all three check policies (#285), and checks three things for each class, and a fourth for a caster:

| Difficulty | Required-path survival | One-hit-kill cap |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Easy       | ≥ 95%                  | 50%              |
| Medium     | ≥ 85%                  | 40%              |
| Hard       | ≥ 75%                  | 30%              |

- **Declared routes (#407).** A module may declare its routes: entrance-to-objective paths, each a list of rooms that must be passed in order, such as "through the flooded stair" and "through the guard hall". The gate judges each route as it judges the fewest-fights route (below): the weakest character playing _cautious_ with every kit at every level, on seeded checks and with every check failing, against the difficulty's survival share. A route may pass through a passage a flag opens: the harness goes first to where something sets the flag for sure, as on any required path. The module qualifies only if every route survives; the verdict gives each route's two lines after the fewest-fights route's, naming the route and its weakest kit and level ("Route guard-hall (through the guard hall), too deadly, FAIL: the level 1, 5th percentile Fighter playing cautious survived 0.0% of 4 runs with its weakest kit, mace …"). A route whose required path is the fewest-fights route's shares its runs. A route that can't be followed fails the module as `unfollowable-route`, naming the route, the room no way reaches and why: only talking opens the way (no style talks, #297), or a passage opens only once a flag is set that nothing the character does sets for sure ("forked-tunnels: route sluice (by the sluice) can't be followed: no way reaches room sluice-room: passage alcove-to-sluice opens only once flag sluice-open is set, and nothing the character does sets it for sure."). The fewest-fights route is still judged, and the reported styles (stealth-first, retreat, use-room, the reaction policies, spending early) still play it alone. A module that declares no routes is judged on its fewest-fights route as before. No shipped module declared routes until the Coldwater Weirgate (#412), so no earlier verdict or pinned figure changed. Following a route uses only the moves the harness already plays: no new action kinds.
- **Region sweeps (#408).** A long module's optional rooms are judged too (D8). For each region the gate plays a sweep: the _cautious_ style visits every room of the region not marked `avoidable`, the nearest not yet visited next by the fewest fights (through rooms of other regions where the way goes), and fights what waits in each, then goes on to the objective; the victory room is left to the objective, which comes last. Each sweep is judged as a declared route is (the weakest character with every kit at every level, on seeded checks and with every check failing), but at one difficulty lower than the module's (`sweepDifficulty`): an Easy module's sweeps must clear Medium and a Medium module's Hard; Hard has none lower, so a Hard module's sweeps are judged at Hard. The module qualifies only if every sweep survives; the verdict gives each region's two lines after the routes', naming the region and the difficulty it is judged at ("Sweep of region upper (Upper Tunnels), too deadly, FAIL: the level 3, 5th percentile Fighter playing cautious survived 0.0% of 4 runs with its weakest kit, … hard needs 75.0%."). A deadly room the character may leave alone is marked `avoidable`, which leaves it out of the sweep. The validator refuses an avoidable room a declared route names; the gate also fails a module as `avoidable-on-route` when a declared route's required path passes an avoidable room on the way between the rooms it names, or, in a module that declares no routes, when the fewest-fights route does ("lair-tunnels: room lair is avoidable, but route pit (by the bone pit) passes it: an avoidable room must lie off every declared route."). A sweep that can't reach one of its rooms (only talking opens the way, or a flag nothing sets for sure opens it) fails the module as `unsweepable-region`, naming the region and the room. A module that isn't long, with or without regions, is not swept; the Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped long module, so no earlier verdict or pinned figure changed. Sweeping uses only the moves the harness already plays: no new action kinds.
- **Too deadly.** The weakest character, the 5th percentile, playing _cautious_, must survive at least the difficulty's share of runs with every starting kit at every recommended level. The verdict names the kit and level it survives least with, and lists each. The same check is made again on the _always-fail_ branch, with every check in its worst band: the module must stay completable there (a run stranded with no way on or out fails the gate, naming the checks it made) and the weakest character must still survive the difficulty's share of runs. A seeded run that made no check is the always-fail run on its seed too, so only runs that made one are played again. Survival is judged on the required path a player who never talks must take (#297), so loot that only talking reaches can't make the cautious run give up and "survive" without reaching its objective; a module whose objective only talking reaches fails as `unreachable-objective`. A caster (#348) plays its first 50 seeded runs with each kit at each level by both casting policies (`POLICY_SEED_COUNT`), and every other run, the rest of the seeded ones and every other branch (always-fail, stealth-first, peaceful), by the one that survives more of those 50, as a player keeps to one way of playing; each kit's line names that policy. Its survival is judged only from level 3 (`CASTER_JUDGED_LEVEL`, owner's choice: early magic is weak and unreliable, so a Cleric or Wizard may struggle at levels 1 and 2, as in old-school play): a level below is listed as reported, and a module all of whose levels are below passes the check, which the verdict shows as "reported (not judged below level 3)". The career check (see Career simulation) still requires some weakest-caster career to reach level 5.
- **Too easy.** Early levels are a character's most vulnerable, so they must never be easy. A ranged weapon a module places or a merchant sells is tried like any other weapon, as its opening volley: one attack from full HP in round 1, with no close-combat disadvantage, by a Dexterity-first build of the same character, whose Strength and Dexterity rolls change places and whose +2 goes on Dexterity (#230). An ordinary enemy is any opponent not marked `boss: true` in the module. For the strongest character, the 95th percentile, at the maximum recommended level, with the kit, or the weapon the module places (wielded with the default kit's armour), and the Fighting Style that kill each enemy most often (Great Weapon Fighting counts for a placed two-handed or versatile weapon, Archery for a placed or sold ranged weapon (#225); a tie keeps Defense, the default), the gate takes each ordinary enemy's chance of dying to one attack from full HP (hit chance, critical hits, Fighting Style, masteries, the enemy's damage defences and Undead Fortitude counted, as in the harness, so a mace beats a shortsword against a Skeleton). More than half of the ordinary enemies over the difficulty's cap fails the module. The measure is per attack, not per turn: Action Surge's second attack, the light-weapon extra attack and Extra Attack's second attack are not counted. A module with no ordinary enemies passes. A caster's best repeatable attack (#348, D5) may be a damaging cantrip on its class list, whether or not it knows it, at its level's dice (`spellKillChance`: a spell attack's hit, a critical hit on a 20 doubling the dice, and Potent Cantrip's half on a miss or a save; a saving-throw cantrip's damage on a failed save), judged when it beats every weapon; the verdict names the cantrip. Beside it the gate reports, never judges, each ordinary enemy's chance of dying to one levelled damage spell on the class list at the highest slot the character has (every ray of Scorching Ray at it, Magic Missile's darts, a save spell's half on a success, Ice Knife's burst; damage at the end of a later turn is not counted).
- **Too easy by control** (#348, D5, a caster with control at the module's maximum level). For the same strongest character, each ordinary enemy's chance that one control used at the fight's opening takes it out of the fight (`controlChance`): the control spells on its class list it has slots for and the Cleric's Turn Undead, the best of them for each enemy. Out of the fight means unable to act on its next three turns (`CONTROL_TURNS`, owner's choice, about a fight's length): it fails its first saving throw and each save it repeats at the end of its turns until then (Turn Undead's and Hold Person's every turn; Sleep's once, as its second failure leaves the target Unconscious for the spell, and since #369 both of Sleep's are rolled with advantage, so four d20s must fail). An immune enemy, or one of a creature type the control doesn't work on, has no chance. More than half of the ordinary enemies over the difficulty's one-hit-kill cap fails the module, as for one attack. Judged on the first save alone, Sleep stopped most foes 60–75% of the time and would have failed 11 modules, and Turn Undead three, so the owner chose (11 October 2026) to weaken control rather than the modules: Sleep and Hold Person left the Wizard's and Cleric's lists, and a turned undead repeats its save each turn. #369 brought them back (owner's choice, 11 October 2026): Hold Person as written, as no shipped module of level 3 or more (its 2nd-level slot) had an ordinary humanoid foe (the Coldwater Weirgate's Bandits, #412, are the first: Hold Person stops each for three turns 27.5% of the time, under Medium's 40% cap); Sleep with its target saving with advantage, since a save at the end of every turn still failed three modules and a 2nd-level Sleep fails as the DC rises with level. Sleep now stops its likeliest foe for three turns at most 31.6% of the time (the Counting-House's goblins and the Silvervein Mine's kobolds, against 40%), and 24.0% in the Smugglers' Cellar and the Thornwood Lodge, against 30%, the closest; no ordinary foe in a shipped module is over its cap with any control, which `tests/shipped-modules.test.mjs` checks for the Wizard and the Cleric. Turn Undead now stops a zombie or skeleton for three turns 27–34% of the time at levels 2–3, under every cap in the shipped modules. In the six modules with undead foes (#349, #350), at each module's maximum level: Abandoned Delve 27.5% (zombie) and 34.3% (skeletons) against a 40% cap, Drowned Chapel 27.5% against 50%, Gravedigger's Lodge 27.5% against 30% (the closest), Hollow Chantry 27.5% (zombies and the ghoul) and 34.3% (the skeleton) against 40%, Silvervein Mine 27.5% and 34.3% against 40%, and Warden's Crypt 27.5% against 40%; `tests/shipped-modules.test.mjs` checks that every ordinary (non-boss) undead foe in a shipped module is measured against Turn Undead, and pins these figures.
- **XP.** All the XP a module offers (every encounter's stat-block XP, wandering encounters' included (#335, #403), or the XP authored for slipping past it (#302) or for ending it peacefully (#304) when that is more, and the most any ending awards) must not take a character past the maximum recommended level + 1, or + 2 in a long module (#404, D6). The character starts one XP short of the level above the maximum, the worst case, and the SRD 5.2 XP table counts levels 6 (14,000 XP) and 7 (23,000 XP) for this check even though characters stop at level 5, so a module whose maximum is 4 may offer XP up to one short of level 6, and one whose maximum is 5 up to one short of level 7 (a long one's up to one short of level 8, 34,000 XP). The _always-succeed_ branch counts toward the limit: the strongest character at the maximum recommended level plays _direct_ with every check in its best band, so every way a check opens is open and every fight is fought, over the seeds in order until a run earns all the XP offered; the most XP a run earned counts if it is more than the module offers. Every XP award a module can make today is in the offered total, so this branch can't fail a module yet; it guards later awards that only a check can reach.

A module the harness can't play fails the gate with the harness's named reason. The verdict gives each class in turn ("… qualifies as hard for the Rogue."), naming each check, whether it passed and, for too easy, the enemies over the cap (and the cantrip that kills each, for a caster), with a line for each check-policy branch: survival on seeded checks, survival when every check fails, and the XP earned when every check succeeds. For a caster it adds too easy by control, the levelled spells' report and the spend-early runs' survival over the 50 seeds both policies play (reported, never judged), with the spells cast and XP earned a run. Beside them it reports, never judging them, the rests the weakest character took in its judged runs (#335): short and long rests a run, and rests a run a wandering encounter interrupted. Every shipped module qualifies on all three branches (#285) without change: only the Abandoned Delve (96.0% seeded, 93.0% when every check fails, before its #442 expansion) and the Warden's Crypt (96.0%, 95.0%) survive less when their checks fail, both still well inside Medium; the others' cautious runs make no check that changes the outcome.

The judged styles never use fight actions, so the gate stays a lower bound; `passesGate` leaves the report out. The one-hit-kill measure weighs one attack of the strongest character, so it never counts a fight action. The harness's played-actions table marks `use-feature` played (by _use-room_ only).

**Alarm, reported (#401).** For a module with an alarm, beside its checks the gate reports the judged runs that raised it: the weakest character's _cautious_ runs with every kit at every recommended level, on seeded checks and with every check failing, how many of each raised it, and those runs by checks, kit, level and what raised it, with their seeds ("Alarm, reported (not judged): … raised it in 438 of 600 seeded runs and 600 of 600 always-fail runs: seeded mace level 1, by examining the Stacked Crates (failure by 5 or more), seeds 0, 7–9, …"). It is never judged; a module without an alarm has no such report.

**Wandering, reported (#403).** For a module whose regions have wandering encounter tables, beside its checks the gate reports the wandering fights the judged runs met: the weakest character's _cautious_ runs with every kit at every recommended level, on seeded checks and with every check failing, how many of each met one and how many fell in one, and the XP the wandering encounters offer ("Wandering encounters, reported (not judged): … met one in 8 of 12 seeded runs and 8 of 12 always-fail runs, and fell in one in 0 and 0; they count in survival, and their 25 XP in the XP limit."). Those fights already count in the judged survival like any other, and their XP in the XP limit; the report is never judged. A module without region tables has no such report.

**Formats (#405).** The save (62) and trace (56) formats bump: a session records the rooms visited and the fights fled.

**Formats (#404).** The adventure (40), save (61) and trace (55) formats bump: a module may declare itself long, and a session records the rests taken in each region of a long module.

**Formats (#403).** The adventure (39), save (60) and trace (54) formats bump: a region may have a wandering encounter table, and a session records the moves within each region, the wandering encounters that have come, an alarm roll waiting, and the omen last heard. The prompt (v40) says the engine alone rolls for wandering encounters, and the scene says what last wandered into the room.

**Formats (#401).** The adventure (38), save (59) and trace (53) formats bump: a module may declare an alarm and mark a band noisy, and a session records what raised the alarm. The prompt (v39) says the engine alone raises the alarm and no tool raises or lowers it.

**Formats (#402).** The adventure (37), save (58) and trace (52) formats bump: a feature may have a fight action, and a session records the fight actions used and the opponents they removed. The prompt (v38) says how fight actions work, and the AI DM gains the `use_feature` tool.

**Formats (#396).** The save (54) and trace (48) formats bump: a session may record the wounds of a fight's opponents left standing when the character fled it, and the actions and events of fleeing and Disengage. The prompt (v36) says how fleeing works, and the AI DM gains the `flee` and `disengage` tools.

**Stealth-first, reported (#302).** Beside its checks the gate reports the weakest character playing _stealth-first_ with every kit at every recommended level on seeded checks: its survival with its weakest kit, and over every run its completion rate, the fights it slipped past and the XP it earned. The report is never judged: a module that stealth could trivialise is judged by the checks above, whose XP limit counts the XP authored for slipping past. `passesGate`, which decides what the browser offers, leaves it out, so it costs nothing at startup. No shipped module authors bypass XP, so sneaking changed no verdict; the Fighter, without Stealth proficiency, slips past 0.7 fights a run at most (the Abandoned Delve).

## Career simulation

Can a new character reach the top level by playing the shipped modules (#290)? `npm.cmd run career` (`src/career-5e.ts`) plays one career on each of seeds 0–199 through every built-in module, or the module files named after it, and exits with code 1 if no career reaches the required level; `--seeds <count>`, `--required-level <level>`, `--class <class>` (`fighter`, the default, `rogue`, `cleric` or `wizard`: any class the gate judges) and `--json` change the run. `npm.cmd run verify` makes the same check on the shipped modules for a career of every class the gate judges (the Fighter and the Rogue from #310, the Cleric and the Wizard from #348, D14), and fails if any class's careers reach no further than level 4.

- **The character.** The gate's weakest of its class, the 5th percentile, created with the class's default choices and kit (the Fighter's mace, the Rogue's shortsword and dagger, the Cleric's mace and daggers, the Wizard's quarterstaff and dagger), starting at level 1 with 0 XP. A caster plays the default casting policy, _conserve_, and makes each new level's cantrip, spell and spellbook choices in its list's order.
- **Order.** The modules in the browser's order: by recommended level range, lowest first, then easy to hard, then by id. A career plays each module once, as soon as its level reaches the module's minimum recommended level, and skips one it hasn't reached yet; a module no career reaches is reported as not played. It plays every module in the gate's style, _cautious_, with seeded checks.
- **Between modules.** A surviving ending settles the character as the character library does: it keeps exactly what it holds at the end (gear, potions, treasure and coin), is credited its XP, and rests to full health. A pending level-4 choice is made by the gate's policy before the next module: +2 to the attack ability, and for the Fighter the longsword mastered. A defeat ends the career in that module, as it does in the library.
- **The check.** It passes when some career reaches the required level: 5, the top level, since the Thornwood Lodge shipped (#291; 4 until then). A module set whose XP can't carry any career that far fails, and so does one where every career falls first. Each module is gated on its own (see Balance gate); the career takes the modules as given.
- **The report** names each module in the order played, with how many careers played it and at what level, the share that survived it, the mean XP it credited the survivors and how many fell there; then how many careers ended at each level and how many fell. In #290's run the weakest character reached level 4 in 62% of careers; most falls were in the two level-1 modules played first. With the Thornwood Lodge (#291), 52% of careers reach level 5: every career that survives the Lodge, where 15 of the 119 that play it fall. Since #310, with the Lodge's one mastiff, 55% of Fighter careers and 68% of Rogue careers reach level 5. The Fighter falls most in the Smugglers' Cellar (36 careers) and the Barrow (17); the Rogue in the Smugglers' Cellar (20) and the Lodge (16). Since the Champion's initiative advantage (#315), 56.5% of Fighter careers reach level 5, and those that reach the Lodge fall there less (4 of 117). With the casters judged (#348), 58% of Fighter, 68.5% of Rogue, 19.5% of Cleric and 18% of Wizard careers reach level 5. The casters fall mostly at level 1, as the old-school curve intends: the Cleric in the Robbers' Barrow (82 careers) and the Smugglers' Cellar (50), the Wizard in the Smugglers' Cellar (55), the Barrow (40) and the Abandoned Delve (26).

## Encounter estimate

Before writing a fight into a module, an author can estimate its danger from bestiary ids (#236): `npm.cmd run estimate -- --levels 2-3 wolf:2` (`src/estimate-5e.ts`). Each argument is a bestiary id with an optional count (`wolf:2`, or `bandit` for one), up to 8 monsters in all, as in a module; `--levels` gives the character levels (1–5, by default 1–3, `--levels 2` for one), and `--seeds`, `--percentiles`, `--styles` and `--json` are the harness's. `--bestiary <file>` estimates against another bestiary file instead of the built-in one.

- **What it plays.** The fight becomes a one-room module: its start room holds the fight, winning ends it in victory and losing in defeat, and monsters sharing a name are numbered. The harness plays that module through the real runtime with the same sampled characters and seeds as the gate, at every level, for each percentile character (the 5th and 95th by default), with every starting kit, in every style.
- **What it reports.** For each level and character, each monster's chance of dying to one attack from full HP, with the kit and Fighting Style that kill it most often (measured as the gate measures its strongest character at the maximum level), and which difficulties' caps it is over; then for each kit and style the survival rate, HP lost in the fight and rounds. Last, the gate's verdict on the module at easy, medium and hard, which always uses the gate's own characters (5th and 95th percentiles), style and seeds whatever `--percentiles` and `--styles` say: the three checks of the balance gate, so a module whose only fight is this one, at these levels, qualifies at the same difficulties. A module with more rooms, potions or other fights before it will measure differently, and the estimate counts every monster as ordinary: mark a boss in the module to exempt it from the cap.
- **Failures.** A bestiary id that isn't in the bestiary fails as `unknown-monster`, and a monster with a trait the encounter engine does not apply as `unsimulated-trait`, each naming the monster; the harness's own failures (`step-limit` and the rest) are reported the same way. The exit code is 1 on a named failure and 2 on a malformed command line.

## House rules

- A player character at 0 HP is defeated at once; there are no death saving throws.
- Second Wind is refused at full health, so a use is never wasted. The 2024 rules allow it.
- At most one long rest per adventure, only at a module's rest site (#335); SRD 5.2 allows one a day anywhere safe. An interrupted rest takes no time and may be taken again.
- At most two short rests per adventure (#334), each in a room with no unresolved hostile encounter; SRD 5.2 limits them only by time. A short rest takes no time, as there is no clock, and a rest that would restore nothing is refused.
- A Potion of Healing is refused at full health for the same reason, and so is a healing spell (#336).
- Magic Missile's darts all strike the one target the spell names (#336); SRD 5.2 lets them be split among targets. Their damage is added up and lands as one: resistance halves the total, and Undead Fortitude's DC counts it all.
- Guidance (#339) adds its d4 to the next graded check, whatever its skill, and then ends; SRD 5.2 adds it to every check with one chosen skill for the minute.
- Prepared spells change only between adventures, in the library (D8, #339); SRD 5.2 lets a Cleric change them after a long rest, and a Wizard (#340) after a long rest from its spellbook.
- A new level's spells are prepared on the sheet before the next adventure (#341); SRD 5.2 lets a Cleric prepare them after a long rest. A new level's cantrip is learned on the sheet the same way (#342), and cantrips are never swapped; SRD 5.2 lets a Cleric replace one cantrip on gaining a level.
- Spirit Guardians end with the fight (#342), not at the next rest as D9 gives a 10-minute spell.
- Bestow Curse lays one of two curses (#342): disadvantage on the target's attacks against the character, or 1d8 necrotic more from its attacks and spells.
- Leaving a room is allowed mid-fight only while every foe left is turned (#341, D13) or by fleeing (#396); the fight stays unresolved, its fallen stay fallen, and its standing foes are met again, unturned, on coming back, with the hit points they had when the character left.
- Fleeing a fight (#396) draws one attack from each foe still in it and able to act, before the character goes, in place of opportunity attacks; SRD 5.2 gives an opportunity attack only to a foe the character moves out of reach of, with its reaction. A Rogue's Disengage avoids them. Foes never follow.
- Prayer of Healing's short rest gives back feature uses only, not hit dice, and doesn't count toward the two short rests (#341).
- Arcane Recovery (#340) applies by itself on the first short rest that has a slot to regain; SRD 5.2 lets the Wizard choose when, and which slots.
- The Wizard's spellbook (#340) is part of the sheet, not an item: it can't be lost, stolen or sold, and spells can't be copied into it yet. A new level's spellbook spells and Scholar's Expertise are chosen on the sheet before the next adventure (#343); SRD 5.2 adds them as the level is gained.
- Evocation Savant (#343) is flavour only: its free Evocation spells are omitted.
- Memorize Spell's swap (#344) lasts only the adventure it is made in; the library's prepared spells are unchanged (D8).
- Haste (#344) is cast on the caster only, its extra action makes one weapon attack only, and its lethargy follows any end before the fight's. Slow (#344) catches four, not six, and its riders on speed, reactions and spellcasting are omitted.
- Spell durations are bands, not minutes (#337, D9): up to 1 minute ends with the fight, 10 minutes to 1 hour at the next rest, 8 hours at a long rest or the adventure's end. Mage Armor gives nothing while armour is worn rather than ending.
- Ability scores are rolled once with 4d6-drop-lowest and placed freely; there are no rerolls.
- Starting gear is common tier only: each class's kits of leather armour and its weapons, of equal value within one class, and no starting coin.
- Sneak Attack (#306) is dealt whenever its rules are met; SRD 5.2 lets the Rogue choose. Without a choice of when to deal it, it is never saved for a later attack.
- Hide (#307) is a Stealth check against the opponents' best passive Perception, not SRD 5.2's DC 15, and gives only advantage on the next attack, not the Invisible condition (see The Rogue, levels 2 and 3).
- Cunning Strike (#308) is chosen with the attack, before the d20 is rolled, and applies only if the attack hits and deals Sneak Attack; a miss spends nothing. Its Poison needs no Poisoner's Kit (see The Rogue, levels 4 and 5).
- Uncanny Dodge (#308) is answered when the hit is known, before its damage is rolled.
- A character holds a second weapon only for the light-weapon extra attack, so both weapons must be light. Two weapons and a shield, or a two-handed weapon with a shield or a second weapon, are refused.
- A character always holds a weapon: its last one can be swapped but not unequipped, as unarmed strikes are not modelled.
- In a fight, one weapon change per turn, with the object interaction; armour and shields change only outside a fight.
- A character carries up to its Strength × 15 lb; taking or buying more is refused rather than slowing it, as there are no positions.
- Armour's stealth disadvantage gives disadvantage on the Stealth check to sneak in (#301).
- Lurking opponents (#303) make one Stealth roll for the group, by the least stealthy of them; SRD 5.2 leaves a group's hiding to the DM. Sneak is offered beside a lurking fight's room as beside any fight's, so the bar shows that a fight waits there.
- Morale is a house rule on top of 5e (see Morale), and so are reaction rolls (#304, see Reaction rolls).

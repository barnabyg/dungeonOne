# 5e rules

Dungeon One uses the 2024 fifth-edition rules in SRD 5.2 ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-start-afresh-on-5e-and-suspend-compatibility.md)). This is the game's rules document: character creation, combat, exploration and items, checks, traps and talk, adventure modules and the balance gate, and the house rules and position rules that depart from SRD 5.2.

This work includes material from the System Reference Document 5.2 ("SRD 5.2") by Wizards of the Coast LLC, available at https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd. The SRD 5.2 is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License, available at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.

## In scope

Ability modifiers, proficiency bonus, saving throws, skills, advantage and disadvantage, action, bonus action and reaction, the 5e XP table and SRD 5.2 stat blocks. Feats beyond the level-4 Ability Score Improvement, multiclassing, species and backgrounds' features other than the ability increase are not used.

## Creating a Fighter

Every character is a Fighter created at level 1 with 0 XP, with a starting kit and three weapon masteries chosen at creation (below). The library refuses any new sheet at another level, with XP, below full health, or not made from its pending dice.

**Ability scores.** The engine rolls 4d6 six times from a seeded stream of its own: SHA-256 of the browser's startup seed and the library's creation number and drops the lowest die of each roll. The six rolls are saved in the character library as a pending creation before the player sees them, and every die, including each dropped one, is shown. While the creation is pending, reloading the page, restarting the server (with any seed) or leaving the creation screen shows the same dice; there are no rerolls and no minimum set. The player places the six results on the six abilities in any order. A fresh creation starts with a default placement: the highest kept total on Strength, then the rest, highest first, in the Fighter priority order Strength, Constitution, Dexterity, Wisdom, Charisma, Intelligence (Constitution for hit points, Dexterity for AC in leather and initiative, Wisdom for Perception and Wisdom saves). Tied totals keep their roll order: the earlier roll takes the higher-priority ability. The default background increase is +2 Strength and +1 Constitution. Saving a character spends its dice; the next creation rolls a new set. Deleting a character and starting again is allowed. A character sheet's **Delete character** control opens a confirmation that says deletion is permanent; the final Delete button is enabled only once the player types the character's name exactly, case and spaces included. Cancel, Escape and closing the confirmation change nothing. There is no undo, archive or recycle bin. Deleting removes only that character: a pending creation keeps its dice, so deletion is never a reroll, and the only way to new dice is still to save a character and start another creation.

**Background increase.** After placement the player adds +2 to one ability and +1 to another, or +1 to three. No score can exceed 20 (4d6 keeps at most 18, so +2 reaches 20 at most). The creation screen places the rolls and the increase in one table, one row per ability. Each row's Roll column shows the four dice of the roll placed there, the dropped one marked, so each roll appears once. Its score, modifier and whether it has reached 20 come from the server, which projects them for the current choices, as it does the two-skill limit; the page computes none of them. The player first picks the increase's shape, then its abilities: with +2 and +1 a bonus moves by swapping with the ability that held it, and with +1 to three no fourth ability can be ticked, so an impossible increase cannot be entered. The server still validates every choice. Backgrounds' other features are not used. Placement and the increase can change until the character is saved; the dice cannot.

**Modifiers.** (score − 10) / 2, rounded down: 3 is −4, 8–9 are −1, 10–11 are +0, 12–13 are +1, 18–19 are +4, 20 is +5.

**Starting kit (#207).** Creation offers three named kits of common-tier gear, and no starting coin. The owner chose to start nearly empty-handed (6 October 2026): a character finds better gear in its first dungeons. The kits are of equal value within 3 gp (SRD 5.2 prices allow no three exactly equal loadouts):

| Kit                      | Gear                         | Value      | With it                                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mace and leather         | Leather armour, mace         | 15 gp      | One 1d6 bludgeoning attack; Sap when mastered                                         |
| Two daggers and leather  | Leather armour, two daggers  | 14 gp      | A 1d4 piercing attack (finesse), then the light-weapon extra attack, Nick if mastered |
| Club, dagger and leather | Leather armour, club, dagger | 12 gp 1 sp | A 1d4 bludgeoning attack, then the extra attack with the dagger, Nick if mastered     |

The creation screen shows each kit's items, value, AC, attack and damage for the current scores, Fighting Style and masteries before saving; the server projects them. Creation starts with the mace kit. The sheet keeps the equipment as a list, armour first and then the weapon attacked with; any legal loadout is a valid sheet, so gear found later (#209) needs no new format.

**Weapon Mastery (#207).** The 2024 Fighter masters three kinds of weapon, chosen at creation from the catalogue's weapons whose mastery the game uses: dagger (Nick), mace (Sap), shortsword (Vex), longsword (Sap) and greatsword (Graze). The club's Slow needs positions and is not offered. Creation starts with the dagger, mace and shortsword, which covers every kit weapon. Once three are ticked the rest are disabled. A mastery applies only while the character wields that weapon.

## The Fighter, levels 1–3

| Level | XP  | Proficiency bonus | HP                 | Features                                        |
| ----- | --- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | 0   | +2                | 10 + Con modifier  | Fighting Style, Second Wind, Weapon Mastery     |
| 2     | 300 | +2                | + 6 + Con modifier | Action Surge (1 use), Tactical Mind             |
| 3     | 900 | +2                | + 6 + Con modifier | Champion: Improved Critical, Remarkable Athlete |

Level 3 is the highest supported level; XP above 900 is kept.

**Earning XP and treasure (#133).** XP is credited only when an adventure ends with the character alive, in a victory or an escape. It comes from each encounter won (the sum of its opponents' stat-block XP) and from any XP the ending awards. Treasure is an item found by examining a feature, or by searching an opponent's body once its fight is won, and carried out alive. Each XP award and each treasure is earned once per character: the sheet keeps a ledger of the treasure found and the XP awards credited, apart from what the character holds, and treasure in the ledger is not there to find on a later visit, even once the character no longer holds it. The level follows the XP at once, and the level-up card names the new hit points and features. A defeat or an abandoned adventure credits nothing.

**Coin (#208).** Coin is SRD 5.2 copper, silver and gold pieces (1 sp = 10 cp, 1 gp = 100 cp). A character's purse is held in copper and shown in mixed denominations, largest first ("3 gp 4 sp"); a new character has none. A module authors coin as an item of kind `coin` with its gold, silver and copper pieces, hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, like treasure; the engine, not the AI DM, decides how much it holds. Taking it empties it into the purse at once, and the ledger records it as a find like a treasure, so it is not there to find again. Carrying coin found in the adventure out counts as escaping with loot, even once it is spent. Coin buys gear from merchants inside adventures (#210); there is no market between adventures.

**Settling replaces possessions (#206).** An adventure starts holding the character's possessions: its equipment, the treasure it holds and its purse. Settling a victory or an escape replaces the possessions on the sheet with what the character holds at the end (what it brought and still holds, the treasure it carried out and the coin in its purse), adds its new finds and XP awards to the ledger, and rests it to full health. A defeat or an abandonment leaves the possessions, the ledger and the XP exactly as at the start. Settling happens once, in the same library write that frees the character, so an interruption between the session save and the library write is settled on the next request without duplicating or losing anything.

**Rest between adventures.** A rest between adventures restores every hit point and every Second Wind and Action Surge use, so each adventure starts at full health with all uses.

- **Armour class:** from the equipment (see Weapons and armour): in a starting kit's leather, 11 + Dexterity modifier; +1 with the Defense style.
- **Initiative:** Dexterity modifier.
- **Attack:** with the first weapon held, the ability modifier + proficiency bonus to hit, and the weapon's damage + the ability modifier. A critical hit needs a 20, or 19–20 from level 3.
- **Saving throws:** proficient in Strength and Constitution; the others use the ability modifier alone.
- **Skills:** two of Acrobatics, Animal Handling, Athletics, History, Insight, Intimidation, Perception, Persuasion and Survival. Creation starts with Athletics and Perception. Once two are ticked the rest are disabled and the screen shows "2 of 2 chosen"; unticking one enables them again.
- **Fighting Style:** one of three SRD 5.2 Fighting Style feats, each applying only with the gear it needs (#144):
  - _Defense:_ +1 AC while wearing body armour.
  - _Great Weapon Fighting:_ with a two-handed weapon, or a versatile one held in two hands (nothing else in hand, no shield), each 1 or 2 on a damage die counts as 3, critical-hit dice included. A versatile weapon held beside a shield, and every one-handed or light weapon, gets nothing. No starting kit holds such a weapon; it comes from found gear, such as the longsword or greatsword.
  - _Two-Weapon Fighting:_ the light-weapon extra attack adds the ability modifier to its damage, as the first attack does. It changes nothing else.
  - _Archery_ is not offered: it gives +2 to hit with ranged weapons, and the game has none yet. It will be added with them (#225).

  The creation screen tags each style "Applies with this kit" or "No effect with this kit" for the kit chosen, with a note saying why; the sheet says, near the top, whether the character's style applies with what it has equipped now, and the Fighting Style feature repeats the note.

- **Second Wind:** a bonus action to regain 1d10 + Fighter level HP, never above maximum HP; 2 uses. Spent uses stay spent for the rest of the adventure; a rest between adventures restores them and every hit point.
- **Weapon Mastery:** three kinds of weapon, chosen at creation (see Starting kit). Of the SRD masteries, Graze, Nick, Sap, Topple and Vex work without positions; Cleave, Push and Slow are omitted, and no catalogue weapon has Cleave, Push or Topple yet.
- **Action Surge (level 2):** one additional action on your turn, except Magic; 1 use. Spent uses stay spent for the rest of the adventure; a rest between adventures restores them and every hit point.
- **Tactical Mind (level 2):** when you fail an ability check, you can expend a use of Second Wind to add 1d10 to it instead of healing; the use is kept if the check still fails.
- **Champion (level 3):** Improved Critical (critical hits on 19–20) and Remarkable Athlete (advantage on initiative and Strength (Athletics) checks; its movement after a critical hit is omitted).

## Weapons and armour

`src/equipment-5e.ts` holds the SRD 5.2 catalogue and the one rules module that derives AC and attacks from what a character has equipped (#207). Prices are stored in copper (1 sp = 10 cp, 1 gp = 100 cp) and shown in mixed coins. Cost and rarity follow effectiveness: within each table, no item is both cheaper and strictly better than another, and a test checks it. Each item's tier limits where it can appear: starting kits hold common items only. Damage type is recorded for monster resistances (13.2).

| Weapon     | Price | Weight | Damage          | Properties        | Mastery        | Tier     |
| ---------- | ----- | ------ | --------------- | ----------------- | -------------- | -------- |
| Club       | 1 sp  | 2 lb   | 1d4 bludgeoning | light             | Slow (omitted) | common   |
| Dagger     | 2 gp  | 1 lb   | 1d4 piercing    | finesse, light    | Nick           | common   |
| Mace       | 5 gp  | 4 lb   | 1d6 bludgeoning | —                 | Sap            | common   |
| Shortsword | 10 gp | 2 lb   | 1d6 piercing    | finesse, light    | Vex            | common   |
| Longsword  | 15 gp | 3 lb   | 1d8 slashing    | versatile (1d10)  | Sap            | uncommon |
| Greatsword | 50 gp | 6 lb   | 2d6 slashing    | heavy, two-handed | Graze          | uncommon |

| Armour         | Price    | Weight | AC               | Notes                               | Tier     |
| -------------- | -------- | ------ | ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------- |
| Leather armour | 10 gp    | 10 lb  | 11 + Dex         | light                               | common   |
| Chain shirt    | 50 gp    | 20 lb  | 13 + Dex (max 2) | medium                              | common   |

- **Armour class:** body armour's AC plus the Dexterity modifier up to its cap (heavy armour adds none, not even a penalty), or 10 + Dexterity with none; +2 with a shield; +1 with Defense while wearing body armour (a shield alone is not armour).
- **Attacks:** the first weapon listed is the one attacked with. A finesse weapon uses the higher of the Strength and Dexterity modifiers, for both the attack and its damage; others use Strength. A versatile weapon uses its larger die when nothing else is held, and its one-handed die with a shield or a second weapon.
- **Hands:** a two-handed weapon refuses a shield or a second weapon; a second weapon is held only for the light-weapon extra attack, so both must be light, and refuses a shield.
- **Strength requirements:** heavy armour below its Strength score costs 10 feet of speed in SRD 5.2; without positions the profile records the shortfall and nothing else changes. A heavy weapon below Strength 13 attacks with disadvantage.

## Carrying capacity (#224)

SRD 5.2 sets a Small or Medium creature's carrying capacity at its Strength score × 15 lb. Its only stated consequence is a Speed of 5 feet while dragging, lifting or pushing more, which does nothing without positions, so the owner chose (6 October 2026) that the engine **refuses** whatever would take a character over capacity.

- **What weighs.** Every catalogue item weighs its SRD 5.2 weight (the tables above), equipped or stowed. Coin weighs 50 coins to the pound (SRD 5.2); the purse is held in copper and carried as the fewest coins worth that much, largest first, as it is shown, so 1 gp 2 sp 3 cp is 6 coins. Module items weigh a default by kind, so modules author no weights: a Potion of Healing ½ lb (SRD 5.2), a key nothing (SRD 5.2 weighs none; one comes with its lock), and treasure 1 lb each (SRD 5.2 weighs none), including the treasure a character has kept. Coin and gear found become purse and gear as they are taken.
- **Refused.** Taking gear, coin, treasure or any item, taking back dropped gear, and buying (#210) are each refused when the weight carried afterwards would exceed capacity, with the engine's reply naming the item's weight, the weight carried and the capacity ("The Chain Mail weighs 55 lb, and you carry 23 lb of the 75 lb your Strength allows: drop something first."). The button reads "Too heavy", and the AI DM's `take` tool lists only what fits; asked anyway, it reads the same reply. Dropping, equipping and selling are never refused for weight: a sale's coin always weighs less than the gear sold.
- **Shown.** The sheet and the creation preview show "Carrying: 14 of 135 lb"; during an adventure "You carry" ends with the weight carried and the capacity, which change at once with each take, drop, purchase or sale. `get_character_status` gives the AI DM the same.
- **Sheets.** A sheet carrying more than its capacity is invalid. No count limits stowed gear any more: #209's 20-item stopgap is gone. Every starting kit weighs at most 13 lb, under the 45 lb of the lowest possible Strength, 3.

## Changing gear (#209)

A character carries its equipment and its **stowed** gear: catalogue weapons, armour and shields it carries but has not equipped. The sheet keeps both. Its AC and attacks always come from what it has equipped, so every change below updates them at once: the result card ends with the new AC and attacks, and the browser's status strip shows them in and out of fights.

- **Finding gear.** A module places gear as an item of kind `gear` naming a catalogue item. Like treasure and coin it is hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, so it is found only by examining. **Take** stows it. Each piece is found once per character: the ledger records it, kept or not. Gear is equipment, not loot, so carrying it out does not make an escape _with loot_.
- **Equip** (outside a fight): body armour, doffing any armour worn, which is stowed; a shield; or a second light weapon beside a light weapon held, for the light-weapon extra attack. Any other weapon is swapped in with **Wield**. Refused: what isn't carried, a shield with a two-handed weapon, a shield or second weapon without a free hand ("Hands full"), and a second weapon when either is not light.
- **Donning time (SRD 5.2).** Light armour takes 1 minute to don and 1 to doff, medium 5 and 1, heavy 10 and 5; a shield takes the Utilize action. The result says how long the change took. There are no deadline clocks in the 5e game yet; when they return they will count this time.
- **Unequip** (outside a fight): armour, a shield or a second weapon, which is stowed. The last weapon held stays in hand; **Wield** another instead. Unarmed strikes are not modelled, so a character always holds a weapon.
- **Wield**: stows the weapons held and wields a stowed weapon. A two-handed weapon is refused while a shield is worn.
- **Drop** (outside a fight): stowed gear only; equipped gear is unequipped first. Dropped gear lies in the room as an item, and **Take** picks it back up.
- **In a fight.** Drawing a second light weapon (Equip) or swapping the weapon held (Wield) uses the turn's one object interaction and no action; swapping from two weapons to one is one interaction. The bar and the AI DM offer only these two in a fight, so the fight's options stay short. A second weapon change in the same turn is refused ("Interaction used"). SRD 5.2 also lets a weapon be equipped or unequipped as part of each attack, and a second object interaction take the Utilize action; both are abstracted into this one interaction per turn. Armour and shields can't be changed and nothing can be dropped until the fight is over.
- **Strength.** Equipping heavy armour below its Strength requirement is allowed; the result and the status strip say the speed drops by 10 feet, which has no effect without positions.
- **Carrying.** What a character carries is limited by weight, not by count; see [Carrying capacity](#carrying-capacity-224).
- **"You carry".** Outside a fight the character's gear is acted on from the room panel's "You carry" list, like carried items (#198), not from the action bar. The list starts with what it wears ("Worn."), the weapons in hand ("In hand.") and its stowed gear ("Carried, not equipped."), each kind once with a count: Unequip on armour, a shield and a second weapon; Wield on a stowed weapon; Equip on stowed armour, a shield or a light weapon; Drop on stowed gear. In a fight the action bar offers only Wield and Equip on stowed weapons, beside the turn's other options, as it does Drink. The AI DM's `equip`, `unequip`, `swap_weapon` and `drop` tools are offered from the same projection, and the engine writes each refusal.
- **Keeping it.** A victory or an escape replaces the character's possessions, stowed gear included, with what it holds at the end, so gear dropped or left behind is gone and gear found and carried out is kept. A defeat or abandonment leaves the character exactly as it started.

## Trading with merchants (#210)

A module may make a creature a **merchant**, with a stock of catalogue gear and the minutes each trade takes. Merchants trade only inside adventures: the owner dropped the market between adventures (6 October 2026). Outside a fight, in the merchant's room:

- **Prices.** A merchant sells each item it stocks, any number of times, at its catalogue price (the tables above). It buys any catalogue gear the character carries for **half its price, rounded down to the copper** (a 1 sp club sells for 5 cp). Prices and stock are the engine's: the AI DM's `trade` tool names only an offer the engine lists (`buy:` an item stocked, or `sell:` stowed gear), with no price. Treasure has no price and is not bought or sold.
- **Stock tiers.** A merchant stocks common gear always and uncommon gear only in a module whose lowest recommended level is 3 or more; no merchant sells rare gear in increment 12. The module validator enforces this.
- **Buying.** The purse pays and the item is stowed. Too little coin is refused with the engine's reply naming the price and the purse ("Too little coin" on the button), and so is a purchase that would take the character over its carrying capacity once the coin is paid ("Too heavy", #224).
- **Selling.** Stowed gear sells at once. Equipped gear sells only once the player confirms it in the panel: the engine refuses an unconfirmed sale of gear that is only equipped, and the AI DM has no offer for it. Body armour sold is doffed first (its doffing minutes are reported); the last weapon held is never sold. The result ends with the new AC and attacks.
- **Time.** Each purchase or sale takes the merchant's authored minutes (1–60), which the result reports. There are no deadline clocks in the 5e game yet; when they return they will count this time, as they will donning time.
- **Coin found here.** Coin found earlier in the same adventure is in the purse at once and can be spent there. Spending it does not make it findable again: the ledger records the find, not the coin.
- **Keeping it.** Trades follow the adventure rollback contract: a victory or an escape keeps what the character holds at the end (gear bought, the change), and a defeat or abandonment leaves its coin and gear exactly as they were at the start.
- **The balance gate** plays no trades, but its one-hit-kill measure also tries each weapon a merchant sells, as it does each weapon a module places.

## Combat

Combat (#128–#130) uses the numbers above. An encounter has two sides, the party and its opponents, and each side may hold several combatants; for now the party is one Fighter, and a module may set several opponents against it. Every die comes from the adventure session's own seeded stream and is recorded with the action that drew it.

- **Initiative.** When a fight begins, each combatant rolls d20 + its initiative bonus (Dexterity modifier). Higher totals act first. A tie goes to the higher Dexterity score; combatants still tied each roll a d20 roll-off, repeated among any still tied, and the higher roll acts first. The browser's initiative table shows the order, each total, hit points and armour class, and highlights and tags the combatant whose turn it is; each combatant's roll and roll-off are under **How initiative was rolled**. Once the fight is over the table folds away behind an **Initiative order** button.
- **Turns.** Combatants act in initiative order, round after round, skipping the defeated. A defeated opponent drops out: it takes no more turns and cannot be targeted, and the fight goes on while any opponent stands.
- **Turn economy.** Each turn has one action, one bonus action and one reaction. On the player's turn an attack on one living opponent of their choice takes the action, the light-weapon extra attack takes the bonus action (unless Nick makes it part of the Attack action), Second Wind takes the bonus action, drinking a potion takes the bonus action (as in SRD 5.2), and Action Surge adds one more action. The player uses them by the action bar's Attack, Extra attack, Drink, Second Wind, Action Surge and End turn buttons or by telling the AI DM. The bar always shows the whole toolkit; an action the engine would refuse now is disabled with the engine's reason, shortened to "Action used", "Bonus action used", "Attack first", "Extra attack used", "Full HP" or "No uses left". Action Surge is in the bar from level 2. The turn lasts until the player ends it or nothing is left that the engine would accept, so a player at full health without Action Surge ends the turn by attacking, as before. The browser's status strip shows what is left this turn and each feature's uses. Nothing uses a reaction yet: opportunity attacks need positions, and no Fighter feature at levels 1–3 takes one. Opponents only attack.
- **Second Wind and Action Surge in combat.** Second Wind rolls 1d10 + Fighter level and restores that much, up to maximum HP. It is refused at full health, when the bonus action is spent and when no use is left. Action Surge is refused below level 2 and when its use is spent. A refusal changes nothing and spends nothing.
- **Light-weapon extra attack (Light, SRD 5.2).** A character holding two light weapons attacks with the first, and after that attack may make one extra attack that turn with the second against any living opponent. Its damage adds no ability modifier unless the modifier is negative, or the character has Two-Weapon Fighting. It takes the bonus action, so it competes with Second Wind and a potion, unless the second weapon has Nick and is mastered, which makes it part of the Attack action. It is offered once per turn, Action Surge included. The action bar shows Extra attack for each living opponent only to a character holding two light weapons, disabled with "Attack first" before an attack this turn, "Extra attack used" once made, and "Bonus action used" without Nick once the bonus action is spent. The AI DM's `light_attack` tool is offered exactly while it is enabled; called otherwise, the engine refuses it.
- **Weapon masteries.** Each applies only to a mastered weapon being wielded.
  - _Sap:_ when the attack hits a creature that survives, the creature is sapped: it has disadvantage on its next attack roll, which spends the effect. The effect also ends at the start of the player's next turn. The browser marks a sapped combatant in the initiative table.
  - _Vex:_ when the attack hits and deals damage to a creature that survives, the attacker has advantage on its next attack roll against that creature, with any weapon, which spends it. It lapses at the end of the attacker's next turn.
  - _Graze:_ when the attack misses, natural 1s included, the target still takes damage equal to the attack's damage modifier, if above 0, of the weapon's type.
  - _Nick:_ see the light-weapon extra attack. Nick acts only on the extra attack: the SRD lets the extra attack be part of the Attack action, and here that holds when the second weapon has Nick.
- **Heavy weapons.** A heavy weapon wielded with Strength below 13 attacks with disadvantage (SRD 5.2).
- **Advantage and disadvantage.** With advantage the engine rolls two d20s and keeps the higher; with disadvantage, the lower. With sources of both it rolls one d20. The kept die decides natural 1s, natural 20s and critical hits. The result card names each source and shows both dice, for example "at disadvantage (Sap): 18 and 6, keeping 6". Advantage and disadvantage come only from the engine's rules: for now, Sap and a heavy weapon below Strength 13 give disadvantage, and Vex gives advantage. Neither the player nor the AI DM can grant them.
- **Feature uses and rests.** There are no in-adventure rests yet. Every adventure starts with all Second Wind and Action Surge uses, because a rest between adventures restores them and every hit point; spent uses are not regained during an adventure, and they, like hit points and carried items, last from one fight to the next. The sheet, the creation preview and the fight all say so in the same words. When in-adventure rests arrive, Second Wind will regain one use on a short rest and all on a long rest, and Action Surge its use on either.
- **Opponent targeting.** Each opponent attacks a living party combatant on its own turn. With one party combatant there is no choice and no die. With more (companions, later), the opponent rolls a die with one side per living party combatant, from the session's seeded stream, and attacks the one at that position counting in initiative order; the die is recorded with the attack.
- **Attacks.** d20 + attack bonus hits when it meets or beats the target's AC. A natural 20 (19–20 for a Champion) is a critical hit, which always hits and rolls the damage dice twice; a natural 1 always misses. Damage is the dice plus the damage modifier, never below 0. With Great Weapon Fighting and a weapon in two hands, each 1 or 2 on a damage die counts as 3: the result card's text and its damage roll show each die as rolled and, for a 1 or 2, that it counts as 3 (such as "d10 2→3").
- **Defeat.** A combatant at 0 HP is defeated. An opponent at 0 HP drops out of the fight. When every opponent is defeated, the encounter's victory ending follows if it names one; otherwise the fight is over and the character explores on. The player character at 0 HP is defeated at once (no death saving throws): the adventure ends in the module's defeat ending, the character's sheet is saved at 0 HP and marked defeated, and it cannot start another adventure. After a victory the character keeps what it holds, is credited the XP it earned and rests to full health (see Earning XP and treasure).
- **Refused actions.** Acting out of turn, attacking an absent, friendly or defeated target, attacking with the action spent, using a feature it can't use now, or acting after the fight is over is refused with an engine-written reason. A refusal changes nothing and draws no dice.
- **Not used yet.** Tactical Mind needs a choice after a failed check and waits for a later ticket; Remarkable Athlete's advantage on Athletics checks is used. Remarkable Athlete's advantage on initiative is not rolled yet; a character reaches level 3 through The Robbers' Barrow and then The Goblin Warren. An opponent uses its stat block's first melee attack; stat-block riders that need advantage (the Goblin Warrior's extra 1d4), bonus actions such as Nimble Escape, and ranged attacks are not used.

**The AI Dungeon Master.** The engine is the only authority over dice, turn order, targets, hits, damage, hit points and endings. The AI DM may only call `look` and `get_character_status` to read the scene, and the action tools `move`, `examine`, `take`, `use_item`, `force_door`, `pick_lock`, `break_door`, `unlock`, `search`, `disarm`, `talk`, `equip`, `unequip`, `swap_weapon`, `drop`, `attack`, `light_attack`, `second_wind`, `action_surge` and `end_turn`. A check tool is called only when the player explicitly asks for that approach ("force the door"); words that name none ("open the door") get a question listing the offered approaches, and no roll. `talk` lists only the creatures' authored topics, and a check already tried is not offered again; calling it anyway gets the engine's refusal, never a reroll. Each action tool is offered only while the engine would accept it, and lists only what is visible and legal; the tools come from the same projection as the browser's action bar, so the AI DM is offered exactly the actions the bar shows enabled: `move` the room's exits, `examine` its features, visible items and carried items, `take` its visible items, `use_item` carried potions while drinking one would be accepted, `attack` the living opponents on the player's turn, and `light_attack` them while the extra attack is enabled. Looking, searching, reading, inspecting or opening something ("search the chest") is `examine`. "Catch my breath" or "second wind" calls `second_wind` only when it is offered; otherwise the AI says it is not available. One message allows one action. Opponents are listed by id and name; when the player's words fit exactly one, by its name or by an ordinal matching the number in its name ("attack the second goblin" is Goblin Minion 2 when Goblin Minions 1 and 2 are listed), the AI calls `attack` with it; it never counts positions in a list. When they name none or fit several ("attack the goblin" with two goblins) it asks which one and calls nothing. It never guesses a target. No tool takes a roll, damage, advantage, extra-action, discovery, item or outcome argument; any extra argument is refused before the engine is reached. The engine writes the reply to every action it resolves or refuses, so the AI cannot narrate a different roll, discovery, item or result, and a reply without a tool call changes nothing. Taking an item that is not visible (one that does not exist, or one still hidden) is refused by the engine.

## Exploring

An adventure module is a set of rooms joined by two-way passages; there is no map and no movement speed. The character starts in the module's start room.

- **Moving.** The character goes through an exit to the room beyond. Entering a room whose fight has not been won begins it at once: initiative is rolled, and opponents who win it act first. The character cannot leave in the middle of a fight; fleeing waits for a later ticket. A room whose fight was won stays clear.
- **Examining.** Examining a feature (looking at, searching, reading, inspecting or opening it) shows its description and makes its authored discovery, if it has one. A discovery is made once and stays on the room panel; examining again repeats it and finds nothing new. An item hidden in a feature becomes visible when that feature is first examined. Visible and carried items can be examined too. Examining draws no dice.
- **Ability checks.** A check is d20 + the ability's modifier, + the proficiency bonus when the character is proficient in the check's skill, against the module's DC; it succeeds when the total meets the DC. A natural 20 or 1 is just its number. The Champion's Remarkable Athlete (level 3) gives advantage on Athletics checks. Each check is rolled once and its outcome remembered: trying again is refused, never rerolled, and the action stays in the bar, disabled, with the reason ("Already tried").
- **Saving throws.** A saving throw is d20 + the ability's modifier, + the proficiency bonus in the Fighter's Strength and Constitution saves, against the DC. Only traps call for one so far.
- **Doors.** A passage may have a door. A stuck door opens with **Force**, a Strength (Athletics) check. A locked door opens with **Pick** or **Break**, each the check the module names, or with **Unlock**, offered while the character carries the door's key, with no roll. Each approach is tried once; an opened door stays open from both sides. Going through a shut door is refused ("Door shut").
- **Traps.** A passage may have a hidden trap. In a module with traps, every room offers **Search**, one Wisdom (Perception) check per room. Every search shows the same DC, the lowest find DC of any trap in the module, so the DC never gives a trap away; the search finds each hidden trap on the room's exits whose own find DC the total meets. A found trap shows on its exit and offers **Disarm**, a check tried once; a failed disarm leaves it armed. Going through an armed trap, found or not, springs it once: a saving throw, then its damage, halved (rounding down) on a success. A trap that drops the character to 0 HP ends the adventure in the trap's defeat ending.
- **Talking.** A room may hold creatures to talk to, each with authored topics. **Talk** asks about one topic: the creature answers with its authored words, or, for a topic with a check (such as Charisma (Persuasion)), its success or failure words. Each topic is asked once, and the room panel keeps what the creature said.
- **Taking.** A visible item is taken and carried. Taking is refused for anything not visible.
- **Potion of Healing.** Drinking one restores 2d4 + 2 HP, never above maximum HP, and uses it up. Outside a fight it is drunk at once; in a fight it takes the bonus action, so it competes with Second Wind. Administering it to another creature is not used, as the party is one character.
- **Not during a fight.** Moving, examining, taking, opening doors, searching, disarming and talking are refused until the fight is won. In a fight a character can only drink a potion or take the combat actions.

The browser's status strip shows the character's HP, an HP bar and its health: healthy, bloodied at half HP or fewer (the SRD 5.2 Bloodied), critical at a quarter or fewer, and defeated at 0. The engine projects the health; the browser applies no rule of its own. In a fight the strip adds the round, whose turn it is, and a pip for each Action (two after Action Surge), the Bonus action and the Reaction; in and out of fights it shows a pip for each Second Wind and Action Surge use, and a line with the character's AC (naming the armour and shield worn) and attacks from its gear as it stands. The room panel shows the room, the exits, the features with any discovery made, the visible items and the carried items (stowed gear among them), leaving out each empty list (a room with no exits, features or items says so in one line); in a fight the panel's details fold away behind a **Room details** button. The room panel also shows each exit's door (shut or open) and a found or sprung trap, and each creature with what it has said. The action bar shows the name of each exit, feature, item, carried potion, door, room, found trap and topic with a **Go**, **Examine**, **Take**, **Drink**, **Force**, **Pick**, **Break**, **Unlock**, **Search**, **Disarm** or **Talk** button, whose accessible name is the full action, such as "Examine Rusted Lantern" or "Force Swollen Door". The history shows each check and saving throw as its own roll group: the die, the ability modifier, the proficiency bonus and the DC, with a Success or Failure tag. The server projects whether the engine would accept each one now; one it would refuse stays in place, disabled, with the reason as visible text linked to the button (a potion at full HP says "Full HP"). After an action, focus stays on the clicked button while it is still enabled, and otherwise moves to the newest conversation-history entry, never to an unrelated action.

## Adventure modules

A 5e adventure module is a JSON file in format version 8 (`src/adventure-5e.ts`). It declares its recommended levels (1–3) and a difficulty (easy, medium or hard); its rooms, each with features (each with an optional discovery), items (the SRD 5.2 Potion of Healing or a key, optionally hidden in one of the room's features or carried by an opponent of its fight; or treasure, coin or gear, which must be, with coin's gold, silver and copper pieces and gear's catalogue item), creatures to talk to (each with topics, a topic optionally with a check and failure words, and optionally a merchant's stock of catalogue gear and minutes per trade) and an optional encounter; the two-way passages between rooms, each with an optional door (stuck, with a force check; or locked, with any of a key, a pick check and a break check) and an optional trap (its find and disarm checks, trigger text, saving throw, damage and defeat ending); a check is a Fighter skill or an ability with a DC from 5 to 30; each encounter with each opponent's SRD 5.2 stat block inline (size, type, AC, average hit points and formula, ability scores, challenge rating, XP and melee attacks) and an optional `boss: true`, which exempts it from the balance gate's one-hit-kill cap, its defeat ending and an optional victory ending; and its endings. The validator rejects:

- unknown room, encounter, feature and ending references, and an unknown item kind;
- a room that cannot be reached from the start room, and an ending that no encounter or trap names;
- a check or trap that guards the only route to an essential room (one whose fight ends the adventure in victory): such a room must be reachable without a check or a trap, where a locked door counts as open when its key can itself be reached that way;
- a stuck door with anything but a force check, a locked door with a force check or with neither key, pick nor break, a key that is not an item of kind key, an unknown skill or ability, and a topic with a check but no failure words (or the reverse);
- an encounter whose victory or defeat ending names the wrong kind, an encounter in no room or in several, and a module without a victory ending;
- an item hidden in a feature without a discovery to reveal it;
- treasure, coin or gear that is not hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, coin without an amount of at least one piece, an amount on anything but coin, gear that names no catalogue item, and a catalogue item on anything but gear;
- a merchant stock entry that is not a catalogue item, an item stocked twice, rare gear, uncommon gear in a module for levels below 3, trade minutes outside 1–60, and two merchants in one room;
- a passage from a room to itself, or two passages between the same rooms;
- an id that is a catalogue item's (such as `longsword`), since those name the character's own gear;
- two features, items, doors, traps, creatures or topics with the same id, and two features in one room, two opponents in one encounter, two doors or two topics of one creature with the same name (ignoring case), since the player names them.

A module in another format version is refused with a message naming the file.

The built-in fixture `adventures/5e/cellar-goblin.json`, _The Goblin in the Cellar_, is one room with one SRD 5.2 Goblin Warrior (AC 15, 10 HP, Scimitar +4 for 1d6 + 2 slashing), for level 1 and declared Hard. `adventures/5e/goblin-storeroom.json`, _The Goblins in the Storeroom_, is one room with a group fight: an SRD 5.2 Goblin Minion (AC 12, 7 HP, Dagger +4 for 1d4 + 2 piercing) and a Goblin Warrior, for level 2 and declared Medium. `adventures/5e/smugglers-cellar.json`, _The Smugglers' Cellar_, is four rooms for level 1, declared Hard: a quiet stair foot with a lantern to read, an alcove whose chest hides a Potion of Healing, an empty rat-gnawed cellar, and a den with a Goblin Warrior whose defeat ends it. `adventures/5e/warden-crypt.json`, _The Warden's Crypt_, is six rooms for level 1, declared Hard: a stair with a stuck door (Athletics DC 13) to a flooded cell with a hidden potion; a hall with a bound smuggler to question (one topic free, one needing Persuasion DC 12); an archway with a dart trap (find Perception DC 13, disarm Dexterity DC 12, Dexterity save DC 12 against 2d4 piercing) to the offering room, whose bowl hides the iron key; an iron door (key, pick Dexterity DC 15, or break Athletics DC 18) to a strongroom with a hidden potion; and the tomb, reached freely, where the warden rises as an SRD 5.2 Zombie (as in the Abandoned Delve) whose defeat ends it. `adventures/5e/abandoned-delve.json`, _The Abandoned Delve_ (#136), is ten rooms for levels 1–2, declared Hard: the first real module, whose premise, map and encounters the owner approved on #136. Its required path is one SRD 5.2 Zombie (AC 8, 15 HP, Slam +3 for 1d8 + 1 bludgeoning) carrying a purse; off it lie a group fight against two Skeletons (AC 14, 13 HP, Shortsword +5 for 1d6 + 3 piercing), an avoidable Giant Spider (AC 14, 26 HP, Bite +5 for 1d8 + 3 piercing), and, behind a bronze door (key from the shrine altar, pick Dexterity DC 15, or break Athletics DC 17), a Ghoul boss (AC 12, 22 HP, Bite +4 for 1d6 + 2 piercing). Only the first attack in each stat block is used, and riders such as the spider's poison, the Ghoul's necrotic damage and paralysis, and the Zombie's Undead Fortitude are left out. It has a stuck storeroom door (Athletics DC 12), a loose-step trap on the shrine stair (find Perception DC 13, disarm Dexterity DC 12, Dexterity save DC 12 against 2d6 bludgeoning), a goblin to question (one topic free, one needing Persuasion DC 12), four treasures and two hoards of coin that grow with the risk, a longsword and a shield in the storeroom's sealed barrel (#209), and two exits: the gate, and a shaft beside the vault where the player chooses to leave or push on. `adventures/5e/tinkers-toll.json`, _The Tinker's Toll_ (#211), is four rooms for level 1, declared Hard: the increment 12 release module, whose premise, map, merchant stock and gear placement the owner approved on #211. A wayside shrine is the start and the exit; at the cart beyond, Merrow the Tinker answers two free topics and trades as a merchant (dagger, shortsword, shield and chain shirt, 10 minutes a trade). At the ford an SRD 5.2 Wolf (AC 12, 11 HP, Bite +4 for 1d6 + 2 piercing; its prone rider and Pack Tactics are left out) attacks, and the reeds hide a shield (found gear) and 3 gp 5 sp. In the toll tower two SRD 5.2 Bandits (AC 12, 11 HP, Scimitar +3 for 1d6 + 1 slashing; the light crossbow is left out with ranged combat) fight together; one carries a 12 gp purse and the strongbox holds a silver seal (treasure). The shrine's offering bowl is empty: with coin in the start room, the gate's cautious run would leave at once with loot and never fight, so the owner moved that coin to the reeds and declared the module Hard rather than Medium. _The Robbers' Barrow_ (level 1) and _The Goblin Warren_ (levels 2–3, whose Goblin Boss is its one boss, with a Potion of Healing among the charms at its gate) are declared Hard. The leather starting kits (#207) made three modules too deadly, and the owner approved the smallest changes that requalify them: the Smugglers' Cellar lost its Giant Rat fight, the Warden's Crypt's Goblin Warrior became the risen warden, a Zombie, with the module declared Hard instead of Medium, and the Goblin Warren gained a potion at its gate.

## Balance harness

`src/balance-5e.ts` plays any 5e module through the real runtime and measures how dangerous it is. `npm.cmd run balance` reports on every built-in module, or on the module files named after it, then gives the balance gate's verdict on each over the same seeds, and exits with code 1 if any module fails with a named reason or does not qualify; `--seeds <count>`, `--percentiles <p,p>`, `--styles <style,style>` and `--json` change the run.

- **Characters.** 10,000 creations are rolled with 4d6-drop-lowest from a fixed seed and placed as a fresh creation starts: `defaultPlacement`, +2 Strength and +1 Constitution, Athletics and Perception, Defense, and mastery of the dagger, mace and shortsword (`FIGHTER_DEFAULT_CHOICES`). The report plays the creation's default kit, the mace; the gate plays every kit. Both play the default masteries, so another choice of masteries is not gated. They are ranked by the sum of their six ability modifiers; the weak and strong characters are the 5th and 95th percentiles. Each plays at every recommended level, at full health with that level's XP.
- **Objective and required path.** A module's objective is its victory if it has one, or else getting out with loot (treasure or coin). The required path is the rooms on the route to it through the fewest fights, then the fewest moves: to the victory fight, or to the nearest loot and out by the nearest exit. A key a key-only door needs is fetched on the way. Every other room is optional, and so is its fight.
- **Styles.** Each picks only actions `projectActions` offers as available, as the action bar does. _direct_ goes into every room and fights every fight, and heals below a quarter of its hit points; _cautious_ skips optional fights but looks into optional rooms with no fight, searches each room for traps and disarms those it finds, and heals below half; _avoid-optional_ enters only the required rooms, and heals below a quarter. All of them examine every feature and fallen body, take every item, attack the opponent with the fewest hit points and follow with the light-weapon extra attack on it, use Action Surge once the action is spent, try a shut door's key, then force, pick and break, and plan around a door that holds. No style talks: no topic changes the outcome.
- **Metrics,** per level, percentile and style: survival and completion rates, each ending's count, for each fight its HP lost, rounds and defeat rate, Second Winds, potions and HP healed, trap damage, XP and treasure earned, and each opponent's chance of dying to one attack from full HP (hit chance and critical hits counted, from the character's own attack, with a heavy weapon's disadvantage and Graze's damage on a miss; Sap and Vex affect only a later attack, and Defense only AC, so none of them changes it).
- **Failures.** A module the harness can't play fails with a named reason instead of passing: `unsupported-action` (the runtime offers an action no style knows), `step-limit` (a run takes more than 2,000 actions), `stranded` (a run is left alive with no action that leads on or out; a module that needs something no style does, such as talking, ends this way) or `unreachable-objective`.
- **Budget.** The default run, seeds 0–199 with every style and both percentiles, qualifies all eight built-in modules in about 10 seconds as part of `npm.cmd test`; the test fails above 30 seconds of CPU time, which other tests running alongside it don't affect. The same seeds always give the same report.

## Balance gate

A module is offered in the browser only if it qualifies at its declared difficulty (`gateAdventure` in `src/balance-5e.ts`), and `npm.cmd run verify` fails if a built-in module doesn't. The gate uses the harness's characters and seeds 0–199, and checks three things:

| Difficulty | Required-path survival | One-hit-kill cap |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Easy       | ≥ 95%                  | 50%              |
| Medium     | ≥ 85%                  | 40%              |
| Hard       | ≥ 75%                  | 30%              |

- **Too deadly.** The weakest character, the 5th percentile, playing _cautious_, must survive at least the difficulty's share of runs with every starting kit at every recommended level. The verdict names the kit and level it survives least with, and lists each.
- **Too easy.** Early levels are a character's most vulnerable, so they must never be easy. An ordinary enemy is any opponent not marked `boss: true` in the module. For the strongest character, the 95th percentile, at the maximum recommended level, with the kit, or the weapon the module places (wielded with the default kit's armour), and the Fighting Style that kill each enemy most often (Great Weapon Fighting counts for a placed two-handed or versatile weapon; a tie keeps Defense, the default), the gate takes each ordinary enemy's chance of dying to one attack from full HP (hit chance, critical hits, Fighting Style and masteries counted, as in the harness). More than half of the ordinary enemies over the difficulty's cap fails the module. The measure is per attack, not per turn: Action Surge's second attack, the light-weapon extra attack, and later Extra Attack, are not counted. A module with no ordinary enemies passes.
- **XP.** All the XP a module offers (every encounter's stat-block XP and the most any ending awards) must not take a character past the maximum recommended level + 1. The character starts one XP short of the level above the maximum, the worst case, and the SRD 5.2 XP table counts levels 4 and 5 for this check even though characters stop at level 3.

A module the harness can't play fails the gate with the harness's named reason. The verdict names each check, whether it passed and, for too easy, the enemies over the cap.

## House rules

- A player character at 0 HP is defeated at once; there are no death saving throws.
- Second Wind is refused at full health, so a use is never wasted. The 2024 rules allow it.
- A Potion of Healing is refused at full health for the same reason.
- Ability scores are rolled once with 4d6-drop-lowest and placed freely; there are no rerolls.
- Starting gear is common tier only: three kits of leather armour and simple weapons, and no starting coin.
- A character holds a second weapon only for the light-weapon extra attack, so both weapons must be light. Two weapons and a shield, or a two-handed weapon with a shield or a second weapon, are refused.
- A character always holds a weapon: its last one can be swapped but not unequipped, as unarmed strikes are not modelled.
- In a fight, one weapon change per turn, with the object interaction; armour and shields change only outside a fight.
- A character carries up to its Strength × 15 lb; taking or buying more is refused rather than slowing it, as there are no positions.
- Armour's stealth disadvantage is recorded for later Stealth checks (15.1); nothing rolls Stealth yet.
- Morale and reaction rolls are planned for later increments.

## Rules that need positions

| Reach                                                  | Omitted                                  |
| Opportunity attacks                                    | Omitted                                  |
| Areas of effect                                        | Omitted                                  |

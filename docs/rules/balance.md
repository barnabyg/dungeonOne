# Balance tools

## Balance harness

`src/balance-5e.ts` plays any 5e module through the real runtime and measures how dangerous it is. `npm.cmd run balance` reports on every built-in module for each class, or on the module files named after it, then gives the balance gate's verdict on each for every class over the same seeds, and exits with code 1 if any module fails with a named reason or does not qualify; `--seeds <count>`, `--percentiles <p,p>`, `--styles <style,style>`, `--checks <policy>`, `--classes <class,class>` (the report's classes, any of `fighter`, `rogue`, `cleric` and `wizard`; every one by default) and `--json` change the run. The gate always plays every check policy and every class, whatever `--checks` and `--classes` say.

- **Characters.** Each class is played (#310): the Fighter and the Rogue, from #339 a level-1 Cleric from its defaults (the Protector, its default spells and either kit), and from #340 a level-1 Wizard from its defaults (its default spellbook and spells, and either kit). 10,000 creations are rolled with 4d6-drop-lowest from a fixed seed and placed as a fresh creation of the class starts, by its own priority. A Fighter gets `defaultPlacement`, +2 Strength and +1 Constitution, Athletics and Perception, Defense, and mastery of the dagger, mace and shortsword (`FIGHTER_DEFAULT_CHOICES`); a Rogue gets Dexterity first, +2 Dexterity and +1 Constitution, Athletics, Perception, Persuasion and Stealth with Expertise in Perception and Stealth, and mastery of the shortsword and dagger (its creation defaults). The report plays the class's default kit (the Fighter's mace, the Rogue's shortsword and dagger); the gate plays every kit of the class. A Rogue's Dexterity-first build is its usual one. Both play the default masteries, so another choice of masteries is not gated. At level 4 each character makes the gate's level choice (#286, `gateLevelChoice`): the Ability Score Improvement's two points go one at a time to the first ability below 20 in the order attack ability (the class's first primary ability, Strength for the Fighter and Dexterity for the Rogue, #308; or Dexterity for the Dexterity-first build), then the class's ability priority (the Fighter's Strength, Constitution, Dexterity, Wisdom, Charisma, Intelligence), so it is +2 to the attack ability unless that passes 20 (Strength 19 gives +1 Strength and +1 Constitution, Strength 20 gives +2 Constitution); the Fighter's fourth mastery is the weapon the gate is trying when a module places or sells one it can master, and otherwise the first not yet mastered in catalogue order, the longsword; the Rogue's level 4 brings no mastery; a Cleric's or Wizard's +2 goes on its spellcasting ability, Wisdom or Intelligence, and each new level's cantrips, spells and spellbook spells are chosen in its list's order (`withOwedChoices`). Each class ranks the sample by the sum of the six ability modifiers its own creation gives (its background increase may land on an odd or an even score, so the classes' 5th percentile can be different rolls); the weak and strong characters are the 5th and 95th percentiles. Each plays at every recommended level, at full health with that level's XP.
- **Objective and required path.** A module's objective is its victory if it has one, or else getting out with loot (treasure or coin). The required path is the rooms on the route to it through the fewest fights, then the fewest moves: to the victory fight, or to the nearest loot and out by the nearest exit. A key a key-only door needs is fetched on the way. No route goes through a hidden passage that only a topic's check opens (#297): no style talks, so the gate judges the route a player who never talks must take. The nearest loot is the nearest such a player can reach, and a module whose objective only talking reaches fails as `unreachable-objective`. Every other room is optional, and so is its fight. On a declared route (#407) the required path first passes the route's rooms in order, each reached by the fewest fights from the last, then goes on to the objective; the harness plays it with the route's next room as its goal until it has passed them all, and then the objective, so a fight on the route is a fight on its path.
- **Styles.** Each picks only actions `projectActions` offers as available, as the action bar does. _direct_ goes into every room and fights every fight, and heals below a quarter of its hit points; _cautious_ skips optional fights but looks into optional rooms with no fight, searches each room for traps and disarms those it finds, and heals below half; _avoid-optional_ enters only the required rooms, and heals below a quarter, but goes round by any room where a flag has closed the way through them (#401); _stealth-first_ (#302) plays as _cautious_, but sneaks into every fight it would enter and, unseen, slips past it unless its goal (the victory fight, loot or a key) is in that room or the way on is shut, when it springs an ambush; a fight it slipped past is met again if its route comes back; _retreat_ (#396) plays as _cautious_, but in a fight, below its heal threshold with no heal left to take, it flees toward the nearest exit room, through a way out whose room has no fight and from which some way to an exit room passes no fight, after Disengage when it has it; once it has fled it heads out that way and leaves. It flees each fight once (met again, it fights on), and with no such way out it fights on. Only _retreat_ flees. _use-room_ (#402) plays as _cautious_, but in a fight, with no heal to take, uses the room's fight action whenever one is offered, against the opponent with the most hit points left, by its first approach; in a module without fight actions it plays exactly as _cautious_. Only _use-room_ uses fight actions. All of them examine every feature and fallen body, take every item, attack the opponent with the fewest hit points and follow with the light-weapon extra attack on it, use Action Surge once the action is spent, try a shut door's key, then force, pick and break, and plan around a door that holds. A style takes another try (#284) a module offers, but never one whose damage could leave it below its healing threshold, so failing checks can't wear a run down. No style talks: no topic changes the outcome. Each answers a reaction roll (#304) by the run's reaction policy: _attack_ by default, or _peaceful_ (see Reaction rolls).
- **Casting policies** (#348). A caster plays every style by a casting policy: _conserve_, the default, or _spend-early_ (`CASTING_POLICIES`). Both heal below the style's heal threshold as before (a healing spell on itself, the lowest slot first, before a potion), cast Mage Armor once before the first fight and Shield when it turns a hit into a miss, and keep a Spiritual Weapon's bonus-action attack and a Vampiric Touch's repeat going on the weakest foe once cast. Otherwise a turn's action is a damaging cantrip (Fire Bolt, Ray of Frost, Shocking Grasp, Chill Touch or Sacred Flame, whose save the policies cast since #348) on the weakest foe, ahead of a weapon. _conserve_ casts a levelled spell only below the heal threshold with no heal left to take: "cantrips until below the heal threshold". _spend-early_ casts one on every turn it can: with two or more foes left untouched, a control spell (Sleep, Hold Person) on the one with the most hit points, never one immune to it; otherwise the damage spell worth most (`castValue`: its mean damage on each foe it catches, no more than each one's hit points, Spirit Guardians, Spiritual Weapon and Vampiric Touch counting a second round), the higher slot on a tie. While concentrating, neither casts another concentration spell. The policies cast no other buff, curse, Slow or restoration spell. A foe asleep or turned is left alone while others stand (damage would end it); a held one is still attacked. Every run records the spells cast.
- **Check policies** (#285). _seeded_, the default, rolls every check as a player meets it. _always-fail_ and _always-succeed_ make every check (a feature's, a door's force, pick or break, a trap's disarm, a topic's and a room's search for traps, each approach of one) total the lowest or the highest any character of the module's maximum recommended level could make: a natural 1 at the lowest ability modifier (−4), or a natural 20 at the highest (+5) plus the proficiency bonus for a skill (`extremeTotals`). Each check so lands in its worst or best band the validator counts reachable, and an unauthored band by 5 falls back to plain failure or success as usual; a search finds no trap, or every trap it could. The d20 is still drawn, so the rest of the dice (attacks, damage, saving throws) are the seeded ones. Trap saving throws are saves, not checks, and stay seeded. A run that leaves loot a failed check left hidden heads for the exits without it. Loot an opponent carried is lost the same way once the opponent leaves no body to search: it fled, it surrendered (no style talks, so it never hands the loot over), or its encounter ended peacefully (#324); the run heads for the next loot or out. A key lost either way counts as absent (#331): the run plans around its door, and a run left with no way on or out is stranded, naming the lost key and why it was lost, which fails the gate (owner decision): the module must give another way, such as another route or a door that can be picked, forced or broken.
- **Metrics,** per level, percentile and style: survival and completion rates, fights slipped past (shown for _stealth-first_), each ending's count, for each fight its HP lost, rounds and defeat rate, Second Winds, potions, short and long rests, rests interrupted (#335) and HP healed, trap damage, XP and treasure earned, and each opponent's chance of dying to one attack from full HP (hit chance and critical hits counted, from the character's own attack, with a heavy weapon's disadvantage and Graze's damage on a miss, the enemy's resistance, vulnerability or immunity to the attack's damage type, and Undead Fortitude's save, which a critical or radiant hit skips; Sap and Vex affect only a later attack, and Defense only AC, so none of them changes it).
- **Failures.** A module the harness can't play fails with a named reason instead of passing: `unsupported-action` (the runtime offers an action no style knows), `step-limit` (a run takes more than 2,000 actions), `stranded` (a run is left alive with no action that leads on or out; a module that needs something no style does, such as talking, ends this way; the reason names the run's check policy and every check it made, with its band and any passage that band closed, so a check that fails into a dead end is named, and every lost key to a door only a key opens with why it was lost, #331), `unreachable-objective` (no route reaches the objective; when only talking would, the reason says so and names the route talking opens, #297) or `unsimulated-trait` (#235: an opponent lists a trait the encounter engine does not apply, named with the opponent; every trait a stat block may list today is applied, so only an unvalidated module meets it). Multiattack, Rampage and the other traits are played by the real engine, so the harness simulates them as a player meets them.
- **Running the gate.** `npm.cmd test` gates all four built-in modules for every class once; it pins each module's figures, so a speed-up that changes how the gate plays fails (#410), and its other checks of the shipped modules read those results. It gates on up to 8 worker threads, one job per module and class, the modules with the most fights first (`tests/fixtures/gate-workers.mjs`, #469): about 70 s on a 20-thread machine, against about 290 s in one thread. A job still running after 10 minutes is stopped and fails, naming its module and class. That timeout guards against a frozen gate and is not a performance budget: no test asserts how long the gate takes, and the log shows each run's time. `scripts/run-tests.mjs` runs this file after the other test files, since its workers beside them would starve the browser tests past their timeouts. Until #469 the test failed above a CPU-time budget (450 s of reference CPU from #447, 600 before), which kept the gate to one thread on a quiet machine. The same seeds always give the same report. The browser server reads the verdicts recorded in `adventures/5e/gate-verdicts.json` (`src/gate-verdicts-5e.ts`), each keyed by a SHA-256 hash of the loaded module, and gates only a module changed since they were recorded, so it starts at once; `shipped-modules.test.mjs` checks the file against the gate's results, and when a change moves a verdict, `npm.cmd run gate:verdicts` records it again. Most of the gate's time is the runtime projecting the action bar, dry-running each candidate action. These keep it down without changing any report: the harness projects only the kinds of action a style may play, so gear changes, trade and talk are never tried, and reuses a character's profile while its sheet is unchanged (#321); it projects only the spells a style may cast, healing only below its heal threshold, and reuses a cautious run whose reaction policy decided nothing as the peaceful run (#347); each caster plays both casting policies on the first 50 seeds only and the other 150 by the better (#348); the 10,000 sample creations are ranked by their ability scores alone; and the runtime does no listing work for a kind of action or a part of the room a projection doesn't ask about, exploring projects each kind only when its choice comes, and the route planner is built once per module (#410). Spells and exploring's kinds are listed by hand in `src/balance-5e.ts` (`CAST_SPELLS`, `FIRST_ACTIONS`, `EXAMINING` and the `EXPLORE_` sets): a spell a new policy casts, or a kind a style takes while exploring, must be named there, or the harness never sees it offered.

## Balance gate

A module is offered in the browser only if it qualifies at its declared difficulty for every class creation offers (`gateModule` in `src/balance-5e.ts`; `GATE_CLASSES` is `OFFERED_CLASS_IDS`: the Fighter and the Rogue from #310, the Cleric and the Wizard from #348; `gateAdventure` judges one class), and `npm.cmd run verify` fails if a built-in module doesn't qualify for any one of them. The gate uses the harness's characters of each class and seeds 0–199, plays checks on all three check policies (#285), and checks three things for each class, and a fourth for a caster:

| Difficulty | Required-path survival | One-hit-kill cap |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Easy       | ≥ 95%                  | 50%              |
| Medium     | ≥ 85%                  | 40%              |
| Hard       | ≥ 75%                  | 30%              |

- **Declared routes (#407).** A module may declare its routes: entrance-to-objective paths, each a list of rooms that must be passed in order, such as "through the flooded stair" and "through the guard hall". The gate judges each route as it judges the fewest-fights route (below): the weakest character playing _cautious_ with every kit at every level, on seeded checks and with every check failing, against the difficulty's survival share. A route may pass through a passage a flag opens: the harness goes first to where something sets the flag for sure, as on any required path. The module qualifies only if every route survives; the verdict gives each route's two lines after the fewest-fights route's, naming the route and its weakest kit and level ("Route guard-hall (through the guard hall), too deadly, FAIL: the level 1, 5th percentile Fighter playing cautious survived 0.0% of 4 runs with its weakest kit, mace …"). A route whose required path is the fewest-fights route's shares its runs. A route that can't be followed fails the module as `unfollowable-route`, naming the route, the room no way reaches and why: only talking opens the way (no style talks, #297), or a passage opens only once a flag is set that nothing the character does sets for sure ("forked-tunnels: route sluice (by the sluice) can't be followed: no way reaches room sluice-room: passage alcove-to-sluice opens only once flag sluice-open is set, and nothing the character does sets it for sure."). The fewest-fights route is still judged, and the reported styles (stealth-first, retreat, use-room, the reaction policies, spending early) still play it alone. A module that declares no routes is judged on its fewest-fights route as before. No shipped module declared routes until the Coldwater Weirgate (#412), so no earlier verdict or pinned figure changed. Following a route uses only the moves the harness already plays: no new action kinds.
- **Region sweeps (#408).** A long module's optional rooms are judged too (D8). For each region the gate plays a sweep: the _cautious_ style visits every room of the region not marked `avoidable`, the nearest not yet visited next by the fewest fights (through rooms of other regions where the way goes), and fights what waits in each, then goes on to the objective; the victory room is left to the objective, which comes last. Each sweep is judged as a declared route is (the weakest character with every kit at every level, on seeded checks and with every check failing), but at one difficulty lower than the module's (`sweepDifficulty`): an Easy module's sweeps must clear Medium and a Medium module's Hard; Hard has none lower, so a Hard module's sweeps are judged at Hard. The module qualifies only if every sweep survives; the verdict gives each region's two lines after the routes', naming the region and the difficulty it is judged at ("Sweep of region upper (Upper Tunnels), too deadly, FAIL: the level 3, 5th percentile Fighter playing cautious survived 0.0% of 4 runs with its weakest kit, … hard needs 75.0%."). A deadly room the character may leave alone is marked `avoidable`, which leaves it out of the sweep. The validator refuses an avoidable room a declared route names; the gate also fails a module as `avoidable-on-route` when a declared route's required path passes an avoidable room on the way between the rooms it names, or, in a module that declares no routes, when the fewest-fights route does ("lair-tunnels: room lair is avoidable, but route pit (by the bone pit) passes it: an avoidable room must lie off every declared route."). A sweep that can't reach one of its rooms (only talking opens the way, or a flag nothing sets for sure opens it) fails the module as `unsweepable-region`, naming the region and the room. A module that isn't long, with or without regions, is not swept; the Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped long module, so no earlier verdict or pinned figure changed. Sweeping uses only the moves the harness already plays: no new action kinds.
- **Too deadly.** The weakest character, the 5th percentile, playing _cautious_, must survive at least the difficulty's share of runs with every starting kit at every recommended level. The verdict names the kit and level it survives least with, and lists each. The same check is made again on the _always-fail_ branch, with every check in its worst band: the module must stay completable there (a run stranded with no way on or out fails the gate, naming the checks it made) and the weakest character must still survive the difficulty's share of runs. A seeded run that made no check is the always-fail run on its seed too, so only runs that made one are played again. Survival is judged on the required path a player who never talks must take (#297), so loot that only talking reaches can't make the cautious run give up and "survive" without reaching its objective; a module whose objective only talking reaches fails as `unreachable-objective`. A caster (#348) plays its first 50 seeded runs with each kit at each level by both casting policies (`POLICY_SEED_COUNT`), and every other run, the rest of the seeded ones and every other branch (always-fail, stealth-first, peaceful), by the one that survives more of those 50, as a player keeps to one way of playing; each kit's line names that policy. Its survival is judged only from level 3 (`CASTER_JUDGED_LEVEL`, owner's choice: early magic is weak and unreliable, so a Cleric or Wizard may struggle at levels 1 and 2, as in old-school play): a level below is listed as reported, and a module all of whose levels are below passes the check, which the verdict shows as "reported (not judged below level 3)". The career check (see Career simulation) still requires some weakest-caster career to reach level 5.
- **Too easy.** Early levels are a character's most vulnerable, so they must never be easy. A ranged weapon a module places or a merchant sells is tried like any other weapon, as its opening volley: one attack from full HP in round 1, with no close-combat disadvantage, by a Dexterity-first build of the same character, whose Strength and Dexterity rolls change places and whose +2 goes on Dexterity (#230). An ordinary enemy is any opponent not marked `boss: true` in the module. For the strongest character, the 95th percentile, at the maximum recommended level, with the kit, or the weapon the module places (wielded with the default kit's armour), and the Fighting Style that kill each enemy most often (Great Weapon Fighting counts for a placed two-handed or versatile weapon, Archery for a placed or sold ranged weapon (#225); a tie keeps Defense, the default), the gate takes each ordinary enemy's chance of dying to one attack from full HP (hit chance, critical hits, Fighting Style, masteries, the enemy's damage defences and Undead Fortitude counted, as in the harness, so a mace beats a shortsword against a Skeleton). More than half of the ordinary enemies over the difficulty's cap fails the module. The measure is per attack, not per turn: Action Surge's second attack, the light-weapon extra attack and Extra Attack's second attack are not counted. A module with no ordinary enemies passes. A caster's best repeatable attack (#348, D5) may be a damaging cantrip on its class list, whether or not it knows it, at its level's dice (`spellKillChance`: a spell attack's hit, a critical hit on a 20 doubling the dice, and Potent Cantrip's half on a miss or a save; a saving-throw cantrip's damage on a failed save), judged when it beats every weapon; the verdict names the cantrip. Beside it the gate reports, never judges, each ordinary enemy's chance of dying to one levelled damage spell on the class list at the highest slot the character has (every ray of Scorching Ray at it, Magic Missile's darts, a save spell's half on a success, Ice Knife's burst; damage at the end of a later turn is not counted).
- **Too easy by control** (#348, D5, a caster with control at the module's maximum level). For the same strongest character, each ordinary enemy's chance that one control used at the fight's opening takes it out of the fight (`controlChance`): the control spells on its class list it has slots for and the Cleric's Turn Undead, the best of them for each enemy. Out of the fight means unable to act on its next three turns (`CONTROL_TURNS`, owner's choice, about a fight's length): it fails its first saving throw and each save it repeats at the end of its turns until then (Turn Undead's and Hold Person's every turn; Sleep's once, as its second failure leaves the target Unconscious for the spell, and since #369 both of Sleep's are rolled with advantage, so four d20s must fail). An immune enemy, or one of a creature type the control doesn't work on, has no chance. More than half of the ordinary enemies over the difficulty's one-hit-kill cap fails the module, as for one attack. Judged on the first save alone, Sleep stopped most foes 60–75% of the time and would have failed 11 modules, and Turn Undead three, so the owner chose (11 October 2026) to weaken control rather than the modules: Sleep and Hold Person left the Wizard's and Cleric's lists, and a turned undead repeats its save each turn. #369 brought them back (owner's choice, 11 October 2026): Hold Person as written, as no shipped module of level 3 or more (its 2nd-level slot) had an ordinary humanoid foe (the Coldwater Weirgate's Bandits, #412, are the first: Hold Person stops each for three turns 27.5% of the time, under Medium's 40% cap); Sleep with its target saving with advantage, since a save at the end of every turn still failed three modules and a 2nd-level Sleep fails as the DC rises with level. Since #443 Sleep at level 4 is over the Weirgate's 40% cap on its three kobolds and Old Sullage (41.0%), and since #445 over the Thornwood Lodge's 30% on the Reaver's Gaoler (31.6%), one of nine foes; no other ordinary foe in a shipped module is over its cap with any control, which `tests/shipped-modules.test.mjs` checks for the Wizard and the Cleric, pinning those foes. Turn Undead now stops a zombie or skeleton for three turns 27–34% of the time at levels 2–4. In the two shipped modules with ordinary undead foes a Cleric can turn (#349), at each module's maximum level: the Abandoned Delve 27.5% (the zombie and the dead delver) and 34.3% (the skeletons) against a 30% cap since it became Hard (#442), the skeletons pinned over it, and the Coldwater Weirgate 34.3% (its drowned dead, at level 4, #443) against 40%. The Bramblecote Cider House's zombie is never turned, as a level-1 Cleric has no Channel Divinity; `tests/shipped-modules.test.mjs` checks that every ordinary (non-boss) undead foe in a shipped module is measured against Turn Undead, and pins these figures.
- **XP.** All the XP a module offers (every encounter's stat-block XP, wandering encounters' included (#335, #403), or the XP authored for slipping past it (#302) or for ending it peacefully (#304) when that is more, and the most any ending awards) must not take a character past the maximum recommended level + 1, or + 2 in a long module (#404, D6). The character starts one XP short of the level above the maximum, the worst case, and the SRD 5.2 XP table counts levels 6 (14,000 XP) and 7 (23,000 XP) for this check even though characters stop at level 5, so a module whose maximum is 4 may offer XP up to one short of level 6, and one whose maximum is 5 up to one short of level 7 (a long one's up to one short of level 8, 34,000 XP). The _always-succeed_ branch counts toward the limit: the strongest character at the maximum recommended level plays _direct_ with every check in its best band, so every way a check opens is open and every fight is fought, over the seeds in order until a run earns all the XP offered; the most XP a run earned counts if it is more than the module offers. Every XP award a module can make today is in the offered total, so this branch can't fail a module yet; it guards later awards that only a check can reach.

A module the harness can't play fails the gate with the harness's named reason. The verdict gives each class in turn ("… qualifies as hard for the Rogue."), naming each check, whether it passed and, for too easy, the enemies over the cap (and the cantrip that kills each, for a caster), with a line for each check-policy branch: survival on seeded checks, survival when every check fails, and the XP earned when every check succeeds. For a caster it adds too easy by control, the levelled spells' report and the spend-early runs' survival over the 50 seeds both policies play (reported, never judged), with the spells cast and XP earned a run. Beside them it reports, never judging them, the rests the weakest character took in its judged runs (#335): short and long rests a run, and rests a run a wandering encounter interrupted. Every shipped module qualifies on all three branches (#285).

**The casters (#348).** The harness plays a Cleric's healing spells (#339): below its style's heal threshold it casts one on itself before a potion, in a fight a bonus-action spell (Healing Word) first, which leaves its action to attack, and out of a fight the one with the most healing dice (Cure Wounds), after a long or short rest it may take. From level 2 it spends Channel Divinity by policy (#341): Turn Undead whenever an undead foe is left to turn, before any attack; turned foes are attacked only once every foe is turned, so the others stay out of the fight; Divine Spark's radiant damage at the weakest untouched foe while it leaves a use for Turn Undead or Preserve Life; and, below the heal threshold, Preserve Life after its healing spells and before a potion. It never leaves a room with every foe turned. A Wizard (#340) casts Mage Armor on itself once, outside a fight before its first one (a base-AC spell that outlasts a fight), attacks with a cantrip, a ranged one (Fire Bolt) in the opening round and a melee one from round 2, when a ranged spell attack has disadvantage, and answers a hit with Shield at its lowest slot, only when Shield's +5 AC turns the hit into a miss. Every other spell is cast by the casting policies (see Balance harness). Every run records the spells cast and the uses of Channel Divinity spent. Until #348 the gate reported the Cleric (#339) and the Wizard (#340) on 50 seeds and judged neither; since #348 it judges both on the same 200 seeds as every class. The weakest survival of each caster, seeded (and when every check fails, where different), at the level and with the kit it survives least with, with the policy that kit played (a level below 3 is reported, not judged):

| The Thornwood Lodge    | Hard     | 82.5% conserve                    | 92.5% spend-early                    |

Before #348 the harness cast no levelled damage or control spell and the Cleric never cast Sacred Flame, and the casters failed most modules. A conserving caster's two level-1 slots go to healing or Shield, so at level 1 spending them early wins more fights; conserving wins the longer modules.

**Formats (#348).** The library (25), save (50) and trace (44) formats bump: a sheet may no longer prepare or hold Sleep or Hold Person (only the test-only caster may, until #369 below), and a turned undead's condition carries the save it repeats. The prompt (v34) says a turned undead saves again at the end of each of its turns.

**Formats (#369).** The library (26), save (51) and trace (45) formats bump: a Wizard's sheet may hold and prepare Sleep and Hold Person again, a Cleric's Hold Person, and a condition's save may carry what gives it advantage (Sleep's). The prompt (v35) says Sleep's target saves with advantage, and the cast tool's description of Sleep says so.

**The Rogue (#310).** The harness plays a Rogue's features whenever its turn offers them: Hide or Steady Aim before an attack and Hide after it, Cunning Strike with a Sneak Attack, and Uncanny Dodge on every hit it can halve (see the Rogue's sections). It picks a lock with its thieves' tools before breaking the door (a locked door can't be forced), and searches for and disarms traps in the careful styles, as every class does. The judged runs never sneak: only the _stealth-first_ style, reported for both classes, sneaks in (owner decision, 9 October 2026). Sneaking in only makes the opponents roll initiative with disadvantage, and in trials it moved the Rogue's survival by no more than the seeds' noise. A Rogue has no Fighting Style, so too easy tries each of its kits and the placed weapons without one. Too easy judges the Rogue's plain attack, as it does the Fighter's. Beside it, the gate reports (never judges) the Rogue's chances with its bonus action before the attack, as the harness plays it: Steady Aim's advantage from level 3, or at level 2 Hide's, as often as its Stealth check meets the best passive Perception in the enemy's fight; advantage brings Sneak Attack's dice with a Finesse or ranged weapon (owner decision, 9 October 2026). A module's declared difficulty is the strictest every class clears with 3 points of slack (#252; a caster's survival below level 3 is reported and sets nothing, #348), so the weakest class sets it: the Thornwood Lodge is Hard because the Rogue survives 85.0% and the Cleric 82.5% (#445; the Rogue 85.5% before), though the Fighter's 94.5% would make it Medium.

**Reactions, reported (#304).** For a module with a reaction-eligible encounter the gate also reports the weakest character's _cautious_ runs on each reaction policy, with every kit at every recommended level on seeded checks: always attacking (the judged runs) and taking the peaceful option, each with its survival with its weakest kit, and over every run its completion rate, the encounters it ended peacefully and the XP it earned. The report is never judged, and `passesGate` leaves it out.

**Retreat, reported (#396).** Beside its checks the gate reports the weakest character playing _retreat_ with every kit at every recommended level on seeded checks: its survival with its weakest kit, and over every run its completion rate, the fights it fled and the XP it earned. The judged styles never flee, so the gate stays a lower bound on survival; `passesGate` leaves the report out. A retreat run differs from the cautious run on its seed only from a fight turn it begins below its heal threshold with no heal left, so a cautious run with no such turn is reused as the retreat run (`retreatChances`, as #347 reuses runs). Fleeing changed no judged verdict or pinned figure. A flight can still cost a run: the attacks it draws at low hit points can kill, and a run that gets away leaves without its objective. The report costs about 3% of the gate's time.

**Use the room, reported (#402).** Beside its checks the gate reports the weakest character playing _use-room_ with every kit at every recommended level on seeded checks: its survival with its weakest kit, and over every run its completion rate, the fight actions it used and the XP it earned ("Use the room, reported (not judged): …"). The judged styles never use fight actions, so the gate stays a lower bound; `passesGate` leaves the report out. In a module without fight actions the use-room run is the judged run on its seed, reused, so the report costs nothing there: no shipped module had a fight action until the Coldwater Weirgate (#412), so no earlier judged verdict or pinned figure changed. The one-hit-kill measure weighs one attack of the strongest character, so it never counts a fight action. The harness's played-actions table marks `use-feature` played (by _use-room_ only).

**Alarm, reported (#401).** For a module with an alarm, beside its checks the gate reports the judged runs that raised it: the weakest character's _cautious_ runs with every kit at every recommended level, on seeded checks and with every check failing, how many of each raised it, and those runs by checks, kit, level and what raised it, with their seeds ("Alarm, reported (not judged): … raised it in 438 of 600 seeded runs and 600 of 600 always-fail runs: seeded mace level 1, by examining the Stacked Crates (failure by 5 or more), seeds 0, 7–9, …"). It is never judged; a module without an alarm has no such report.

**Wandering, reported (#403).** For a module whose regions have wandering encounter tables, beside its checks the gate reports the wandering fights the judged runs met: the weakest character's _cautious_ runs with every kit at every recommended level, on seeded checks and with every check failing, how many of each met one and how many fell in one, and the XP the wandering encounters offer ("Wandering encounters, reported (not judged): … met one in 8 of 12 seeded runs and 8 of 12 always-fail runs, and fell in one in 0 and 0; they count in survival, and their 25 XP in the XP limit."). Those fights already count in the judged survival like any other, and their XP in the XP limit; the report is never judged. A module without region tables has no such report.

## Career simulation

Can a new character reach the top level by playing the shipped modules (#290)? `npm.cmd run career` (`src/career-5e.ts`) plays one career on each of seeds 0–199 through every built-in module, or the module files named after it, and exits with code 1 if no career reaches the required level; `--seeds <count>`, `--required-level <level>`, `--class <class>` (`fighter`, the default, `rogue`, `cleric` or `wizard`: any class the gate judges) and `--json` change the run. `npm.cmd run verify` makes the same check on the shipped modules for a career of every class the gate judges (the Fighter and the Rogue from #310, the Cleric and the Wizard from #348, D14), and fails if any class's careers reach no further than level 4.

- **The character.** The gate's weakest of its class, the 5th percentile, created with the class's default choices and kit (the Fighter's mace, the Rogue's shortsword and dagger, the Cleric's mace and daggers, the Wizard's quarterstaff and dagger), starting at level 1 with 0 XP. A caster plays the default casting policy, _conserve_, and makes each new level's cantrip, spell and spellbook choices in its list's order.
- **Order.** The modules in the browser's order: by recommended level range, lowest first, then easy to hard, then by id. A career plays each module once, as soon as its level reaches the module's minimum recommended level, and skips one it hasn't reached yet; a module no career reaches is reported as not played. It plays every module in the gate's style, _cautious_, with seeded checks.
- **Between modules.** A surviving ending settles the character as the character library does: it keeps exactly what it holds at the end (gear, potions, treasure and coin), is credited its XP, and rests to full health. A pending level-4 choice is made by the gate's policy before the next module: +2 to the attack ability, and for the Fighter the longsword mastered. A defeat ends the career in that module, as it does in the library.
- **The check.** It passes when some career reaches the required level: 5, the top level, since the Thornwood Lodge shipped (#291; 4 until then). A module set whose XP can't carry any career that far fails, and so does one where every career falls first. Each module is gated on its own (see Balance gate); the career takes the modules as given.
- **The report** names each module in the order played, with how many careers played it and at what level, the share that survived it, the mean XP it credited the survivors and how many fell there; then how many careers ended at each level and how many fell. Over the four modules, in browser order (the Cider House, the Delve, the Weirgate and the Lodge), 80.5% of Fighter, 79.5% of Rogue, 44% of Cleric and 48% of Wizard careers reach level 5, every career that survives the Lodge. Falls in the four, in order: Fighter 7, 17, 11 and 4; Rogue 4, 17, 1 and 19; Cleric 53, 39, 8 and 12; Wizard 14, 54, 10 and 26. The casters fall mostly at level 1 and in the Delve, as the old-school curve intends. The Lodge's falls are 3 over the plan's projection for the Rogue and the Cleric, a gap the owner accepted on #444.

## Encounter estimate

Before writing a fight into a module, an author can estimate its danger from bestiary ids (#236): `npm.cmd run estimate -- --levels 2-3 wolf:2` (`src/estimate-5e.ts`). Each argument is a bestiary id with an optional count (`wolf:2`, or `bandit` for one), up to 8 monsters in all, as in a module; `--levels` gives the character levels (1–5, by default 1–3, `--levels 2` for one), and `--seeds`, `--percentiles`, `--styles` and `--json` are the harness's. `--bestiary <file>` estimates against another bestiary file instead of the built-in one.

- **What it plays.** The fight becomes a one-room module: its start room holds the fight, winning ends it in victory and losing in defeat, and monsters sharing a name are numbered. The harness plays that module through the real runtime with the same sampled characters and seeds as the gate, at every level, for each percentile character (the 5th and 95th by default), with every starting kit, in every style.
- **What it reports.** For each level and character, each monster's chance of dying to one attack from full HP, with the kit and Fighting Style that kill it most often (measured as the gate measures its strongest character at the maximum level), and which difficulties' caps it is over; then for each kit and style the survival rate, HP lost in the fight and rounds. Last, the gate's verdict on the module at easy, medium and hard, which always uses the gate's own characters (5th and 95th percentiles), style and seeds whatever `--percentiles` and `--styles` say: the three checks of the balance gate, so a module whose only fight is this one, at these levels, qualifies at the same difficulties. A module with more rooms, potions or other fights before it will measure differently, and the estimate counts every monster as ordinary: mark a boss in the module to exempt it from the cap.
- **Failures.** A bestiary id that isn't in the bestiary fails as `unknown-monster`, and a monster with a trait the encounter engine does not apply as `unsimulated-trait`, each naming the monster; the harness's own failures (`step-limit` and the rest) are reported the same way. The exit code is 1 on a named failure and 2 on a malformed command line.

# Balance tools

## Balance harness

`src/balance-5e.ts` plays any 5e module through the real runtime and measures how dangerous it is. `npm.cmd run balance` reports on the built-in modules, or the module files named after it, for each class, then gives the balance gate's verdict on each and exits 1 if any fails or doesn't qualify (`--help` lists its options). The gate always plays every check policy and every class, whatever the options say.

- **Characters.** For each class, 10,000 creations are rolled with 4d6-drop-lowest from a fixed seed, placed by the class's own priority, and ranked by the sum of the ability modifiers its creation gives; the weak and strong characters are the 5th and 95th percentiles. Each takes the class's creation defaults (`FIGHTER_DEFAULT_CHOICES` and the other classes' defaults), so other choices, such as other masteries, are not gated. The report plays the class's default kit; the gate plays every kit. Each plays at every recommended level, at full health with that level's XP. Level choices follow `gateLevelChoice`: the Ability Score Improvement goes to the attack ability (the class's first primary ability, or Dexterity for a Dexterity-first build) until 20, then by the class's ability priority; the Fighter's fourth mastery is a weapon the module places or sells that the gate is trying, else the first unmastered in catalogue order. A caster picks each level's cantrips and spells in its list's order (`withOwedChoices`).
- **Objective and required path.** A module's objective is its victory if it has one, or else getting out with loot (treasure or coin). The required path is the route to it through the fewest fights, then the fewest moves: to the victory fight, or to the nearest loot and out by the nearest exit. Every other room, and its fight, is optional. No style talks, so the gate judges the route a player who never talks must take: the nearest loot is the nearest such a player can reach, and a module whose objective only talking reaches fails as `unreachable-objective`. On a declared route the required path first passes the route's rooms in order, then goes on to the objective.
- **Route planning.** A door with a check counts as passable until it blocks the way. A door only a key opens counts only with one of its keys carried; otherwise the plan fetches the nearest of its keys that isn't lost, and the door stays shut once every key is lost. A way a flag opens is shut until the flag is set; the plan then goes to the nearest room where something not yet done sets it for sure (as the validator counts it). It never plans through a way a flag has closed. A way only an item used on a feature opens: the plan fetches the item first, then goes to the feature and uses it. A hidden passage only talking opens is never passable.
- **Styles.** Each picks only actions `projectActions` offers as available. _direct_ enters every room and fights every fight, healing below a quarter of its hit points. _cautious_ skips optional fights but looks into optional rooms with no fight, searches each room for traps and disarms those it finds, and heals below half. _avoid-optional_ enters only the required rooms and heals below a quarter, but goes round by any room where a flag has closed the way. _stealth-first_ plays as _cautious_, but sneaks into every fight it would enter and, unseen, slips past it unless its goal is in that room or the way on is shut, when it ambushes; a fight it slipped past is met again if its route comes back. _retreat_ plays as _cautious_, but below its heal threshold with no heal left it flees each fight once toward the nearest exit, by a way out with no fight on it (after Disengage when it has it), and then leaves. _use-room_ plays as _cautious_, but with no heal left uses the room's fight action whenever offered, against the opponent with the most hit points, by its first approach. Only _retreat_ flees and only _use-room_ uses fight actions.
- **What every style does.** It examines every feature and fallen body, takes every item, attacks the opponent with the fewest hit points (with the light-weapon extra attack on it), uses Action Surge once the action is spent, and tries a shut door's key, then force, pick and break. It takes another try a module offers, but never one whose damage could leave it below its heal threshold. It never talks and never uses Tactical Mind (every Second Wind is kept for healing), so the gate stays a lower bound. It answers a reaction roll by the run's reaction policy: _attack_ by default, or _peaceful_.
- **Class play.** A Rogue takes Hide or Steady Aim before an attack and Hide after it, adds Cunning Strike to a Sneak Attack, halves every hit it can with Uncanny Dodge, and picks a lock before breaking the door (see [the Rogue](classes.md#rogue)). A Cleric heals itself below the heal threshold with a spell before a potion (a bonus-action spell in a fight, the most healing dice out of one); spends Turn Undead whenever an undead foe is left to turn, attacking turned foes only once every foe is turned, and never leaves a room with every foe turned; uses Divine Spark on the weakest untouched foe while a use remains for Turn Undead or Preserve Life; and uses Preserve Life after its healing spells. A Wizard casts Mage Armor once before its first fight, a ranged cantrip in round 1 and a melee one from round 2, and Shield only when its +5 turns a hit into a miss. Every class takes Last Stand whenever its death saving throw offers it, drinking the first potion offered (house rule, #499). A caster holding a Pearl of Power uses it as soon as a slot is spent outside a fight (#538).
- **Casting policies** (`CASTING_POLICIES`). A caster plays every style by _conserve_ (the default) or _spend-early_. Both heal as above and otherwise act with a damaging cantrip on the weakest foe, ahead of a weapon. _conserve_ casts a levelled spell only below the heal threshold with no heal left. _spend-early_ casts one every turn it can: with two or more foes untouched, a control spell (Sleep, Hold Person) on the one with the most hit points, never one immune; otherwise the damage spell worth most (`castValue`). Neither casts a second concentration spell, nor any other buff, curse or restoration spell. A foe asleep or turned is left alone while others stand.
- **Check policies.** _seeded_, the default, rolls every check as a player meets it. _always-fail_ and _always-succeed_ make every check total the lowest or highest any character of the module's maximum level could make (`extremeTotals`), so each lands in its worst or best reachable band; a search finds no trap, or every trap. The d20 is still drawn, so other dice stay seeded; trap saving throws stay seeded. Loot a failed check left hidden is left behind, and so is loot an opponent carried that left no body (it fled, surrendered or ended peacefully). **A lost key with no way on strands the run, and the gate fails it:** a key lost either way counts as absent, the run plans around its door, and a run left with no way on or out is stranded. The module must give another way, such as another route or a door that can be picked, forced or broken.
- **Metrics,** per level, percentile and style: survival and completion, fights slipped past and fled, endings, each fight's HP lost, rounds and defeat rate, healing and rests used, trap damage, XP and treasure, and each opponent's chance of dying to one attack from full HP.
- **Failures.** A module the harness can't play fails with a named reason: `unsupported-action` (the runtime offers an action no style knows), `step-limit` (a run takes too many actions), `stranded` (a run is alive with no action that leads on or out; the reason names the check policy, every check made with its band and any passage it closed, and every lost key), `unreachable-objective` (when only talking would reach it, the reason names the route talking opens) or `unsimulated-trait` (an opponent lists a trait the engine doesn't apply; only an unvalidated module meets it).
- **New kinds.** The harness projects only the action kinds and spells a style may play. Spells and exploring's kinds are listed by hand in `src/balance-5e.ts` (`CAST_SPELLS`, `FIRST_ACTIONS`, `EXAMINING` and the `EXPLORE_` sets): a spell a new policy casts, or a kind a style takes while exploring, must be named there, or the harness never sees it offered.

## Balance gate

A module is offered in the browser only if it qualifies at its declared difficulty for every class creation offers (`gateModule`; `GATE_CLASSES` is `OFFERED_CLASS_IDS`; `gateAdventure` judges one class), and `npm.cmd run verify` fails if a built-in module doesn't. The gate plays each class's characters on seeds 0–199 and all three check policies. How to choose the declared difficulty is in [the authoring guide](../module-authoring.md#settled-authoring-rules).

| Difficulty | Required-path survival | One-hit-kill cap |
| ---------- | ---------------------- | ---------------- |
| Easy       | ≥ 95%                  | 50%              |
| Medium     | ≥ 85%                  | 40%              |
| Hard       | ≥ 75%                  | 30%              |

- **Too deadly.** The weakest character, playing _cautious_, must survive at least the difficulty's share of runs with every kit at every recommended level, on seeded checks and again with every check failing, where the module must also stay completable. The verdict names the kit and level it survives least with. Judged runs never sneak, flee or use fight actions: only the reported styles do. A seeded run that made no check stands in for its always-fail run.
- **Casters.** A caster plays its first `POLICY_SEED_COUNT` seeds with each kit at each level by both casting policies, and every other run by the one that survived more of them; each kit's line names it. Casters are judged from level 3 (`CASTER_JUDGED_LEVEL`): early magic is weak, so a level below is reported, not judged, and a module whose levels are all below passes the check. The career check still requires some weakest-caster career to reach level 5.
- **Declared routes.** Each declared route is judged as the fewest-fights route is (the weakest character, _cautious_, every kit and level, seeded and always-fail), and every route must survive. The verdict gives each route's lines after the fewest-fights route's. A route that can't be followed fails the module as `unfollowable-route`, naming the room no way reaches and why: only talking opens it, or a flag nothing sets for sure. The reported styles play the fewest-fights route alone.
- **Region sweeps.** In a long module, for each region the _cautious_ style visits every room not marked `avoidable`, the nearest unvisited next, fights what waits there, then goes on to the objective (the victory room comes last). Each sweep is judged as a route is, but one difficulty lower than the module's (`sweepDifficulty`; Hard stays Hard). A deadly room the character may leave alone is marked `avoidable`. The gate fails a module as `avoidable-on-route` when a required path passes an avoidable room, and as `unsweepable-region` when a sweep can't reach one of its rooms. A module that isn't long is not swept.
- **Too easy.** Early levels must never be easy. For the strongest character at the maximum recommended level, with the kit, placed weapon and Fighting Style that kill each enemy most often, the gate takes each ordinary (non-boss) enemy's chance of dying to one attack from full HP: hit chance, critical hits, masteries, damage defences and Undead Fortitude counted, but not a second attack in the turn. A ranged weapon the module places or sells is tried as an opening volley by a Dexterity-first build. More than half the ordinary enemies over the cap fails the module; a module with none passes. A caster's best attack may be a damaging cantrip on its class list (`spellKillChance`), judged when it beats every weapon. The Rogue's plain attack is judged; its advantage from Hide or Steady Aim, and each enemy's chance against a levelled damage spell, are reported. Fight actions are never counted.
- **Too easy by control.** For a caster with control at the maximum level, each ordinary enemy's chance that one control at the fight's opening (a control spell it has slots for, or Turn Undead) keeps it from acting for `CONTROL_TURNS` turns (`controlChance`): it must fail its first save and each repeated save until then. Sleep's target rolls both its saves with advantage. An immune enemy, or one of a type the control doesn't affect, has no chance. More than half the ordinary enemies over the cap fails the module.
- **Class parity.** From level 4, each caster's weakest kit within 5 points of the Fighter's; see [Class parity](#balance-gate) below.
- **XP.** A character starting one XP short of the level above the module's maximum, and earning all the XP the module offers (every encounter's stat-block XP, wandering ones included, or its bypass or peaceful XP when more, plus the most any ending awards), must not pass the maximum + 1, or + 2 in a long module. Levels 6–8 use the SRD 5.2 XP table for this check though characters stop at 5. The _always-succeed_ branch, the strongest character playing _direct_, counts too if it earns more.

A module the harness can't play fails with the harness's named reason. The verdict gives each class in turn, naming each check and whether it passed, with a line per check-policy branch.

**Reported, not judged.** The gate also reports stealth-first, reactions (each reaction policy), retreat, use-room, the alarm (which runs raised it, by what and on which seeds), wandering encounters (the runs that met and fell in one; those fights already count in survival and their XP in the limit), the spend-early runs and the rests taken. `passesGate`, which decides what the browser offers, ignores them all. A reported style's run is reused from the judged run on its seed when it would play the same.

**Class parity.** At each recommended level from 4 (`PARITY_LEVEL`), the module verdict sets each caster's weakest kit on the fewest-fights route beside the Fighter's, from the judged runs (the weakest character, _cautious_, seeded and always-fail, the caster's better casting policy), and gives the gap in points. It plays no runs of its own, except at a level where the caster is sure to hold an item: there it plays the same runs carrying it. **It is judged** (#541, D4): a caster more than 5 points (`PARITY_POINTS`) behind the Fighter on either branch fails the module, in `passesGate` and verify alike, and the line names the class, level and gap. A module whose levels are all below 4 has no parity line.

**Sure-held items** (#539, D5). A caster item counts for a class at a level when it lies in a room on the required path of a module played earlier (ahead in the browser's order, `earlierModules`) whose lowest recommended level is that level or less, hidden in a feature with no check (a failed check may leave it hidden, and an opponent may flee or surrender with what it carries), is never missing on Scarce (no caster item is), and the class may attune it; at most 3 (`sureHeldItems`). The too-easy checks and the parity line play with them, and the verdict names them ("carrying the …"); the survival check plays without them, so it stays a lower bound. The callers that know the shipped set (the recorded verdicts, the browser, `npm.cmd run balance`) pass the earlier modules; a module gated alone holds none. The career simulation plays what each character holds, attuned items included. On the shipped modules it gives:

| Module gated    | Level | Classes        | Sure-held items                                          |
| --------------- | ----- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Thornwood Lodge | 4, 5  | Cleric, Wizard | Wand of the War Mage +1 (Coldwater Weirgate's dry shelf) |

Thornwood's own Pearl of Power counts for no module, as none is played after it; a Fighter or Rogue holds nothing that counts.

**Recorded verdicts.** `npm.cmd test` gates the four built-in modules for every class once, on worker threads (`tests/fixtures/gate-workers.mjs`), and pins each module's figures, so a change that alters how the gate plays fails. The browser server reads `adventures/5e/gate-verdicts.json` (`src/gate-verdicts-5e.ts`), each verdict keyed by a SHA-256 hash of the loaded module, and gates only a module whose hash has changed. An engine change doesn't change the hash, so `shipped-modules.test.mjs` checks the file against the gate's results; when a change moves a verdict, run `npm.cmd run gate:verdicts` and commit the file.

**Test timeouts are hang guards, not performance budgets.** A gate job that freezes is stopped and fails, naming its module and class; no test asserts how long the gate takes. Set such timeouts generously and don't creep them up.

## Career simulation

`npm.cmd run career` (`src/career-5e.ts`) asks whether a new character can reach level 5 by playing the shipped modules. It plays one career per seed: the gate's weakest character of a class, with the class's default choices and kit (and the _conserve_ policy for a caster), from level 1 and 0 XP. It plays the modules in the browser's order, each once, as soon as its level reaches the module's minimum, in the _cautious_ style on seeded checks; a module no career reaches is reported as not played. Between modules a surviving character settles as the library does (keeps what it holds, is credited its XP, rests to full) and makes any pending level choice by the gate's policy; a defeat ends the career. The check passes when some career reaches the required level, so a module set whose XP can't carry a career that far fails. `npm.cmd run verify` runs it for every class the gate judges. Each module is gated on its own; the career takes the modules as given. The report gives each module's careers played, survival, mean XP credited and falls, then how many careers ended at each level.

## Encounter estimate

`npm.cmd run estimate` (`src/estimate-5e.ts`) estimates a fight from bestiary ids before it is written into a module. The fight becomes a one-room module whose start room holds it, and the harness plays it with the gate's sampled characters and seeds at the chosen levels, with every kit in every style. It reports each monster's one-hit-kill chance and the caps it is over, the survival, HP lost and rounds for each kit and style, and the gate's verdict at each difficulty. A module with more rooms, potions or other fights will measure differently. The estimate counts every monster as ordinary: mark a boss in the module to exempt it from the cap. An unknown id fails as `unknown-monster`, and an unsimulated trait as `unsimulated-trait`.

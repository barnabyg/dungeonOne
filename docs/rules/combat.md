# Combat and talk

## Combat

Combat (#128–#130) uses the numbers above. An encounter has two sides, the party and its opponents, and each side may hold several combatants; for now the party is one Fighter, and a module may set several opponents against it. Every die comes from the adventure session's own seeded stream and is recorded with the action that drew it.

- **Initiative.** When a fight begins, each combatant rolls d20 + its initiative bonus (Dexterity modifier). A level-3 Champion rolls it with advantage (Remarkable Athlete, #315): two d20s, the higher kept. A surprised Champion's advantage and disadvantage cancel, as SRD 5.2 says, so it rolls one d20; the card says "advantage (Remarkable Athlete) and disadvantage (surprised) cancel", and the initiative breakdown names both. Higher totals act first. A tie goes to the higher Dexterity score; combatants still tied each roll a d20 roll-off, repeated among any still tied, and the higher roll acts first. The browser's initiative table shows the order, each total, hit points and armour class, and highlights and tags the combatant whose turn it is; each combatant's roll and roll-off are under **How initiative was rolled**. Once the fight is over the table folds away behind an **Initiative order** button.
- **Turns.** Combatants act in initiative order, round after round, skipping the defeated. A defeated opponent drops out: it takes no more turns and cannot be targeted, and the fight goes on while any opponent stands.
- **Turn economy.** Each turn has one action, one bonus action and one reaction. On the player's turn an attack on one living opponent of their choice takes the action, the light-weapon extra attack takes the bonus action (unless Nick makes it part of the Attack action), Second Wind takes the bonus action, drinking a potion takes the bonus action (as in SRD 5.2), and Action Surge adds one more action. From level 5 the Attack action makes two attacks (Extra Attack): the bar keeps an attack button for each living opponent after the first, labelled "Second attack on _name_ with _weapon_", and the status strip says a second attack is left to make. The player uses them by the action bar's Attack, Extra attack, Drink, Second Wind, Action Surge and End turn buttons or by telling the AI DM. The bar always shows the whole toolkit; an action the engine would refuse now is disabled with the engine's reason, shortened to "Action used", "Bonus action used", "Attack first", "Extra attack used", "Full HP" or "No uses left". Action Surge is in the bar from level 2. The turn lasts until the player ends it or nothing is left that the engine would accept, so a player at full health without Action Surge ends the turn by attacking, as before. The fight's first turn says so ("It is your turn. Your turn ends by itself once nothing is left to do."), and the browser's End turn names what it would leave unused ("Left unused: Action Surge", #392). The browser's status strip shows what is left this turn and each feature's uses. Only the Rogue's Uncanny Dodge (#308) and reaction spells such as Shield (#337) use a reaction: opportunity attacks need positions, and no Fighter feature at levels 1–5 takes one; fleeing (#396) draws an attack from each foe in their place, which takes no reaction. Opponents only attack.
- **Second Wind and Action Surge in combat.** Second Wind rolls 1d10 + Fighter level and restores that much, up to maximum HP. It is refused at full health, when the bonus action is spent and when no use is left. Action Surge is refused below level 2 and when its use is spent. A refusal changes nothing and spends nothing.
- **Light-weapon extra attack (Light, SRD 5.2).** A character holding two light weapons attacks with the first, and after that attack may make one extra attack that turn with the second against any living opponent. Its damage adds no ability modifier unless the modifier is negative, or the character has Two-Weapon Fighting. It takes the bonus action, so it competes with Second Wind and a potion, unless the second weapon has Nick and is mastered, which makes it part of the Attack action. It is offered once per turn, Action Surge included. The action bar shows Extra attack for each living opponent only to a character holding two light weapons, disabled with "Attack first" before an attack this turn, "Extra attack used" once made, and "Bonus action used" without Nick once the bonus action is spent. The AI DM's `light_attack` tool is offered exactly while it is enabled; called otherwise, the engine refuses it.
- **Weapon masteries.** Each applies only to a mastered weapon being wielded.
  - _Sap:_ when the attack hits a creature that survives, the creature is sapped: it has disadvantage on its next attack roll, which spends the effect. The effect also ends at the start of the player's next turn. The browser marks a sapped combatant in the initiative table.
  - _Vex:_ when the attack hits and deals damage to a creature that survives, the attacker has advantage on its next attack roll against that creature, with any weapon, which spends it. It lapses at the end of the attacker's next turn.
  - _Graze:_ when the attack misses, natural 1s included, the target still takes damage equal to the attack's damage modifier, if above 0, of the weapon's type.
  - _Nick:_ see the light-weapon extra attack. Nick acts only on the extra attack: the SRD lets the extra attack be part of the Attack action, and here that holds when the second weapon has Nick.
- **Heavy weapons.** A heavy melee weapon wielded with Strength below 13, or a heavy ranged weapon with Dexterity below 13, attacks with disadvantage (SRD 5.2).
- **Ranged weapons (#230).** A ranged attack uses Dexterity, takes the action like any attack, and spends one arrow or bolt of the kind its weapon needs; the attack's line says how many are left ("2 arrows left."). With none it is refused before any die is rolled ("You have no arrows left for the shortbow."), and the action bar shows Attack disabled with "No arrows" or "No bolts"; the AI DM is not offered `attack`. Switching weapons uses the turn's one object interaction (**Wield**), with no free swap. SRD 5.2 gives a ranged attack disadvantage within 5 feet of a hostile creature that can see you; without positions every opponent closes in, so the owner chose (6 October 2026) **no disadvantage in a fight's first round** (an opening volley while foes close) and **disadvantage from round 2 on**, named "Close combat" on the result card. When a fight is won, half the ammunition spent in it, rounded down, is recovered at once ("You recover 1 arrow from the fight. You have 2 arrows."); in a fight that is lost, or an adventure abandoned, the ammunition rolls back with the rest of the possessions. Opponents' ranged attacks (a Bandit's light crossbow, a Goblin's shortbow) stay out of scope.
- **Advantage and disadvantage.** With advantage the engine rolls two d20s and keeps the higher; with disadvantage, the lower. With sources of both it rolls one d20. The kept die decides natural 1s, natural 20s and critical hits. The result card names each source and shows both dice, for example "at disadvantage (Sap): 18 and 6, keeping 6". Advantage and disadvantage come only from the engine's rules: for now, Sap, a heavy weapon below its ability 13, a ranged attack from round 2 (Close combat) and the attacker's poisoned or prone condition give disadvantage, and Vex, Pack Tactics and a prone or paralysed target give advantage. Neither the player nor the AI DM can grant them.
- **Conditions (#232).** The engine owns every condition: it records on the combatant which condition it is, what gave it (the opponent and its attack), how many of the combatant's turns it lasts and the save that ends it, if any. Only poisoned, prone, paralysed (#234), and Sleep's incapacitated and unconscious (#340) are in play; frightened and restrained wait for a monster that needs them, and 0 HP is still instant defeat. The balance harness plays riders through the real engine, and each fight's record counts the conditions put on the character, by kind (#270).
  - _Poisoned_ (SRD 5.2): disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks. No ability check is rolled in a fight, and conditions end with the fight, so only the attack rolls are affected yet. Source: the Giant Spider's Bite (DC 11 Constitution save; up to 10 turns, a minute, repeating the save at the end of each of its turns).
  - _Prone_ (SRD 5.2): disadvantage on its own attack rolls; attack rolls against it have advantage. Without positions every attacker counts as within 5 feet, so every attack against a prone combatant has advantage. Standing up is abstracted: a prone combatant spends its next turn getting up, so it stays prone until the end of that turn, attacking at disadvantage, and then stands. Source: the Wolf's Bite (DC 11 Strength save).
  - _Paralysed_ (#234, SRD 5.2): the combatant can't take actions, bonus actions or reactions, automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws (no die is rolled; the narration says why), attack rolls against it have advantage, and every hit on it is a critical hit, doubling its damage dice. Without positions every attacker counts as within 5 feet. The player's action bar offers only Wait (its End turn), with the reason beside it, and the engine refuses every other fight action, from the bar or the AI DM, with "You are paralysed and can't act until it ends; you can only wait." A paralysed opponent spends its turn doing nothing but any repeat save; no shipped monster can paralyse one yet. A paralysed ally does not count for Pack Tactics. Source: the Ghoul's Claw (SRD 5.2: +4, 1d4 + 2 slashing; DC 10 Constitution save; paralysed until the end of the target's next turn, with no repeat save). The SRD's exemption of Undead and elf targets is not modelled: the Fighter is the only target. A rider may instead give paralysis for up to 10 turns with a repeat save, as poison does.
  - _Frightened_ (#341, SRD 5.2, from Turn Undead): disadvantage on its attack rolls and ability checks. Without positions its source is always in sight, and its bar on moving closer changes nothing. A turned undead is Incapacitated too, so it can't act at all.
  - _Incapacitated_ and _Unconscious_ (#340, SRD 5.2, from Sleep): an incapacitated combatant takes no actions, bonus actions or reactions, and loses its concentration. An unconscious one is incapacitated too, fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws without a roll, and attack rolls against it have advantage and every hit is a critical hit, as against a paralysed one ("Critical hit: Giant Rat is unconscious!"). It drops prone as well, which adds nothing more here. Both last only while Sleep holds the target (see The Wizard's spells).
  - _Riders._ A monster's attack may carry a rider: extra damage of its own type on a hit (the Giant Spider's 1d6 poison), with its dice doubled by a critical hit, and a condition, after a saving throw if the rider names one (d20 + the target's save bonus against the DC; the player's Strength and Constitution saves add its proficiency). A hit that drops the target to 0 HP gives no condition and rolls no save. The same condition again replaces the old one.
  - _Ending._ At the end of each of its turns a combatant rolls the repeat save against each condition that allows one, and a success ends it; then a condition whose turns have run out ends. Every condition ends when the fight does. Each save, condition and ending is an event with its dice, narrated and replayed exactly from the session's seed.
  - _Shown._ The browser tags each combatant's conditions in the initiative table, and the character's on the status strip, each with its source and how it ends for screen readers; the AI DM's scene and character status report them, and no tool can give, remove or change one.
- **Pack Tactics (#232).** A monster with Pack Tactics (the Wolf, the Giant Rat) has advantage on its attack rolls while an ally on its side is alive and able to act. Without positions, the ally need not be beside the target.
- **Damage types (#233).** Every damage roll has one of the 13 SRD 5.2 damage types (acid, bludgeoning, cold, fire, force, lightning, necrotic, piercing, poison, psychic, radiant, slashing, thunder): weapons, monster attacks, riders and traps. A module or bestiary naming any other type is refused.
- **Resistance, vulnerability and immunity (#233).** A stat block may list damage resistances, vulnerabilities and immunities, each type in at most one list. After the damage is rolled (critical dice, Great Weapon Fighting and modifier included), immunity makes it 0, resistance halves it (rounding down) and vulnerability doubles it; a rider's extra damage and Graze's damage on a miss are adjusted by their own type. The attack's event records the damage rolled and which defence applied; the result card says so ("= 7 bludgeoning, doubled to 14 (vulnerable)", "halved to 3 (resistant)", "ignored (immune)") and its damage roll shows the damage taken in bold. The bestiary's Skeleton is vulnerable to bludgeoning; the Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul are immune to poison damage.
- **Condition immunities (#233).** A stat block may list the conditions (poisoned or prone) it cannot be given: a rider's condition then rolls no save and has no effect. The Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul are immune to poisoned; their SRD 5.2 immunity to exhaustion (and the Ghoul's to charmed) is left out, as neither condition is in play.
- **Monster saving throws (#233).** A monster's saving throw is d20 + the ability's modifier, plus its proficiency bonus (from its challenge rating: +2 up to CR 4, +3 up to 8, and so on) in each save its stat block lists in `saveProficiencies`; the Zombie is proficient in Wisdom saves (+0). Like every die, the d20 comes from the session's seeded stream.
- **Undead Fortitude (#233).** When damage reduces a monster with Undead Fortitude (the Zombie) to 0 HP, unless the damage is radiant or from a critical hit, it makes a Constitution saving throw against DC 5 + the damage taken (the attack's damage and its rider's, after any resistance); on a success it drops to 1 HP instead and fights on. The attack's line shows it at 0 HP, and a following line gives the save: "Success: Zombie refuses to fall and has 1/15 HP" or "Failure: Zombie stays down", then its defeat. It may save again each time it is reduced to 0.
- **Feature uses and rests.** Every adventure starts with all Second Wind and Action Surge uses, because a rest between adventures restores them and every hit point; spent uses, like hit points and carried items, last from one fight to the next, and only a short rest restores some, and a long rest (both below) all, during the adventure. The sheet and the creation preview say so once, under the features, for a character with a feature with limited uses (spell slots alone don't count) (#431). The fight and the feature descriptions leave it out: the resource pips show the uses left, and no rest happens mid-fight. Since the AI DM's scene lists the features with their descriptions, dropping the rule from them is prompt v44.
- **Feature recovery (#333).** Each feature with uses records in its class data how many it has at each level and what a **short rest** and a **long rest** each restore: a number of uses, or all of them (SRD 5.2). Second Wind regains one use on a short rest and all on a long rest; Action Surge regains its use on either. The adventure session tracks the uses left of each such feature by its id.
- **Hit dice (#333).** A character has one hit die of its class's size per level (a level-3 Fighter has 3d10, a level-3 Rogue 3d8). The adventure session tracks the hit dice left, all of them at the start; the AI DM's character status reports them ("Hit dice: 3 of 3 d10 left"), and the browser's status strip shows a pip for each outside a fight's turns (they can't be spent in a fight). Only a short rest spends them; a long rest (#335, below) and a rest between adventures regain them all (SRD 5.2, D2).
- **Short rests (#334).** Outside a fight, in a room with no unresolved hostile encounter (none, or one won or ended peacefully), the character may take a short rest, **at most two per adventure** (a house rule; SRD 5.2's limit is the clock, which this game leaves out). The player chooses how many hit dice to spend, up to those left. They are spent one at a time: each rolls the class's hit die and heals the roll + the Constitution modifier, at least 0 per die, up to the maximum, and once the character is at full HP no more are spent (the rest of the number chosen stays in the pool); at full HP a rest, taken only to regain feature uses, spends none. Then each feature regains its short-rest uses, up to its most. A rest must do something: one at full HP with every use, one that spends no hit dice and restores no use, one in a fight or with foes in the room, and a third are refused, as is spending more hit dice than are left. Like everything in the adventure, the rest is undone if the adventure is abandoned or lost. The result card shows each die ("You spend a hit die: d10 7 + 2 = 9; you regain 9 HP and have 16/20 HP") and each feature's uses regained ("Second Wind regains 1 use (2 of 2 left)."). In the browser the rest sits in the character column, under what the character carries, outside a fight: the short rests and hit dice left and, while a rest would restore something, how many hit dice to spend (the most it can, unless the player chooses fewer) and Rest, disabled with its reason when the engine would refuse it (foes in the room, or both short rests taken). The AI DM has a `rest` tool, offered only while the engine would accept a rest, whose `hit_dice` lists the numbers it would accept. The balance harness rests whenever the character is below its style's heal threshold and a rest is offered, before drinking a potion.
- **Long rests (#335).** A module may mark a room as a **rest site** (`restSite: true`). There, outside a fight and with no unresolved hostile encounter in the room, the character may take a long rest, **at most one per adventure** (D1, a house rule; SRD 5.2 limits it to one a day, and there is no clock). It restores every hit point, every hit die (SRD 5.2, D2) and each feature's long-rest uses (Second Wind and Action Surge all of theirs); it leaves the short rests taken as they were. A long rest away from a rest site, after the one an adventure allows, in a fight, with foes in the room, or at full HP with every hit die and use is refused. The card reads "You take a long rest: you regain 6 HP (13/13 HP) and 1 hit die (2 of 2 d10 left). Long rests: 0 of 1 left in this adventure.", then each feature's uses regained. In the browser, at a rest site the character column shows a **Long rest** part under Short rest: the long rests left and, while one would restore something, Long rest, disabled with its reason when the engine would refuse it (foes here, or the long rest taken); the AI DM's character status lists "Long rests: 1 of 1 left" in a module with a rest site. The AI DM has a `long_rest` tool with no arguments, offered only while the engine would accept a long rest. The balance harness takes a long rest at a rest site whenever the character is below its heal threshold, before a short rest or a potion. Every shipped module has a rest site in each of its two regions, and _The Abandoned Delve_ (#442) a wandering encounter on a rest (a dead delver, a Zombie, on 25 or less).
- **Wandering encounters and interrupted rests (#335).** A module may author one **wandering encounter** (`wanderingEncounter`: an encounter in no room and a `chance` from 1 to 100). Each short or long rest first rolls a d100 from the session's seeded stream; at the chance or less the rest is **interrupted**: it restores nothing, is not counted against the rests an adventure allows (SRD 5.2: an interrupted rest gives no benefit and may be started again), and the wandering encounter's fight begins in the room where the character rests, as any fight begins. The card shows the d100 ("You keep watch as you rest: d100 12, 25 or less: Prowling Rat comes upon you. Your short rest is interrupted and restores nothing.", or "… d100 63, over 25: nothing disturbs you."). It fires at most once: once it has come, no rest rolls for it again. Its fight is credited like any other, once, with its opponents' XP, and losing it ends the adventure in its defeat ending. Its opponents carry nothing and never surrender. A module without a wandering encounter rolls nothing, so its rests are never interrupted. Since #403 this one encounter is a one-entry wandering encounter table rolled only on rests: it plays and draws its dice as before, and a module has it or region tables, never both.
- **Opponent targeting.** Each opponent attacks a living party combatant on its own turn. With one party combatant there is no choice and no die. With more (companions, later), the opponent rolls a die with one side per living party combatant, from the session's seeded stream, and attacks the one at that position counting in initiative order; the die is recorded with the attack.
- **Attacks.** d20 + attack bonus hits when it meets or beats the target's AC. A natural 20 (19–20 for a Champion) is a critical hit, which always hits and rolls the damage dice twice; a natural 1 always misses. Damage is the dice plus the damage modifier, never below 0. With Great Weapon Fighting and a weapon in two hands, each 1 or 2 on a damage die counts as 3: the result card's text and its damage roll show each die as rolled and, for a 1 or 2, that it counts as 3 (such as "d10 2→3").
- **Defeat.** A combatant at 0 HP is defeated. An opponent at 0 HP drops out of the fight. When every opponent is defeated, has fled or has surrendered (see Morale), the encounter's victory ending follows if it names one; otherwise the fight is over and the character explores on. The player character at 0 HP is defeated at once (no death saving throws): the adventure ends in the module's defeat ending, the character's sheet is saved at 0 HP and marked defeated, and it cannot start another adventure. After a victory the character keeps what it holds, is credited the XP it earned and rests to full health (see Earning XP and treasure).
- **Refused actions.** Acting out of turn, attacking an absent, friendly or defeated target, attacking with the action spent, using a feature it can't use now, or acting after the fight is over is refused with an engine-written reason. A refusal changes nothing and draws no dice.
- **Multiattack (#235).** A stat block's `multiattack` (2–4) is how many attacks the opponent makes on its turn. For each, a seeded die picks the target when more than one party combatant stands, as for any opponent, and a second die picks which of its attacks it makes when it has more than one; a combatant that falls is not picked again, and the turn's attacks stop when the fight ends. The narration says "(attack chosen by a die: 2)" and the result card shows the "attack die". A monster without Multiattack makes one attack, its first, and draws no attack die. Source: SRD 5.2's Multiattack, where a block names the attacks it may combine; here the die chooses among all of them.
- **Monster traits (#235).** The opponent AI uses every trait deterministically: no choice is left to the AI DM, and the only dice are the ones named here.
  - _Rampage_ (house, after the gnoll's trait): when an opponent with it drops a combatant to 0 HP with an attack on its turn, it makes one bonus attack at once, its target and attack chosen by dice as above. It takes the bonus action, so once a turn. The SRD's move of half its speed is omitted. While the Fighter is the only party combatant, a drop ends the fight, so Rampage cannot yet happen in play; the engine applies it with more party combatants.
  - _Nimble Escape_ (SRD 5.2, goblins; and the house Kobold): a bonus-action Disengage or Hide. Without positions there are no opportunity attacks to avoid, and hiding needs cover the game does not model, so the owner decided (#235) that it changes nothing in a fight. Fleeing (#237) did not change that: a fleeing monster leaves without provoking an attack, so a goblin's Disengage has nothing to avoid. The engine and the balance harness carry the trait, and a test checks that a fight with it plays exactly as one without. A stronger abstraction, disadvantage on the next attack against the goblin until its next turn, cost every goblin module about 20 points of the gate's weakest survival and was rejected.
- **Morale (#237, a house rule).** Every stat block gives a morale DC, or `"never"` for undead and mindless monsters, which never check. A side checks morale when its first combatant falls, and again when defeat or flight leaves it at half its starting numbers or fewer; each check is made once per fight, and when both come at once (a pair's first fall) the side checks once. Each combatant on it that is still in the fight, has a morale DC and is not already fleeing makes a Wisdom saving throw against that DC, in initiative order, with d20s from the session's seeded stream: "Goblin 2 checks morale as the first of its side falls: a Wisdom saving throw, 9 − 1 = 8 against DC 8. Success: it stands its ground." One that fails is fleeing: on its next turn it leaves the fight instead of acting ("Goblin 2 flees the fight"), unless it is incapacitated (paralysed). Until then it can be attacked, and one cut down first is defeated. A fled monster can't be attacked, leaves no body and takes what it carried with it (owner decision, 6 October 2026). It gives half its XP, rounded down, if it exchanged blows with the character first (it attacked, or was attacked, hit or miss), and none if it did not (owner decision, 7 October 2026): the award reads "Defeated Goblin 1 and Goblin 3; drove off Goblin 2". Its going can bring its side to half strength. Fleeing provokes no free attack: there are no opportunity attacks without positions, and the owner decided (#237) against adding one. The initiative table tags a monster Fleeing, then Fled, and the AI DM's scene reports it. The player character has no morale DC and never flees. The DCs are house numbers: 8 for goblin minions and warriors, kobolds, bandits and giant rats (a Wisdom 8 goblin fails about 40% of the time); 6 for the Goblin Boss, Wolf, Giant Spider, Bugbear Warrior and Ogre; 5 for the Hobgoblin Warrior and Gnoll Ravager; never for the Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul. The balance harness plays fleeing through the real runtime, so it counts in survival and XP, and each fight's record counts who fled; the one-hit-kill check still counts every ordinary enemy, whether or not it may flee.
- **Surrender (#238).** A module may author a `surrender` for an opponent that checks morale (never for an undead or mindless one): a description, topics as for a creature, and optional XP for sparing it. Such an opponent surrenders instead of fleeing, in the same way: a failed morale saving throw reads "Failure: it will surrender on its turn.", the initiative table tags it Surrendering, and on its next turn, unless it is paralysed, it yields instead of acting ("Goblin 2 throws down its arms and surrenders.") and is tagged Surrendered. Until then it can be attacked, and one cut down first is defeated. A surrendered opponent can't be attacked, its yielding counts toward its side's half strength, and the fight is won when no opponent is left fighting. It leaves no body. Once the fight is won it is a creature in the room, under its own name, with its description and its authored topics; the AI DM can ask it only about those, as with any creature. A topic may `give` items the opponent carries: answering it (after a passed check, when it has one) offers them ("Goblin 2 offers you the Stolen Ring 2."), and they lie in the room to take like a discovered item; a failed check offers nothing, and, like every check, is not tried again. Each is found once per character, as any treasure, coin or gear is, so a character that found it before is offered nothing. If the opponent is cut down instead, its body holds the same items; if it flees, they leave with it. It gives combat XP as a fled opponent does, half its XP if it exchanged blows with the character first and none otherwise, plus the module's XP for sparing it, all in the fight's award ("Defeated Goblin 1 and Goblin 3; spared Goblin 2"), earned once with it. Monsters in the bestiary carry no surrender: it belongs to an adventure. The balance harness plays surrender through the real runtime like fleeing, counts who surrendered in each fight, and never talks; the XP check counts, for an opponent that may surrender, the greater of its XP and half its XP plus its sparing XP.
- **Not used yet.** An opponent uses its stat block's first melee attack and its rider, if any, or with Multiattack (#235) several of its attacks, and its traits and defences; stat-block riders that need advantage (the Goblin Warrior's extra 1d4) and ranged attacks are not used.

**The AI Dungeon Master.** The engine is the only authority over dice, turn order, targets, hits, damage, hit points and endings. The AI DM may only call `look` and `get_character_status` to read the scene, and the action tools `move`, `sneak`, `ambush`, `react`, `examine`, `take`, `use_item`, `force_door`, `pick_lock`, `break_door`, `unlock`, `search`, `disarm`, `talk`, `equip`, `unequip`, `swap_weapon`, `drop`, `attack`, `light_attack`, `second_wind`, `action_surge` and `end_turn`. A check tool is called only when the player explicitly asks for that approach ("force the door"); words that name none ("open the door") get a question listing the offered approaches, and no roll. `talk` lists only the creatures' authored topics, a surrendered opponent's included (#238), and a check already tried is not offered again; calling it anyway gets the engine's refusal, never a reroll. Each action tool is offered only while the engine would accept it, and lists only what is visible and legal; the tools come from the same projection as the browser's action bar, so the AI DM is offered exactly the actions the bar shows enabled (`get_character_status` names each attack with its bonus and damage, and the arrows and bolts held, #230): `move` the room's exits, `examine` its features, visible items and carried items, `take` its visible items, `use_item` carried potions while drinking one would be accepted, `attack` the living opponents on the player's turn, and `light_attack` them while the extra attack is enabled. Looking, searching, reading, inspecting or opening something ("search the chest") is `examine`. "Catch my breath" or "second wind" calls `second_wind` only when it is offered; otherwise the AI says it is not available. One message allows one action. While the character is paralysed (#234) only `end_turn` is offered, and any other action tool gets the engine's refusal. Opponents are listed by id and name; when the player's words fit exactly one, by its name or by an ordinal matching the number in its name ("attack the second goblin" is Goblin Minion 2 when Goblin Minions 1 and 2 are listed), the AI calls `attack` with it; it never counts positions in a list. When they name none or fit several ("attack the goblin" with two goblins) it asks which one and calls nothing. It never guesses a target. No tool takes a roll, damage, advantage, extra-action, discovery, item or outcome argument; any extra argument is refused before the engine is reached. The engine writes the reply to every action it resolves or refuses, so the AI cannot narrate a different roll, discovery, item or result, and a reply without a tool call changes nothing. Taking an item that is not visible (one that does not exist, or one still hidden) is refused by the engine.

## Sneaking in, surprise, sneaking past and lurking monsters (#301–#303)

**Stealth** is a Dexterity skill anyone can roll; a class's skill choices give proficiency in it (the Fighter's list does not include it; the Rogue's does, and a fresh Rogue has Expertise in it, #306). Armour whose SRD 5.2 entry gives disadvantage on Stealth (chain mail and plate) gives the check disadvantage.

**Passive Perception.** Every stat block gives its passive Perception: in the bestiary (format 8) the SRD 5.2 value (for example Wolf 15, Giant Rat 12, Goblin Warrior 9, Zombie 8), and for a house stat block, such as the Gnoll Ravager, 10 + its Wisdom modifier. A stat block authored inline in a module (module format 21) must give its own; the validator refuses one without it.

**Sneaking in.** Beside each exit to a room where a fight not yet won waits, the browser offers **Sneak** next to **Go**, and the AI DM a `sneak` tool listing only those rooms; sneaking anywhere else is refused ("No fight waits in the Alcove: there is no one to sneak up on."). Sneaking takes the same way as going in (a door must be open, a trap on the way springs first), then rolls a Stealth check whose DC is the highest passive Perception among the fight's opponents (the first of them on a tie). Meeting it leaves the character **unseen** in the room (#302): the fight has not begun, and the character may spring an ambush or slip past. Missing it starts the fight as usual. The engine alone decides: the AI DM has no tool or parameter that declares surprise or an ambush.

**Ambush and surprise (SRD 5.2).** Unseen, the browser offers **Ambush** and the AI DM an `ambush` tool (called when the player asks to attack or ambush the opponents); it starts the fight with every opponent surprised. A surprised combatant rolls initiative with disadvantage: two d20s, the lower kept. Nothing else changes; the 2024 rules have no surprise round. The sneak card shows the Stealth roll (and any armour disadvantage), the passive Perception it beat or missed and whose it is, and who has not noticed the character; the ambush card names who is surprised, the initiative table tags each surprised opponent, and the initiative breakdown shows both dice.

**Sneaking past (#302).** Unseen, the character may go on through any open way out of the room (a **Go** button, or the AI DM's `move`: "I slip past them to the north door"), back the way it came included, or leave the adventure from an exit room. The fight is then **bypassed**: unresolved and still there, its opponents keep what they carry, and coming back through its room meets it again. While unseen nothing else in the room can be done, as it would give the character away: examining features or items, taking, doors, searching, disarming, talking, gear changes and trading are refused ("Not while you are sneaking past Giant Rat: you would be seen."); only drinking a potion is allowed. After a failed sneak the fight is on, and moving is refused as in any fight.

**Remembered.** The Stealth check is remembered with the fight it was made against and never rerolled: once made, sneaking up on the same fight again is refused before any die is drawn ("going in again starts it"), so coming back to a bypassed fight starts it. Only an encounter with `sneakAgain: true` offers a fresh sneak after the character has slipped past it, with a new Stealth check that replaces the old one.

**XP for slipping past.** A bypassed fight gives no XP by default. An encounter may author `bypassXp`, credited on surviving completion under the encounter's own award (`adventure/encounter/id`) when the fight was slipped past and never won: an encounter is credited once, won or slipped past, so a character that slipped past a fight earns nothing more for it by winning it in a later adventure, and one that slips past and then wins it in the same adventure earns the fight's XP alone. The balance harness's _stealth-first_ style sneaks and slips past, and the gate reports it; the gate's XP limit counts the bypass XP (see Balance harness and Balance gate).

**Lurking monsters (#303).** A module may mark an encounter `lurking: true`: its opponents lie in wait. The first time the character comes into its room (by going in, sneaking in, or starting there), the least stealthy of them (the lowest Stealth bonus, the first of them on a tie) rolls a Stealth check for them all against the character's passive Perception. Meeting it hides them: the character is **surprised** and rolls initiative with disadvantage. Missing it, the character spots them and the fight opens normally. One roll for the group, by its weakest member, mirrors sneaking in, where the character's one roll meets the opponents' best passive Perception. The engine alone decides: the AI DM has no tool or parameter that declares or removes surprise, and the system prompt says so.

- **Stealth bonus.** A stat block may give `stealth`, its Stealth bonus. The bestiary (format 9) gives SRD 5.2's where it lists one: Goblin Minion, Goblin Warrior, Goblin Boss and Bugbear Warrior +6, Giant Spider +7, Wolf and Dire Wolf +4. The house Kobold has +4, its Dexterity modifier (+2) and proficiency bonus (+2), as a Nimble Escape sneak like the goblins. Every other stat block, the house Gnoll Ravager included, rolls its Dexterity modifier. An inline stat block (module format 23) may give its own.
- **Passive Perception.** The character's is 10 + its Wisdom modifier, + its proficiency bonus if it is proficient in Perception (twice with Expertise, #306), + 5 if a rule gives it advantage on Perception checks or − 5 for disadvantage; advantage and disadvantage together cancel, adding nothing (SRD 5.2). No rule in the game gives either yet. A level-1 Fighter with Wisdom 10 and Perception has 12.
- **The card.** "Giant Rat is lying in wait in the Rat-Gnawed Cellar. Giant Rat's Stealth check: d20 10 + 4 = 14 against your passive Perception 12 (10 + 0 Wisdom + 2 proficiency). Success: you did not notice it, and you are surprised and roll initiative with disadvantage." On a failure: "Failure: you spot it, and you are not surprised." The roll's chip shows the lurker's die against the passive Perception, and the initiative table tags the character Surprised, with both initiative dice.
- **Remembered.** The roll is remembered with its fight and never rerolled: coming back into the room (after slipping past the fight, #302) reuses it and draws no die. Hidden lurkers always start the fight, so only a roll the character won is ever reused.
- **Sneaking into a lurking room.** Both checks are made as the character comes in, its Stealth first, then the lurkers'. They combine as each side's awareness of the other: _the character's sneak succeeds and the lurkers are spotted_: the character is unseen (#302) and may ambush or slip past; _the sneak fails and the lurkers are hidden_: the fight begins and only the character is surprised; _both succeed_: neither side has noticed the other, so they stumble on each other and the fight begins with everyone surprised (2024 rules: surprise is per combatant, each caught unaware rolling with disadvantage), and there is no ambushing or slipping past; _both fail_: the fight opens normally. The sneak card says when the lurkers were hidden too.
- **Balance.** The harness and the gate play lurking through the real runtime, so it counts in survival; each run records how many fights surprised the character. The Coldwater Weirgate's Brindle and drowned guildsmen lurk (#443); the fixture _The Lurking Tunnels_ (`tests/fixtures/modules.mjs`) is the rat tunnels with the Giant Rat lurking (Stealth +4), and the gate plays it. Lurking took that fixture's weakest survival from 64.5% to 56.0%; the rat tunnels fail the gate either way.

## Reaction rolls (#304, a house rule)

Reaction rolls are a house rule on top of 5e: SRD 5.2 leaves how a creature greets the characters to the DM.

**Reaction-eligible.** A module marks an encounter reaction-eligible by giving it a `reaction`; the default is off. It may mark single opponents `reacts: true`: then only they react (and the rest follow their lead), and otherwise every opponent does. Mindless opponents never react: an Undead, or any stat block whose morale is `"never"`, the bestiary's mark for undead and mindless monsters. The validator refuses a reaction-eligible encounter with any mindless opponent, even one that would only follow another's lead.

**When it is rolled.** When a reaction-eligible fight would begin with no one surprised: on going into its room (or starting there), after any sneak (#301) or lurking (#303) roll. So it is rolled on going in, after a failed sneak (the opponents noticed the character), when lurkers were spotted, and on coming back to a fight slipped past (#302). It is not rolled while the character is unseen (it has not been noticed), on an ambush (the character chose to attack), when lurkers hid from the character (they are springing their own attack), or when a sneak and hidden lurkers meet and everyone is surprised.

**The roll.** 2d6 + the character's Charisma modifier, looked up in the reaction table (`REACTION_TABLE` in `src/reaction-5e.ts`, the one place to retune it):

| 2d6 + Charisma | Band        |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| 2 or less      | Hostile     |
| 3–5            | Unfriendly  |
| 6–8            | Uncertain   |
| 9–11           | Indifferent |
| 12 or more     | Friendly    |

With Charisma −1 a friendly band is out of reach; with +3 a hostile one is.

**Options.** A hostile band always fights: the fight begins at once. Every other band offers only the options its module authors, each band at least one, from **attack** (the fight begins, no one surprised) and **let pass** (the encounter ends peacefully and the character goes on), and **parley**, **toll** and **trade** (#305, below). Until the character chooses, the fight has not begun and nothing else can be done ("Giant Rat is waiting to see what you do: you may attack or pass peacefully."). An option the band doesn't offer is refused ("That is not offered: you may attack.").

**Let pass.** The encounter is settled: its room is free to explore, no fight waits there on coming back, and sneaking into it is not offered. The opponents keep what they carry: there are no bodies to search. An encounter may author `peacefulXp` (1–10,000) in its reaction, credited on surviving completion under the encounter's own award (`adventure/encounter/id`), so an encounter is credited once, won, slipped past or ended peacefully ("Parted peacefully with Giant Rat").

**Remembered.** The roll is remembered with its fight and never rerolled: refusals and projections draw no die, and should the fight be met again the roll is reused, drawing none.

**The card.** "Giant Rat sees you. Reaction roll: 2d6 (3 + 4) − 1 Charisma = 6: uncertain. The rat freezes, whiskers twitching. You may attack or pass peacefully." The band's authored words follow the roll, and the roll's chip shows the two d6s, the Charisma modifier and the band. The action bar offers one button per option: **Attack** and **Pass peacefully**.

**The AI DM.** The engine alone rolls the reaction and decides its band and options; the AI DM narrates the band from the engine's result. While the opponents react, `react` is its only action tool, listing only the band's options; it picks the one the player's words name ("I attack anyway" is attack). No tool takes a band, any extra argument is refused, and an option not offered gets the engine's refusal. Five AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite reactions`, on _The Wary Cellar_, `adventures/eval/wary-cellar.json`) cover going in, attacking anyway, passing peacefully, an option not offered and a player asking to change the band.

**Balance.** The harness has a reaction policy: _attack_, the default, attacks whenever the band offers it; _peaceful_ takes a peaceful option whenever one is offered. Each takes the other when its own is not offered. The gate judges the _attack_ runs (the worst case for survival) and, for a module with a reaction-eligible encounter, reports both policies beside its checks; its XP limit counts peaceful XP. No shipped module is reaction-eligible yet, so no verdict changed. The fixture _The Wary Tunnels_ (`tests/fixtures/modules.mjs`) authors every band, and the gate plays it: always attacking, the weakest character survives 91.5% of 200 runs; taking the peaceful option, 96.5%.

## Parley, tolls and trade (#305, a house rule)

Parley, tolls and trade extend the reaction-roll house rule: SRD 5.2 has the Persuasion, Deception and Intimidation skills, but how they move a creature's attitude is the DM's call, and here it is the module's. A reaction may author each of them once, and a band offers one only by naming it among its options. Every band must still offer attack or let pass, so the character can always end the reaction.

**Deception.** The skill list gains Deception (Charisma), SRD 5.2's skill for lies and bluffs. No Fighter can choose it: the Fighter's skill list doesn't have it, so a Fighter rolls it with its Charisma modifier alone.

**Parley.** `parley` is `{ "approaches": [...], "bands": {...} }`: one to three approaches, each `{ "skill", "dc" }` with a different one of `persuasion`, `deception` and `intimidation` and a DC from 5 to 30, and the graded check's bands (#281), each with optional `text` and one of a `shift` (−4 to 4, not 0: that many bands up toward friendly or down toward hostile, stopping at either end) or an `outcome`: `let-pass` (the encounter ends peacefully, for its peaceful XP), `fight` (the fight begins, no one surprised) or `surprise-attack` (only on `failure-by-5`: the opponents attack, and the character is surprised and rolls initiative with disadvantage). A failure or success by 5 or more left out counts as plain failure or success; a band left out, or with only words, leaves the reaction where it was. A shift to hostile begins the fight, since hostile always fights. A parley is made once per encounter and remembered with it: the band it leaves the reaction in stands, its options exclude the parley, and asking again is refused ("You have already parleyed: you may …"), never rerolled. Parley approaches have no circumstances (#284) and no retry. The check goes through the same rolls as any other (a d20 from the session's seeded stream, the check card with its band, the band's words), and the balance harness's always-fail and always-succeed check policies (#285) apply to it.

**Toll.** `toll` is `{ "coins": { "gp", "sp", "cp" }, "text"? }`: what the opponents let the character pass for, and their words as it is paid. Paying it takes the price from the purse and ends the encounter peacefully, like let pass, for its peaceful XP. With too little coin it is refused ("The toll is 5 sp, and you have 0 cp.") and its button is disabled ("Too little coin"). The coin is spent inside the adventure, so it rolls back with it: settling a victory or an escape keeps the loss, and a defeat or abandonment restores it. Nothing lowers or waives a toll.

**Trade.** `trade` is a merchant's stock and minutes (#210, with every merchant rule: catalogue gear only, no rare gear, uncommon gear only for level 3 and up, 1–60 minutes). While the band holds and offers trade, the reacting opponents are a merchant: they appear among the room's creatures with their wares and the prices they pay, and buying, selling gear and selling gems and art objects work as with any merchant. Trading ends nothing: the character still answers the reaction, and once it does (or a band without trade holds) the trade is over. Trade is open, not chosen, so it is no button and no `react` option.

**The card.** "Bandit sees you. Reaction roll: 2d6 (1 + 1) + 3 Charisma = 5: unfriendly. "Five silver, or blood." You may attack, parley (Persuasion DC 12, Deception DC 14, Intimidation DC 13) or pay the toll (5 sp)." A parley's card shows its check and band, the band's words, then what it did: "Bandit is now uncertain (was unfriendly). You may attack or pay the toll (5 sp).", "Bandit lets you pass: the encounter ends peacefully.", "Bandit turns hostile and attacks." or "Bandit attacks before you are ready: you are surprised and roll initiative with disadvantage." A toll's card: "You pay the toll of 5 sp. She bites a coin and waves you on. Bandit lets you pass: the encounter ends peacefully. Purse: 1 gp 5 sp." The action bar has one **Parley** button per approach, its skill and DC under it ("Deception DC 14"; the button is named in full, "Parley with Deception DC 14"), and **Pay the toll (5 sp)**; trade's Buy and Sell sit on the wares and on "You carry" as with any merchant.

**The AI DM.** `react` lists only the band's options and takes a parley's approach, listing the offered ones; it picks the approach the player's words describe: an honest appeal is Persuasion, a lie or bluff ("I tell them we're from the guild") Deception, and a threat Intimidation. `trade` is offered while the band offers trade. No tool takes a band, a price or a retry: any extra argument is refused, an option not offered gets the engine's refusal ("That is not offered: you may …"), and the system prompt says nothing lowers or waives a toll. Six AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite parley`, on _The Bandit's Toll_, `adventures/eval/bandit-toll.json`) cover the three approaches, paying the toll, asking for it to be waived and asking to change the band.

**Balance.** The harness's _peaceful_ reaction policy takes let pass, then a toll it can afford (one with too little coin is not offered), then a parley with the character's best offered approach (its skill bonus furthest above, or least below, the approach's DC; the first on a tie), and attacks only when none is offered; _attack_ is unchanged. The harness never trades. No shipped module authors a parley, toll or trade yet, so no verdict changed. The fixture _The Bandit's Toll_ (`tests/fixtures/modules.mjs`) authors all three.

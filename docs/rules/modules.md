# Adventure modules

## Adventure modules

A 5e adventure module is a JSON file in format version 43 (`src/adventure-5e.ts`). It declares its recommended levels (1–5) and a difficulty (easy, medium or hard); its rooms, each with its map cell `at`, `[x, y]` from −50 to 50, and an optional `floor` from −9 to 9 (#455, see The map), features (each with an optional discovery and an optional check, #281), items (the SRD 5.2 Potion of Healing or Potion of Greater Healing, or a key, optionally hidden in one of the room's features or carried by an opponent of its fight; or treasure, coin, gear, a spell scroll or a wand, which must be, with treasure's catalogue gem or art object, coin's gold, silver and copper pieces, gear's catalogue item, a scroll's `spell` and a wand's catalogue `wand`, #346), creatures to talk to (each with topics, a topic optionally with a check and failure words, and optionally a merchant's stock of catalogue gear and minutes per trade) and an optional encounter; the two-way passages between rooms, each with an optional door (stuck, with a force check; or locked, with any of a key, a pick check and a break check) and an optional trap (its find, `{ "dc" }` only, as the search chooses Perception or Investigation (#309); its disarm check, trigger text, saving throw, damage of an SRD 5.2 type and defeat ending); a check is a Fighter skill or an ability with a DC from 5 to 30, and a Dexterity check may name `"tool": "thieves-tools"` (#309), which only a character carrying thieves' tools can make; a door's pick must be such a check, and only a pick or a trap's disarm may name the tool; a check may instead offer `approaches` (#283): 2–4 of those, each with a different skill, ability or tool; and any check but a trap's find may have `bands` (#281): any of `failure-by-5`, `failure`, `success` and `success-by-5`, each with optional `text` and up to six `effects`, each `{ "type": "discovery", "feature" }`, `{ "type": "item", "item" }` or `{ "type": "damage", "dice", "sides", "modifier", "damageType", "defeatEndingId" }`; each encounter with each opponent's `monster`, the id of a bestiary monster, with an optional `name` and `description` replacing the monster's own, or else its name, description and a one-off SRD 5.2 stat block inline (size, type, AC, average hit points and formula, ability scores, challenge rating, XP, melee attacks, each with an optional rider, its morale, and the optional lists a bestiary stat block may have), an optional `icon` (#377: the id of an icon in `src/icons-5e.ts`, shown beside its name in place of its monster's, or of crossed swords for an inline stat block), an optional `boss: true`, which exempts it from the balance gate's one-hit-kill cap, and an optional `surrender` (#238: a description, topics, each optionally with `gives`, the ids of items the opponent carries, and optional `xp`); its defeat ending and an optional victory ending, an optional `bypassXp` (#302: 1–10,000 XP for slipping past it unfought) an optional `sneakAgain: true` (#302: a fresh sneak after slipping past), an optional `lurking: true` (#303: its opponents lie in wait) and an optional `reaction` (#304: `bands`, the `options` and optional `text` of each of `unfriendly`, `uncertain`, `indifferent` and `friendly`, and an optional `peacefulXp`; #305: an optional `parley`, `toll` and `trade`, see Parley, tolls and trade), and each opponent may be marked `reacts: true`; and its endings. A passage may be `hidden: true` (#282) or `vertical` (#455: `stairs`, `ladder` or `shaft`), and a band's effects may also be `{ "type": "open", "passage" }` or `{ "type": "close", "passage" }`. A room may be a `restSite: true` (#335), where a long rest may be taken, and the module may have one `wanderingEncounter` (#335): `{ "encounterId", "chance" }`, an encounter placed in no room and the chance, 1–100 on a d100, that it interrupts each rest (see Wandering encounters and interrupted rests). A module may declare `regions` (#395): 1–10 parts of it, such as an upper and a lower level, each `{ "id", "name" }`; then every room names its `region` (see Regions and moves). A module may declare `flags` (#397): 1–30 ids; a room, a feature and an item may name the flag it `sets`, an encounter may have `sets`, `{ "won", "bypassed", "peaceful", "fled" }`, at least one naming a flag, a band's effects may also be `{ "type": "set", "flag" }`, and a passage may have `openWhen` and `closeWhen`, each a flag (see Flags); an encounter may have one of `presentWhile`, `presentUntil`, each a flag, and `movesTo`, `{ "roomId", "when" }` (#398, see Encounters that appear, vanish or move with flags); a feature may have `changed`, `{ "when", "description" }`, and a topic `offeredWhile` and `offeredUntil`, each a flag (#399, see Features and topics that change with flags); and a feature may have `uses` (#400): up to four, each `{ "item", "text", "effects" }` with an optional `"consumes": true` (see Using an item on a feature); and a feature may have a `fightAction` (#402): `{ "once", "check", "bands" }` (see Fight actions); and a module may declare an `alarm` (#401): `{ "flag", "text" }` with optional `fights`, 1–10 `{ "region", "round" }`, and a check's band may be `"noisy": true` (see Alarms); and a module may declare `routes` (#407): 1–6, each `{ "id", "name", "rooms" }`, its rooms 1–20 of the module's, the rooms the route must pass in order (see Balance gate); and a long module's room may be `avoidable: true` (#408), left out of its region's sweep (see Balance gate). An item may be a `tool` (#284). Any approach but a trap's find may have `advantage` and `disadvantage` (#284), each 1–4 circumstances: `{ "type": "holds", "item", "name" }`, `{ "type": "discovered", "feature", "name" }`, `{ "type": "won", "encounter", "name" }` or `{ "type": "flag", "flag", "name" }` (#397), each with optional `"not": true`; and a check may have a `retry`: `{ "cost": { "type": "damage", "dice", "sides", "modifier", "damageType", "defeatEndingId" } }`, `{ "cost": { "type": "item", "item" } }` or `{ "after": <circumstance> }`. The validator rejects:

- unknown room, encounter, feature and ending references, and an unknown item kind;
- an opponent naming a monster that is not in the bestiary (the message names the module, the opponent and the id), and an opponent with both a bestiary monster and an inline stat block;
- a room that cannot be reached from the start room, and an ending that no encounter, trap or check's damage names;
- a check's band that names an unknown feature as a discovery, a feature without a discovery, an unknown item, an item that is not hidden in a feature, or an ending that is not a defeat; a band no character of the module's highest level can roll at its DC (its total is a natural 1 at −4 up to a natural 20 at +5, plus the proficiency bonus for a skill), naming the check, the band, the total it needs and the range; `bands` with no band, and a band with neither words nor effects; fewer than two or more than four `approaches`, two with the same skill or ability, and `approaches` beside a skill, ability or DC; and an item hidden in a feature with a check that no check's item effect reveals;
- a check, trap or hidden passage that guards the only route to an essential room (one whose fight ends the adventure in victory): such a room must be reachable without a check or a trap, where a locked door counts as open when its key can itself be reached that way (and is not hidden in a feature with a check), or through a check every band of which leaves a way forward (#282: a door's success opens it, and any band's `open` effects open hidden passages; a band no character can roll doesn't count). A passage some band can close is never counted on, and a close that can cut off an essential room or every exit is rejected, naming the check, the passage and the room. A check only thieves' tools can make is never counted on, as the character may carry none (#309): a door only they open is named as such, and a route only they make sure is rejected as needing thieves' tools. The same holds for reaching some exit;
- a circumstance naming an unknown item, feature or encounter, an item that is coin or gear (never held), or a feature without a discovery; a circumstance without a name or of an unknown type; a retry with both or neither of `cost` and `after`, an `after` with `not` (it could never change back), a cost naming an unknown item or one that isn't a tool, or a tool the check's own advantage needs held (it is used up before the roll), and a damage cost whose ending is not a defeat (a cost's ending counts as reached);
- an `open` or `close` naming an unknown passage, an `open` of a passage that isn't hidden, and a hidden passage that no band opens;
- a stuck door with anything but a force check, a locked door with a force check or with neither key, pick nor break, a pick that is not a Dexterity check with thieves' tools, thieves' tools on any check but a pick or a disarm, a key that is not an item of kind key, an unknown skill or ability, and a topic with a check but no failure words (or the reverse);
- an encounter whose victory or defeat ending names the wrong kind, an encounter in no room (but a wandering encounter) or in several, and a module without a victory ending; an encounter (#398) with more than one of `presentWhile`, `presentUntil` and `movesTo`, moving to an unknown room or its own, naming an unknown flag, or ending the adventure in victory; a wandering encounter with any of them; and a room that two encounters may hold;
- a `restSite` that isn't `true`; a `wanderingEncounter` with other keys, naming an unknown encounter, with a chance that isn't a whole number from 1 to 100, or naming an encounter placed in a room, one with a victory ending, `bypassXp`, `sneakAgain`, `lurking` or a reaction, or one with an opponent that can surrender (#335);
- more than 30 encounters, wandering ones included (D5, #403); a region's wandering table (#403) with keys other than `chance`, `every` and `entries`, a chance that isn't a whole number from 1 to 100 or is less than its number of entries, an `every` outside 1–20, no entries or more than 6, an entry without an `omen` or with keys other than `omen` and `encounterId`, or an encounter that couldn't be the `wanderingEncounter` (above); an encounter in two entries; and a module with both a `wanderingEncounter` and a region table;
- a room without a cell, a cell that isn't two whole numbers from −50 to 50, or a floor that isn't a whole number from −9 to 9; and a layout that breaks the map's rules (#455, see The map): two rooms on one cell of a floor, a passage on one floor that isn't straight or runs through a room, two passages on a floor that cross, a passage between floors that isn't `vertical` or skips a floor, and a `vertical` passage between rooms on one floor or of an unknown kind. Each message names the rooms, passages and cells;
- a `long` that isn't `true`, and a long module (#404) with fewer than two regions or a region without a rest site;
- routes (#407) other than a list of 1–6, a route with keys other than `id`, `name` and `rooms`, two routes with the same id, a route with no rooms or more than 20, and a route naming an unknown room or one room twice;
- an `avoidable` (#408) that isn't `true`, an avoidable room in a module that isn't long (only a long module is swept), one a declared route names (D8: an avoidable room must lie off every declared route, so the character can leave it unvisited) and one whose fight wins the module's victory; and a long module with a room from which no exit can be reached by passages nothing can close (D9: every region has an exit the character can reach from each of its rooms, so a hurt character can leave with what it found). A passage a flag's `closeWhen`, a band's or a use's close can shut is never counted on, even if the character came in by it; any other passage counts, as one the character has come through stays open. A long module with no exit at all is refused the same way;
- a region with keys other than `id`, `name` and `wandering`, two regions with the same id, a room naming an unknown region, a declared region no room names, and a module where some rooms name a region and others don't (#395);
- flags (#397) given twice; a flag named anywhere (a `sets`, a `set` effect, an `openWhen` or `closeWhen`, a circumstance) that the module doesn't declare; a declared flag nothing sets, and one nothing uses (no passage, circumstance, feature's `changed` or topic's offer names it), as a declared region no room names is refused; an encounter's `sets` naming no outcome or one but `won`, `bypassed`, `peaceful` and `fled`; a hidden passage with `openWhen` (a band opens a hidden passage, a flag one there to see), and a passage that opens and closes on the same flag; a topic offered while and until the same flag, and one that `gives` (#399) but can be withdrawn (`offeredUntil`) or waits on a flag not set for sure; a use (#400) naming an unknown item, an item the character never carries to use (coin, gear, a scroll or a wand), treasure used up, the same item twice on one feature, no effects, or an effect as a band's would be refused. A hidden passage must be opened by a band or a use. A way a use opens counts toward reaching an essential room or an exit only once the feature's room and the item are reached for sure: an item in plain sight or hidden in a feature with no check (not one a band may never reveal, nor one an opponent carries), which nothing else may use up first. A use that closes a way is a closer like a band's, refused when it can cut off an essential room or every exit, naming the use. Reachability extends to flags: as flags only turn on, the rooms reached and the flags set for sure are grown together until neither changes, and the rules above are checked over them. A flag is set for sure by entering a room reached, examining its features, taking its items (but those hidden in a feature with a check or carried by an opponent, whose body may never be searched) and ending its fight, and by a check reached for sure every band of which sets it. A fight's flag is sure only when every final outcome the fight can reach sets it: its `won` flag, and its `peaceful` one too when it is reaction-eligible (#304), as it may end peacefully and never be won; slipping past it (#302) and fleeing it (#396) leave it unresolved, met again on coming back, so neither counts, and neither of their flags is ever counted on. So an essential room, or every exit, reachable only through a way a flag opens that only some bands of a check set is rejected, naming the passage and its flag, and a passage a flag closes is never counted on: one that can cut off an essential room or every exit is rejected, naming the flag;
- a fight action (#402) whose `once` is not `fight` or `adventure`, whose check's approaches name a tool, a movement or circumstances, whose bands author no effect, a band with more than two effects, two of one type or a removal beside another effect, a condition other than prone, poisoned or paralysed, prone for other than 1 turn, damage outside a band's damage ranges; a fight action in a room where no fight is ever fought (no encounter of its own, none flags move there, and no wandering encounter at a rest site); and one that can remove an opponent but is used once per fight, or in a room where a boss (`boss: true`) may be fought (D4: a boss is never removed), or where an opponent may be fought that carries a door's key or an item a use (#400) needs (removed, it would be lost, stranding the character as a lost key does, #331; its other loot may be lost);
- an alarm (#401) naming a flag the module doesn't declare, a region it doesn't declare or one twice, or a round outside 1–10; anything else (a `sets`, a `set` effect, a use) setting the alarm's flag; and a band marked `noisy` in a module without an alarm, or `noisy` other than true.
- an item hidden in a feature with neither a discovery nor a check to reveal it;
- a reaction (#304) with a band missing or with no authored option, a hostile band (it always fights), an option that is not `attack`, `let-pass`, `parley`, `toll` or `trade` or is given twice, a band offering neither `attack` nor `let-pass` (#305: the character could never end the reaction), a band offering a parley, toll or trade its reaction doesn't author and one authored that no band offers, a parley with no approach, more than three, one that isn't Persuasion, Deception or Intimidation or one given twice, a parley band with neither text, shift nor outcome, with both a shift and an outcome, a shift of 0 or beyond ±4, an unknown outcome or `surprise-attack` on any band but `failure-by-5`, a toll without at least one coin, and trade stock that breaks a merchant's rules (below), a reaction-eligible encounter whose fight ends the adventure, a mindless opponent anywhere in a reaction-eligible encounter (an Undead, or a stat block whose morale is `"never"`), and an opponent marked `reacts` in an encounter with no reaction;
- a surrender on an undead or mindless opponent (its morale is `"never"`), on an opponent whose fight ends the adventure, a topic that gives an item that doesn't exist or that the opponent doesn't carry, an item given by two topics, and `gives` on a topic of anything but a surrender;
- treasure, coin or gear that is not hidden in a feature or carried by an opponent, coin without an amount of at least one piece, an amount on anything but coin, gear that names no catalogue item, a catalogue item on anything but gear, treasure that names no catalogue gem or art object, and a gem or art object on anything but treasure;
- loot carried by a bestiary opponent that its treasure type couldn't produce (see Monster loot);
- findable treasure worth more than the budget for the module's maximum recommended level, naming the module, the total and the excess, and an item whose tier is not allowed at that level, naming the module, the item and its tier (see Treasure);
- a second magic item, a magic item in a module whose maximum recommended level is below 3, and a magic item that needs attunement, naming the module and the item (#345, see Magic items; a wand is one, #346);
- a spell scroll without a spell, or of a cantrip, a reaction spell or a spell the game doesn't know, a spell on anything but a scroll, a wand that names no catalogue wand, and a wand on anything but a wand (#346);
- a merchant stock entry that is not a catalogue item, a magic item (#345), an item stocked twice, rare gear, uncommon gear in a module for levels below 3, trade minutes outside 1–60, and two merchants in one room;
- a passage from a room to itself, or two passages between the same rooms;
- an id that is a catalogue item's (such as `longsword`), since those name the character's own gear;
- two features, items, doors, traps, creatures or topics (a surrender's included) with the same id, and two features in one room, two opponents in one encounter, two doors or two topics of one creature with the same name (ignoring case), since the player names them.

A module in another format version is refused with a message naming the file.

The bestiary (`adventures/5e/bestiary.json`, `src/bestiary-5e.ts`) is a JSON file in format version 10 holding the monsters adventure modules fight, each with an id, a default description, its `levelBand` (the character levels it suits, `min` to `max`, 1–20), its `treasureType` (see Monster loot, #240) and its stat block, which carries its XP and its `morale`: a DC from 1 to 30, or `"never"` (see Morale). The table below lists them. Every shipped module names its monsters by id, and many opponents take the module's own names, such as the Abandoned Delve's skeletons and the Thornwood Lodge's Baiting Bear. A stat block may list `traits` (Pack Tactics, Undead Fortitude, Nimble Escape and Rampage), give `multiattack` (2–4 attacks a turn), its `stealth` bonus (#303, −5 to 20; its Dexterity modifier without one), `saveProficiencies` (abilities), `damageResistances`, `damageVulnerabilities` and `damageImmunities` (SRD 5.2 damage types) and `conditionImmunities` (poisoned, prone or paralysed, or exhaustion, #340, which only makes it succeed on Sleep's saves; the Zombie, Skeleton and Ghoul are immune to it, as SRD 5.2 gives them), and give an attack a `rider`: extra `damage` and a `condition` (poisoned, prone or paralysed) with an optional `save` (ability and DC 5–30); poisoned and paralysed also give their `turns` (1–10) and may set `repeatSave`, while prone takes neither, as it always ends when the target gets up. The Giant Spider's poison and the Wolf's knockdown are derived house riders: the SRD 5.2 blocks differ in their numbers. The Ghoul's paralysis (#234) is SRD 5.2's. Its validator rejects a missing or unknown treasure type, a missing or malformed morale, an Undead with a morale DC, a duplicate monster id, a missing level band or one whose maximum is below its minimum, a `multiattack` outside 2–4, a malformed stat block, a damage type outside SRD 5.2, an unknown trait, ability or condition in those lists or one listed twice, a damage type in more than one of the three damage lists, a rider with neither damage nor a condition, an unknown condition, a repeat save without a save, and turns on prone or missing from poisoned or paralysed, and a bestiary in another format version is refused with a message naming the file.

Four modules ship, one for each level band of the four-module set (`docs/four-module-plan.md`), and together they carry a new character from level 1 to level 5; engine tests play the fixtures in `tests/fixtures/` (#251). `adventures/5e/abandoned-delve.json`, _The Abandoned Delve_ (#136), is a long module of sixteen rooms for level 2, declared Hard since its expansion (#442; ten rooms and Medium before, Hard until #252): the first real module, whose premise, map and encounters the owner approved on #136, and whose expansion the owner approved on #441. Its two regions, the Keep Cellars and the Crypts, each have a rest site (the Porter's Lodge, the Shrine) and an exit (the gate, the shaft). Its routes are the shrine stair (one SRD 5.2 Zombie, AC 8, 15 HP, Slam +3 for 1d8 + 1 bludgeoning, the dead guard, whose purse lies under the guard post's table, #240; then a loose-step trap, find Perception DC 13, disarm Dexterity DC 12, Dexterity save DC 12 against 2d6 bludgeoning) and the old well, where the porter's Coil of Rope used on the Well Head opens a way down, sets `goblin-freed` and unlocks two of the goblin's topics; that route also meets Mags Corrow, an SRD 5.2 Bandit, in the ossuary. The Cellars sweep adds a Wolf in the fallen kitchen. Avoidable are two Skeletons (AC 14, 13 HP, Shortsword +5 for 1d6 + 3 piercing) in the barracks, a Giant Spider (AC 14, 26 HP, Bite +5 for 1d8 + 3 piercing plus 1d6 poison, and poisoned on a failed DC 11 Constitution save) in the web crypt, and, behind a bronze door (key from the shrine altar, pick Dexterity DC 15, or break Athletics DC 17, noisy on a failure by 5), the vault's Ghoul boss (AC 12, 22 HP, Claw +4 for 1d4 + 2 slashing, paralysing on a failed DC 10 Constitution save), avoidable since the gate held the Crypts sweep with it to 68.5% (the proposal's fallback). A monster without Multiattack uses only its first attack, so the Ghoul claws (#234); its Bite, with the bite's necrotic damage, and its Multiattack are left out (see the bestiary's omissions). The vault's Coffer Stack is a fight action (Athletics or Strength DC 13, 2d6 bludgeoning, prone on a success by 5). The alarm `delve-roused` (a Crypts fight past round 4, the noisy door or sarcophagus bands, a failed sneak by 5) moves Mags to the shaft bottom; nothing closes. The lord's sarcophagus opens by Athletics DC 14 or Investigation DC 13, the latter at advantage with the goblin's directions. A dead delver (a Zombie) may come on any rest, 25%, once. It also has a stuck storeroom door (Athletics DC 12) with a shortsword and a shield in its sealed barrel (#209; a longsword until #239), two Potions of Healing and a Spell Scroll of Cure Wounds, and 590 gp 7 sp of its 600 gp budget. Climbing out with loot pays 600 XP. `adventures/5e/thornwood-lodge.json`, _The Thornwood Lodge_ (#291), is a long module of sixteen rooms for levels 4–5, declared Hard since its expansion (#445; six rooms before): the increment 14 level 4–5 module, whose premise, map, monsters, checks and treasure the owner approved on #291, and whose expansion the owner approved on #444. Its two regions, the Lodge and the Wolfstone, each have a rest site (the Falconer's Mews, the Sheepfold), an exit (the forest gate, the start; the drovers' camp) and a wandering table (15% every 4 moves: a Loose Mastiff, an SRD 5.2 Mastiff, in the Lodge; a Crag Wolf, an SRD 5.2 Wolf, in the Wolfstone). The hunt ride joins the two exits, and no loot lies in either exit or rest site. Brann the Charcoal-burner trades the longsword and greatsword at the forest gate, and Wenna the Drover the shield, chain shirt and chain mail at the drovers' camp (10 minutes a trade; the uncommon gear is allowed because the lowest recommended level is 4, and the owner left out ranged weapons). Each has a free topic. Brann's "the poachers' path" (Persuasion DC 14 or Intimidation DC 16) opens a hidden way to a poachers' hide (a Potion of Healing and Trapper's Tongs, a tool), and Wenna's "the sheep track" (Persuasion DC 13 or Intimidation DC 15) a hidden way to an old watchtower (a Knotted Rope, a tool, and a Potion of Healing); both rooms are avoidable, since the gate's harness never talks. The woodpile at the gate hides a Potion of Healing and a Hooded Lantern (a tool), and the drover's cart a Potion of Healing and a Crowbar (a tool). Its routes are the lodge hall and the Wolfstone's gate, and each route and region sweep holds one fight. The hall's trophy wall takes Perception (DC 15) or History (DC 13): a success reveals a topaz, a success by 5 or more a Potion of Healing as well, and a failure by 5 or more deals 1d6 piercing damage; it may be searched again once the character holds the Hooded Lantern. **Level 4–5 monsters (#288).** Five SRD 5.2 monsters, each name confirmed in SRD 5.2.1, join the Ogre: the Dire Wolf (Large Beast, AC 14, 22 HP), Brown Bear (Large Beast, AC 11, 22 HP), Bandit Captain (Medium Humanoid, AC 15, 52 HP, proficient in Strength, Dexterity and Wisdom saves), Owlbear (Large Monstrosity, AC 13, 59 HP) and Warrior Veteran (Medium Humanoid, AC 17, 65 HP). Their bands follow the estimator (`npm.cmd run estimate -- --levels 4-5 <id>`), one monster against one Fighter: the Dire Wolf and Brown Bear are an easy fight alone at levels 4–5 (94–100% survival), so they suit packs and pairs there; the Bandit Captain is about as dangerous as the Ogre (about 20% survival for the weakest character at level 4, over 95% at level 5); the Owlbear and Warrior Veteran kill almost every level-4 character and are a hard, boss-sized fight at level 5 (17–30% for the weakest character, 69–82% for the strongest), so their band is level 5 alone. Morale DCs: Dire Wolf 6, Brown Bear 5, Bandit Captain 6, Owlbear 4, Warrior Veteran 4. The Bandit Captain and Warrior Veteran carry gold; the beasts and the Owlbear carry nothing.

SRD 5.2 monsters left out because their blocks need what the engine lacks: the Hobgoblin Captain (its Aura of Authority is a 10-foot area), the Minotaur of Baphomet (10-foot reach, and a recharging Gore that depends on moving 10 feet straight at the target) and the Wight (Life Drain lowers maximum hit points, and it raises zombies).

**Omitted from the bestiary's stat blocks (#235).** Each was removed from the stat block, so the engine never meets it:

- the Ghoul's Multiattack (two Bite attacks in SRD 5.2) and its Bite's 1d6 necrotic damage: it keeps to its paralysing Claw, the Abandoned Delve's boss fight that #234 qualified;
- the Goblin Boss's Multiattack (two Scimitar attacks in SRD 5.2): the owner kept it to one attack, as it took the weakest survival of the module it then guarded from 77.5% to 22.5%, and its Redirect Attack reaction (no monster takes a reaction);
- the Hobgoblin Warrior's Longbow, the Bugbear Warrior's thrown Light Hammer and the Ogre's Javelin: ranged attacks are deferred;
- the Bugbear Warrior's Grab and its Abduct trait (the grappled condition is out of scope), its Light Hammer's advantage against a grappled target, and its 10-foot reach (no positions);
- the Kobold's Sunlight Sensitivity (the game does not model light), and the Gnoll Ravager's Rampage move of half its speed;
- the Dire Wolf's and Brown Bear's knockdown save (#288): SRD 5.2 knocks a Large or smaller target prone on any hit, with no save. A prone character spends its next turn getting up, so a no-save knockdown would cost a turn on nearly every hit; like the Wolf's, each is a derived house rider with a DC 13 Strength save;
- the Mastiff's knockdown save (#291): SRD 5.2 knocks a Medium or smaller target prone on any hit; like the Wolf's, it is a derived house rider with a DC 11 Strength save;
- the Brown Bear's Multiattack makes one Bite and one Claw in SRD 5.2; the engine picks each of its two attacks by a die, so it may bite or claw twice (#288);
- the Bandit Captain's Pistol and the Warrior Veteran's Heavy Crossbow (ranged attacks are deferred), and both monsters' Parry reaction (no monster takes a reaction) (#288);
- every monster's skills, senses, languages, speed and gear: nothing in a fight uses them.

# Exploring

## Exploring

An adventure module is a set of rooms joined by two-way passages, laid out on a map of rooms (#455); there is no tactical grid and no movement speed. The character starts in the module's start room.

- **The map (#455).** Each room sits on one cell of a square grid, `"at": [x, y]` (x grows east, y south), on a floor (`"floor"`, 0 unless authored; up is positive). The module's author places every room by hand, and the validator keeps the layout readable (`src/map-5e.ts`): no two rooms on one cell of a floor; a passage on one floor is a straight run, horizontal, vertical or a 45° diagonal, with no room on a cell between its ends; passages on a floor don't cross; and a passage between floors is marked `"vertical"` (`stairs`, `ladder` or `shaft`) and joins adjacent floors only. The mapping tool, `npm.cmd run map` (#456), draws a module's or a layout sketch's map and checks it with the same code. It also warns, without failing, about a region whose rooms on a floor surround another region's room, a passage of three cells or more, and a passage of two cells or more whose description reads as a doorway or a step. [`docs/module-authoring.md`](module-authoring.md) is the authoring guide. The map lays out rooms only: a character is in one room at a time, with no position inside it.
- **The player's map (#457).** The browser's Map pop-up shows the map as fog of war, worked out from the rooms the session has visited (no save format changed): each room visited, and each way out of one that the room panel would show from there (a hidden passage once a band opens it), to a visited room or to a stub on the far room's own cell: from the character's room the stub is named, as its exit is; from any other room it is an unnamed "?". A way's door, trap found and closure are marked as on the room panel's exits; a stair, ladder or shaft is listed on each visited room it leaves, naming the room it reaches once visited or when it leaves the character's room. Choosing the room at the far end of a way from the character's room makes the same move as Go to, and is refused whenever Go to would be; choosing any other room shows its known-rooms record.
- **Directions (#458).** Each exit leads a way worked out from the map: up or down for a passage between floors, else the compass point (one of eight) from its room's cell to the far room's. The room panel lists it before the exit's name ("North: Press Room", "Down: Gear Hall"), heads the exit's row of Go and Sneak with it, and names Go, Sneak and Flee by it ("Go north to Press Room"). The AI DM's scene gives each exit its `direction`, and its move, flee and sneak tools list each exit with it ("press-room (Press Room, north)"). The prompt (v45) says there is no tactical grid but the rooms lie in directions: the DM describes the ways out by them, and moves through the exit a direction the player asks for leads, or, with none that way, says so. The known rooms' ways (#406) don't carry directions.

- **Moving.** The character goes through an exit to the room beyond. Entering a room whose fight has not been won begins it at once: initiative is rolled, and opponents who win it act first. In the middle of a fight it can't simply go: it may only flee (below), or leave while every foe left is turned (#341, D13). A room whose fight was won stays clear.
- **Fleeing a fight (#396, a house rule).** On its turn, with its action, the character may flee through any exit it could otherwise go through: a shut door, a closed way and a way not yet found are refused ("Door shut", "Way closed", "There is no way from here to there"), without a roll. Before it goes, each opponent still in the fight and able to act (not incapacitated, so not turned, asleep or paralysed) makes one attack against it, in initiative order, standing in for opportunity attacks without positions (D1): with Multiattack, one of its attacks, a die choosing which as on its turn. A hit may wait for Uncanny Dodge or a reaction spell as any other does; the flight goes on once it is answered. 0 HP from one of these attacks is a defeat in the fight's defeat ending, and the character never leaves. A Rogue that has taken Disengage this turn (Cunning Action, a bonus action) draws no attacks. Then it goes through the exit as a move does: a trap on the way springs, and the move counts (#395). The fight stays unresolved in its room, as a turned-away fight does (D13): its fallen stay fallen, its opponents that fled or surrendered stay gone (and count as driven off or spared if it is won later), and those still standing keep their hit points and are met again on coming back, their conditions and the character's effects lasting the fight ended. Foes don't follow (D2): pursuit needs monster movement. A wandering encounter fled (#335) is met again only if a later rest is interrupted, with the same wounds. Fleeing into an exit room offers leaving as usual, which ends the adventure in an escape. The fight bar's **Flee** row, on one line, offers a button for each exit the character could take, named by the room it leads to (its accessible name "Flee to Foot of the Stair"), and after them the attacks a flight would draw, the same for every exit: "Draws an attack from Giant Rat" for one foe, "Draws 2 attacks" for several (naming them in its tooltip), "Draws no attacks" once disengaged, or the reason every flight is refused now ("Action used"). An exit behind a shut door or a closed way gets no button. The AI DM's `flee` tool lists only those exits and says whose attacks the flight draws; a Rogue's `disengage` tool is offered beside it, and only while some exit is open to flee by. The card reads "You turn to flee: Giant Rat and Zombie each get an attack on you as you go.", then each attack, then "You get away from the Rat-Gnawed Cellar into the Foot of the Stair, leaving Giant Rat and Zombie behind. The fight is unresolved: they wait there if you come back, wounds and all, and the fallen stay fallen." A monster's own fleeing is morale (see Combat), not this.
- **Regions and moves (#395).** A module may group its rooms into **regions**, such as an upper and a lower level: a group of rooms with a display name, which the map shades. In such a module every room belongs to one region. The room panel shows the current room's region above its name, and the AI DM's scene names it; a module without regions shows none. The adventure session counts the character's **moves**: each time it goes through an exit into another room, sneaking in included, the count rises by one; a refused move, or a trap on the way that ends the adventure, adds none. The count is part of the session's state, so a reload replays it, and a trace records it after every turn. A region's wandering encounter table (#403, below) rolls on its count of moves; later increment 17 tickets hang rest limits, the known-rooms panel and the gate's region sweep on regions and the count.
- **Flags (#397).** A module may declare **flags**: named facts about the adventure session, such as "the portcullis is raised", that only ever turn on (D3: a door that opens and later shuts is two flags). The engine alone sets a flag, from what the character does: a feature examined (`sets` on the feature, whatever its check's band), an item taken or given (`sets` on the item), a room first entered (`sets` on the room; the start room's is set as the adventure begins), an encounter's outcome (`sets` on the encounter: `won`, `bypassed` for slipping past it unfought, `peaceful` for ending it peacefully, or `fled` for the character fleeing it), or a check's band (a `set` effect). Setting a flag again changes nothing. A passage may be open only once a flag is set (`openWhen`) or closed once one is (`closeWhen`), anywhere in the module, as a band's open and close effects are in one check: a pulled lever in one room raises a portcullis in another. A way a flag keeps shut or has closed is refused like a band-closed one ("Way closed"), its door and trap gone with it while it is closed; the room panel shows "The way is closed." and, once a flag opens it, "The way is open now.", and the AI DM's scene says "The way is closed." or "This way has opened." beside the exit. When an action sets a flag that opens or closes a way, its card says so, as a band's does ("The way between the Foot of the Stair and the Rat-Gnawed Cellar is open."). The flags set are part of the session's state, in the order set, so a reload replays them from the actions and a trace records them after every turn. No AI DM tool takes a flag: a player asking for one to be set changes nothing. Five AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite flags`, on _The Winch Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/winch-tunnels.json`) cover heaving the winch that raises a portcullis, going through once the way opened, a way still closed, a player asking for the portcullis to be up, and fleeing (#396) a fight in the rat cage, whose gate drops shut behind the character as it comes in: no way is open, so no `flee` tool is offered and the AI DM calls none. The balance harness plays flags through the real runtime: its route planner treats a way a flag opens as shut until the flag is set, and then goes first to the nearest room where something not yet done sets the flag for sure, exactly as the validator counts it (`sureFlagSetters` in `src/adventure-5e.ts`: entering the room, examining a feature, taking an item or ending its fight), even a room it has been through, such as one it slipped through unseen; there it examines the features and takes the items as it does everywhere. A flag only some bands of a check set is never planned for. A way a flag has closed is never planned through. Setting a flag uses actions the harness already plays (move, examine, take and fighting), so the played-actions table gains no kind. The Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped module to declare flags.
- **Encounters that appear, vanish or move with flags (#398).** An encounter may be in its room only once a flag is set (`presentWhile`: guards come once the alarm is raised), only until one is (`presentUntil`: they leave), or in another room once one is (`movesTo`: `{ "roomId", "when" }`, so defenders fall back, gather or take up an ambush); at most one of the three. Only an encounter not yet met goes where the flags place it: once the character has sneaked up on it, been lain in wait for, met its reaction, fought it, slipped past it or fled it, it stays where it was met, whatever flags are set after (the session's state remembers where, `metEncounters`). A moved encounter keeps everything else: its opponents, lurking, reaction, sneaking and XP, and what its opponents carry goes with it, so their bodies are searched where they fell. The room panel, the Sneak control (offered toward the room where a fight now waits) and the AI DM's scene all show the encounter where it is now. When a flag places a fight not yet met in the character's own room, its opponents come in at once ("Someone comes into the Foot of the Stair." and their descriptions) and the fight begins as one the character walked into would, after a reaction roll if it reacts, but with no one lying in wait and no one surprised; nothing comes into a room while a fight is under way there, while the character waits there unseen or while opponents react to it. The encounter whose fight wins the adventure, and the wandering encounter, never appear, vanish or move. No room may hold two encounters, whatever their flags: as one met stays, two could both be there. Reachability counts a fight a flag places only where it is sure to be met: one that moves once its new room is reached and either its flag is set for sure or its own room is reached too, one that appears once its room is reached and its flag is set for sure, and one that vanishes never. So when an essential room or every exit lies behind a flag only such a fight sets, the validator names the encounter ("encounter cellar-rat can cut off every exit: once flag alarm is set it moves to room den, where it may never be met, so flag rat-dead its fight sets is never set for sure."). The balance harness plays it through the real runtime, and its route planner places each fight where the flags set so far put it: a flag that moves a fight onto the required path makes it a fight on the path, which the cautious style must win, and the gate's survival rate counts it. No new action kinds.
- **Alarms (#401).** A module may declare an **alarm** (`alarm`: `{ "flag", "text", "fights"? }`): one of its flags, which only **noise** sets. Noise is a sneak into a fight that fails by 5 or more; a fight in one of the alarm's `fights` regions (each `{ "region", "round" }`, the round 1–10) that goes on past that round, whichever encounter it is (the wandering encounter's included); a check (a feature's, a door's force, pick or break, a trap's disarm, a topic's) that lands in a band marked `"noisy": true` (the dropped tray, the stuck door forced); or an opponent that flees a fight and survives it, counted when the fight is won or the character leaves it. The engine raises the alarm once, at the first noise, and remembers what raised it in the session's state (`alarm`: the sneak's encounter, the fight's encounter, room and round, the check and its band, or the fled opponent), so a reload replays it and a trace records it; later noise changes nothing, and noise made by the action that ends the adventure raises nothing. Nothing else may set the alarm's flag: no `sets`, `set` effect or use. What the alarm changes is authored with flag conditions, as any flag's are: an encounter that moves or appears (defenders gathered into one hall, an ambush laid by a door), a passage it closes (a barred gate), a topic withdrawn (a creature that will no longer talk). Once it is raised, the room panel shows the module's `text` under the room's description, in every room ("A gong booms below, and goblin voices shout. The tunnels are roused."), and the card of the action that raised it names the noise ("The alarm is raised by examining the Stacked Crates (failure by 5 or more). A gong booms below …"; "… by a sneak into the Rat-Gnawed Cellar that failed by 5 or more", "… by the fight in the Rat-Gnawed Cellar going on past round 2", "… by Giant Rat that fled the fight in the Rat-Gnawed Cellar and lived"), followed by any way it opened or closed. The result's `alarm` event is what a rule answering the alarm reads. The AI DM's scene says "The alarm is raised:" and the module's words; no tool raises or lowers it, and the prompt (v39) says the engine alone decides it. Four AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite alarm`, on _The Alarmed Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/alarm-tunnels.json`) cover a player asking to raise the alarm, asking for it to be quiet again, asking what the noise was, and taking the crawl round the grate it closed. Reachability never counts the alarm as set: a way only it opens is never counted on, and a way it closes is a closer, refused when it can cut off an essential room or every exit (a passage the alarm closes can't be the only way). The balance harness meets the alarm through the real runtime: its noise comes from actions the harness already plays, the route planner goes round a way it has closed, and the avoid-optional style, where a flag has closed the way through the required rooms, goes round by any room. No new action kinds. The Coldwater Weirgate's weir bell (#412) is the first shipped alarm.
- **Using an item on a feature (#400).** A feature may accept items the character carries (`uses`: up to four, each `{ "item", "text", "effects", "consumes" }`, one per item): fitting the missing stone snake into a door, pouring oil on a coffin, throwing meat to a chained beast. Each use has the module's words, may use the item up (`"consumes": true`), and has 1–6 effects drawn from a band's: a discovery, an item revealed, damage (which can end the adventure in its defeat), a passage opened (a hidden one) or closed, or a flag set. The browser offers **Use … on …** in the feature's row of the action bar, beside Examine, for each item the feature accepts that the character carries ("Use Stone Snake", named in full "Use Stone Snake on Stone Door"); the AI DM has a `use_on` tool listing only the offered pairs, each `feature:item`. A use takes no roll. Its card gives the words, then "The Stone Snake is used up." when it is, then its effects as a band's card does. Each use is made once and remembered in the session's state (`featureUses`), so its revelations follow from it after a reload; a kept item's use stays in the bar, disabled ("Already used"). The engine refuses, without effect, a use in a fight ("Not while you are fighting."), on a feature not here, of an item the character doesn't carry ("You don't carry that."), of an item the feature doesn't accept ("The Potion of Healing is no use on the Stone Door."), and one already made. The balance harness uses an item on a feature as soon as it carries one the feature accepts, with the room's examinations, before it leaves the room (`use-on`, marked played in the harness's played-actions table); its route planner treats a hidden way only a use opens as shut until a use has opened it, fetches the item first and then goes to the feature, as it fetches a key, so the gate's required path goes by the item's room. Three AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite items`, on _The Snake Door_, `adventures/eval/snake-door.json`) cover the right item, the wrong item and an item not carried; prompt v37.
- **Wandering encounter tables by region (#403).** A region may have a **wandering encounter table** (`wandering`: `{ "chance", "every"?, "entries" }`): a chance from 1 to 100, and 1 to 6 entries, each an **omen** (`{ "omen" }`, words only: a distant chain, wet footsteps) or a wandering encounter (`{ "encounterId", "omen" }`, an encounter in no room, with the words it becomes once it has come). The engine rolls the table, a d100 from the session's seeded stream, in three cases: every `every` moves (1–20) within the region (the session counts the moves that end in each such region, `regionMoves`; without `every`, moves never roll); on each short or long rest in the region, before the rest; and when the alarm (#401) is raised, the table of the region the character is in as it is raised. At the chance or less an entry comes, and the same d100 picks it: the entries share the chance equally in order (chance 30 with three entries: 1–10 the first, 11–20 the second, 21–30 the third), so the chance must be at least the number of entries. A wandering encounter that comes begins its fight where the character is, as the wandering encounter of #335 does (its opponents carry nothing and never surrender; its fight is credited once, and losing it ends the adventure in its defeat ending); it **comes at most once** (the session remembers it, `wanderersCome`, even if the character flees its fight) and from then on is its omen. An omen is said and changes nothing, and may come again and again. On a rest, a fight that comes **interrupts** it: the rest restores nothing and isn't counted, as in #335; an omen, or nothing, lets the rest go on. A move whose roll falls due but that meets a fight (or a foe unseen or reacting, or a Tactical Mind offer) loses that roll; the next falls due `every` moves on. The alarm's roll waits instead: raised in a fight, it is rolled as soon as the character is out of it (won, fled or slipped away), still with the table of the region where it was raised (`wanderingDue`). The card shows the d100 ("You listen as you go: d100 37, 100 or less: Far above, a chain clinks against stone, then is still.", "The alarm carries through the dark: d100 40, 100 or less: Prowling Rat comes upon you.", "… over 10: nothing stirs."). Wandering encounters count against a module's encounters, whose cap rises from 20 to 30 (D5). The AI DM's scene says what last wandered into the room ("An omen, heard here: …", "A wandering encounter came upon you here: Prowling Rat."), until the character moves on; no tool summons, rolls for, delays or keeps away a wandering encounter, and the prompt (v40) says the engine alone rolls for them. Four AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite wandering`, on _The Wandering Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/wandering-tunnels.json`) cover a player trying to draw one out, asking for a promise that none will come, asking to skip a roll, and asking what the omen was. The balance harness meets wandering encounters through the real runtime, as part of the moves, rests and alarms it already plays (no new action kinds): their fights count in survival on seeded and always-fail checks like any other, and their XP in the XP limit; the gate reports, for a module with region tables, how many judged runs met one and fell in one ("Wandering encounters, reported (not judged): …"). The Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped module with region tables.
- **Long modules (#404).** A module with two or more regions may declare itself **long** (`"long": true`). A long module counts rests **per region** rather than per adventure, a house rule for a module too big for two short rests and one long rest: the character may take two short rests and one long rest in each region, the long one only at a rest site, so every region of a long module must have a rest site. The session remembers the rests taken in each region (`regionRests`, by region id), so a reload replays them and a trace records them; it still counts the adventure's rests in all (`shortRests`, `longRests`), which the harness's metrics report. A rest is refused at the region's limit ("You have taken the two short rests each region allows: rest again in another region."), and allowed again in a region where the character hasn't used its rests; going back to a region doesn't give its rests back. The rest cards, the character column's rest parts, the AI DM's character status and its `rest` and `long_rest` tools say the rests left "in this region" ("Short rests: 1 of 2 left in this region."). A long module has twice the treasure budget for its maximum level (see Treasure) and an XP limit one level higher (see the balance gate's XP check), D6. The career simulation places a long module in its order like any other, by its minimum recommended level. The Coldwater Weirgate (#412) is the first shipped long module.
- **Known rooms (#405).** The session remembers the rooms the character has been in (`visitedRoomIds`, the start room first, each once) and the fights it has fled (`fledEncounterIds`), so a reload replays them and a trace records them. The engine projects the **known rooms**: every room visited, and every room seen through a visited room's exits (a hidden way only once opened), grouped by region in the module's order (one group in a module without regions), the current room marked. A visited room has a record, as the engine sees it now: the discoveries made there (features and bodies examined), the creatures talked to and what each said, the doors on its open ways (open or shut) and the traps found or sprung on them, and its fight, once met: under way, won, ended peacefully, slipped past, fled from or still waiting (left with its foes turned, or placed there by a flag after the character met it). A room only seen has its name alone. The browser's **Known rooms** panel, after the room and the fight, lists them as small buttons in a row per region, after the region's name, each marked "(you are here)", "(visited)" or "(seen)" (a visited room went unmarked until the owner asked for the mark, 14 October 2026, as a room left behind looked forgotten); choosing one shows its record below (choosing it again hides it) and never moves the character. No record shows until a room is chosen, since the room panel already shows the current room, and none is chosen after a reload. The browser applies no rule of its own. The AI DM sees them in its scene (#406).
- **Known rooms in the AI DM's scene (#406).** The AI DM's scene has a line for every known room but the character's own (which is the scene's room), in the known rooms' order: a room only seen is "Smugglers' Den (Lower Tunnels): seen, not yet visited."; a visited one gives its ways on as they stand now, each marked "(closed)", "(opened)" for a hidden way a check or a use opened, or "(open now)" for a way a flag opened, then its record: each discovery ("Discovered at the Rusted Lantern: …"), what each creature said ("Old Hermit said of the den: …"), its doors ("Doors: Iron Door to the Strongroom, shut."), the traps found ("Traps: Dart Trap on the way to the Offering Room, armed.") and its fight ("Fight: fled from, against Giant Rat."). The flags the character knows of are those it has seen work, so the summary shows them as the ways they opened or closed, and the alarm as the scene's alarm (#401); a flag's id is never shown, as a flag that moves an unmet fight or changes a topic elsewhere is nothing the character could know. The summary is capped at 4,096 bytes as JSON (`KNOWN_ROOMS_SCENE_BYTES`): over it, the rooms visited first lose their records first and keep their ways, then the rooms only seen are left out, then the rooms visited first. On the fixture long module with every room visited the whole scene stays under 8,192 bytes. The summary decides nothing: no tool takes a known room, `move` still goes only through the scene's exits and the engine refuses any other, and the prompt (v41) tells the AI DM to answer what the character remembers from it, to move one room toward a known room that is not an exit by the ways it gives, and to add nothing it doesn't hold. Three AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite memory`, on _The Remembered Tunnels_, `adventures/eval/memory-tunnels.json`) cover recalling a discovery made in another room, what a creature said, and heading back toward a known room through the right exit; a case may name words its reply must use (`recalls`), without which its narration check fails.
- **The AI DM in the release module (#413).** Seventeen AI DM evaluation cases (thirteen until #443, which added opening Larrow's trade only when her band offers it, and sneaking past the sleeping bandits and back) (`--suite weirgate`, `FIFTH_WEIRGATE_DM_CASES`) play _The Coldwater Weirgate_ as it ships, with Ada at level 3 (D7; `FIFTH_WEIRGATE_DM_CHARACTER`), each from a seed: fleeing Brindle's fight to the yard, and not fleeing when the player asks to go untouched (D1); turning the drain gear with the keeper's crank, and calling nothing when the player jams a mace into it; breaking the Iron Grate when asked, which on its seed fails by 5 or more and rings the weir bell; saying that alarm, and refusing to quiet it; dropping the counterweight on Grisk by Athletics or by Sleight of Hand, as the player's words pick out, and refusing the lock paddles, which are in another room; saying the barge-horn omen heard; and, from the Pump Room in the Culverts, recalling what the keeper's ledger in the Keep said and heading back toward the sluice-house by the outfall. The live runs exposed two prompt gaps, fixed in prompt v42: refused with an item use_on doesn't list, the AI DM examined the feature instead, and asked to flee "so fast they don't get a swing", it fled. The prompt now says such a use calls no tool in its place, not examine nor use_on with another item, and that when the player makes going untouched part of a flee, the AI DM names the attacks and asks whether the character still flees, without calling a tool.
- **Moving only when asked to go (#425).** The live Abandoned Delve evaluation failed three cases on prompt v42: asked to grab the potion behind the guard post's weapon rack, or to sneak back to the broken gate and ambush whoever was there, the AI DM moved there (into the zombie fight, in the first), taking the move as a first step toward the request; and asked to slip past the zombie that had already noticed Ada, it fled. Prompt v43 says the AI DM calls move only when the player asks to go: asked to take, examine, search or do something in another room, even one through an exit, it says the room must be gone to first, without calling a tool, and moves nowhere in its place; moving one room toward a known room (#406) is for a request to go there, not to do something there; asked to sneak up on or ambush someone where sneak is not offered, it says there is no one to sneak up on and moves nowhere; and slipping, sneaking or creeping past a fight is not fleeing: the AI DM never flees in its place, and where flee lists that way, names the attacks the character would draw and asks whether it flees instead. One case changed: `clear-attack` asked to attack "with my sword", but the test Fighter wields a mace, and the AI DM sometimes corrected the player instead of attacking; it now says "with my mace" (owner-approved).
- **Fight actions: using the room in a fight (#402).** A feature may offer a **fight action** (`fightAction`: `{ "once", "check", "bands" }`), the single-player stand-in for teamwork against a big monster: toppling a statue onto an opponent, cutting the chain of a hanging cage, pushing a foe into a chasm. On the character's turn, with its action, it makes the action's check (one approach, or 2–4 as `{ "approaches" }`, each a skill or an ability and a DC only) against one opponent still in the fight, and the band's effects land on that opponent: `{ "type": "damage", "dice", "sides", "modifier", "damageType" }`, dealt as a spell's damage is (its resistances, Undead Fortitude, its fall and its side's morale check); `{ "type": "condition", "condition", "turns" }`, prone, poisoned or paralysed for that many of its turns with no saving throw, through the engine's own condition rules (prone always 1: it gets up on its next turn); or `{ "type": "remove" }`, which takes it out of the fight. A band has words, 1–2 effects of different types, or both; a removal stands alone; a band left out, or with only words, does nothing to the opponent, and an unauthored failure or success by 5 or more counts as plain failure or success. A removed opponent counts as defeated for the fight's XP ("Defeated Scarred Rat"), checks morale as a fall does, and leaves no body, so anything it carried is lost with it (D4; the issue's wording, over the proposal's "carrying its loot with it"), which is why the validator never lets a removal reach an opponent carrying a door's key or an item a use needs. Each fight action is used once per fight (`"once": "fight"`: once in each encounter fought in its room, such as the room's own and a wandering one at a rest site; fleeing and coming back is the same fight) or once per adventure (`"once": "adventure"`), and one that can remove an opponent must be once per adventure (D4: once per feature). Its check is rolled once from the seeded stream, never rerolled, and remembered with its band in the session's state (`fightActions`, with the removed opponents in `removedOpponents`), so a reload replays it; the character's poison (or fear) gives it disadvantage, as conditions give ability checks, and Tactical Mind and Guidance don't apply. The engine refuses, without a roll, a fight action outside a fight, on a feature not here or with none, one already used ("You already used the Leaning Statue in this adventure."), one with several approaches and none chosen, against an opponent not in the fight (fallen, fled, surrendered, or never there), and one when it isn't the character's turn or its action is spent. The browser shows a **Use the room** row in the Fight section with a button for each feature, opponent and approach ("Use Leaning Statue against Scarred Rat with Athletics", the approach under it), disabled with "Already used" once spent; the AI DM has a `use_feature` tool, offered only in a fight while the engine would accept it, listing each `feature:opponent` pair and the approaches each offers (prompt v38). Its card gives "You use the Leaning Statue against Scarred Rat.", the check, the band's words, then what it did ("The Leaning Statue hits Scarred Rat. Damage …", "Scarred Rat is knocked prone …", "Scarred Rat is out of the fight, defeated, and whatever it carried is lost with it."). The balance harness's judged styles never use fight actions, so the gate stays a lower bound; the reported _use-room_ style does (see Use the room, reported), and the gate's one-hit-kill measure, which weighs one attack, never counts them. Three AI DM evaluation cases (`--suite room`, on _The Statue Cellar_, `adventures/eval/statue-cellar.json`) cover toppling the statue onto an opponent, a target not in the fight, and a second use.
- **Features and topics that change with flags (#399).** A feature may have another description while a flag is set (`changed`: `{ "when", "description" }`): the altar after the ritual, the hall after the alarm. Its discovery and check are unchanged, so a discovery made before reads the same after. The room panel, the examine card and the AI DM's scene give the description as it is now. A creature's topic may be offered only once a flag is set (`offeredWhile`: the hermit talks about the crypt once the character has seen it) or only until one is (`offeredUntil`), or both. A topic not offered is in neither the action bar nor the AI DM's scene, and asking about it is refused ("There is no one here to ask about that."). A topic already asked and since withdrawn stays in the room panel with what the creature said, but can't be asked again or retried. A surrendered opponent hands over what it carries only through a topic's `gives` (#238), so such a topic must stay there to ask: it can't be withdrawn, and may wait only on a flag set for sure. As topics' checks may open hidden ways, reachability counts a topic's check only once it is surely offered: never one that can be withdrawn, and one that waits on a flag only once that flag is set for sure. All of it follows from the flags in the session's state, so a reload shows the same.
- **Examining.** Examining a feature (looking at, searching, reading, inspecting or opening it) shows its description and makes its authored discovery, if it has one. A discovery is made once and stays on the room panel; examining again repeats it and finds nothing new. An item hidden in a feature becomes visible when that feature is first examined. Visible and carried items can be examined too. Examining draws no dice, except that a feature may have a check (#281), made the first time it is examined: then its bands, not the examination, make its discovery and reveal what is hidden in it. Examining it again repeats what the band revealed and rolls nothing.
- **Ability checks.** A check is d20 + the ability's modifier, + the proficiency bonus when the character is proficient in the check's skill (twice with Expertise, #306), against the module's DC; it succeeds when the total meets the DC. A natural 20 or 1 is just its number. The Champion's Remarkable Athlete (level 3) gives advantage on Athletics checks. Each check is rolled once and its outcome remembered: trying again is refused, never rerolled, and the action stays in the bar, disabled, with the reason ("Already tried"). Every check site (forcing, picking or breaking a door, searching a room, disarming a trap, asking about a topic) goes through one check path (#280): one d20 from the session's seeded stream, a check event and its card, then the site's own event, and the outcome remembered under the site's id.
- **Graded checks (#281).** An authored check (a feature's, a door's force, pick or break, a trap's disarm, a topic's) grades its total into a band: **failure by 5 or more** (DC − 5 or less), **failure**, **success**, or **success by 5 or more** (DC + 5 or more). Each band the module authors may have words and effects; a failure or success by 5 or more that the module leaves out counts as plain failure or success, and a plain band it leaves out does only what the site always does (a door opens on a success, a trap is disarmed, a topic gets its reply or failure words). The effects are a **discovery** (a feature's authored discovery is made and shows on the room panel), an **item** (an item hidden in a feature is revealed, there to take; like other finds, treasure, coin and gear are found once per character, and a band never awards an item, only reveals it), **damage** (dice of an SRD 5.2 damage type, with no saving throw; at 0 HP the adventure ends in the effect's defeat ending), and a route **opened** or **closed** (#282): a hidden passage is no exit at all until a band opens it, and then it is an exit like any other, from both sides; a closed passage stays closed for the rest of the adventure, its door and trap are gone with it, and going through is refused ("Way closed"). The room panel marks a way a band opened ("A way you found.") and one it closed ("The way is closed."), and the AI DM's room description says which ways a check opened and which are closed. The band is remembered with the check, so what it revealed stays revealed after a reload. The card shows the roll, the band (the compact line shortens a band by 5 or more to "Failure by 5+" or "Success by 5+"), the band's words and each effect, a damage effect with its dice. The AI DM's tools return the band and its effects; its prompt says it narrates only those, and a tool call with any argument the tool doesn't list (such as a band or an effect of its own) is refused without a roll. A room search is one Perception roll for the whole room, so a trap's find DC is never graded.
- **Alternative approaches (#283).** An authored check may offer two to four approaches to one obstacle, each a different skill or ability with its own DC, such as Athletics (DC 12) or Acrobatics (DC 14) to get over a wall, or Persuasion or Intimidation to get past a guard. The bands and their effects belong to the obstacle, whichever approach is rolled. An Athletics or Strength approach may be marked `"movement": "climb"` or `"jump"` (#307), so a Thief makes it with Dexterity (Second-Story Work); marking any other approach is refused. Until one is rolled, the action bar offers one button per approach, its skill shown under it (the button is named in full, such as "Examine Crumbling Wall with Athletics"), and the AI DM's tool for that action takes the approach, listing the ones offered (and null for any other target). An action that names no approach is refused with the choices ("Choose how"), and one naming an approach that isn't offered is refused ("Not offered"), without a roll. Once one approach is rolled, the obstacle's outcome is remembered: the others are withdrawn, one plain button is left (Examine rolls nothing more; Force, Talk and the rest say "Already tried" or "Already asked"), and asking again by any approach is refused, unless the module authors a retry (#284), which offers every approach again.
- **Retries (#284).** A check is never tried again unless its module authors a **retry**: **after a cost**, damage (dice of an SRD 5.2 damage type, with no saving throw) or a carried tool the module names, used up; or **after a changed circumstance**, once the character holds a named item, has made a named discovery or has won a named encounter. A retry is offered only after a failure (or failure by 5 or more), only while its condition holds: a cost retry after every failure while the cost can be paid, and a circumstance retry once for each change, so it is not offered when the circumstance already held at the last try. Its cost is paid before the roll; damage that drops the character to 0 HP ends the adventure in the cost's defeat ending, and the check is not made. The retry rolls the check again from the session's seeded stream, and its band applies on top of what earlier tries revealed (a discovery, item or opened way stays; a closed way stays closed). Once a check's success could only open ways that have all been closed, it offers no other try (#289): a bridge that fell is not there to cross again. A success that would also make a discovery or reveal an item keeps its retry. The site's other actions still refuse a check already made ("Already tried", "Already asked", and Examine rolls nothing): only the retry, its own button named "Try again" with its cost or circumstance under it (such as "Costs 1d4 bludgeoning damage", "Uses up the Iron Spike" or "Knotted Rope"), rolls again; a check with several approaches offers one retry per approach. The AI DM's tool for that check takes `retry` only while some target offers one, listing each and why; `retry: true` where none is offered is refused without a roll ("No other try"), so asking for another try changes nothing.
- **Circumstances (#284).** Each approach to a check may name circumstances that give it **advantage** or **disadvantage**: the character holds a named item, has made a named discovery or has won a named encounter, or a named flag is set (#397), or, with `not`, hasn't (fighting nearby gives disadvantage to listen). Each has an authored name, which the check's card gives as the source ("at advantage (Knotted Rope)"). They combine with Remarkable Athlete as SRD 5.2 says: any advantage and any disadvantage cancel, and several sources are still one advantage. They are judged when the check is rolled, after a retry's cost is paid. The AI DM cannot grant either: a tool call that adds an `advantage` argument is refused without a roll. A trap's find has none, as a room's search is one roll.
- **Tools (#284).** A module item of kind `tool`, such as a rope or an iron spike, does nothing by itself and weighs nothing: a circumstance or a retry's cost names it. It is not kept after the adventure.
- **Tactical Mind (#315).** Right after a level-2+ Fighter fails an ability check at any check site (forcing, picking or breaking a door, searching a room, disarming a trap, asking about a topic, examining a feature), while it has a use of Second Wind left, the browser offers **Tactical Mind: add 1d10** beside the other actions and the AI DM a `tactical_mind` tool. The engine rolls only the d10 and adds it to the check's total; it never rerolls the d20. The check is graded again on the new total, and the new band is remembered in place of the old one, so asking again is still refused. The site's outcome follows the new total (the door opens, the search finds the traps whose DC it meets, the trap is disarmed, the creature answers). When the band changes, the new band's words and effects apply; what the failure already did, such as its damage, stands (a lesser failure deals no damage again), and anything the old band revealed or closed follows the new band instead. The use of Second Wind is spent only if the check now succeeds. The offer lasts until the next action: any other action, accepted, takes it away. It is not offered after a saving throw, a sneak, a lurk, a parley or Hide in a fight, which act on their result at once. The card reads "Tactical Mind: you add 1d10 to the Athletics check. 8 + 4 = 12 against DC 12. Success: a use of Second Wind is spent (1 of 2 left)." The balance harness never uses it: it keeps every use of Second Wind for healing, so the gate stays a lower bound.
- **Saving throws.** A saving throw is d20 + the ability's modifier, + the proficiency bonus in the class's saves (the Fighter's Strength and Constitution, the Rogue's Dexterity and Intelligence), against the DC. Only traps call for one so far.
- **Doors.** A passage may have a door. A stuck door opens with **Force**, a Strength (Athletics) check. A locked door opens with **Pick**, a Dexterity check with thieves' tools (see Thieves' tools), **Break**, the check the module names, or **Unlock**, offered while the character carries the door's key, with no roll. Each approach is tried once; an opened door stays open from both sides. Going through a shut door is refused ("Door shut").
- **Traps.** A passage may have a hidden trap. In a module with traps, every room offers **Search**, one check per room: Wisdom (Perception) to spot a trap or Intelligence (Investigation) to work out where one must be, whichever the character is better at (Perception on a tie, #309). It is one button, not two approaches: the engine chooses the better skill, as no player would choose the worse one. Every search shows the same DC, the lowest find DC of any trap in the module, so the DC never gives a trap away; the search finds each hidden trap on the room's exits whose own find DC the total meets. A found trap shows on its exit and offers **Disarm**, a check tried once; a failed disarm leaves it armed. A module may author a disarm with thieves' tools (see Thieves' tools). Going through an armed trap, found or not, springs it once: a saving throw, then its damage, halved (rounding down) on a success. A trap that drops the character to 0 HP ends the adventure in the trap's defeat ending.
- **Thieves' tools (#309).** SRD 5.2's thieves' tools (25 gp) pick a lock or disarm a trap. **Pick** is a Dexterity check with them, and a module may author a trap's disarm the same way, alone or as one of its approaches; the proficiency bonus is added only when the character's class is proficient with them (the Rogue). The check is offered only while the character carries a set: without one the Pick button and the `pick_lock` tool are not offered, a disarm offers only its other approaches, and asking anyway is refused with the ways still open named: "You carry no thieves' tools, so you can't pick the Iron Door's lock. You could break it open or find its key." A tools check is remembered like any other. The shipped traps (the Abandoned Delve's loose step, the Coldwater Weirgate's two bell-wire deadfalls and the Thornwood Lodge's man-trap) offer thieves' tools or a bare-handed Dexterity check at the same DC (agent-made choice, #309), so a Fighter disarms them as before and a Rogue adds its proficiency.
- **Talking.** A room may hold creatures to talk to, each with authored topics. **Talk** asks about one topic: the creature answers with its authored words, or, for a topic with a check (such as Charisma (Persuasion)), its success or failure words. Each topic is asked once, and the room panel keeps what the creature said.
- **Taking.** A visible item is taken and carried. Taking is refused for anything not visible.
- **Potions of Healing.** Drinking a Potion of Healing restores 2d4 + 2 HP, and a Potion of Greater Healing (#239) 4d4 + 4, never above maximum HP, and uses it up. Outside a fight it is drunk at once; in a fight it takes the bonus action, so it competes with Second Wind. Administering it to another creature is not used, as the party is one character.
- **Not during a fight.** Moving, examining, taking, opening doors, searching, disarming and talking are refused until the fight is won. In a fight a character can only drink a potion, take the combat actions or flee.

The browser's status strip shows the character's HP, an HP bar and its health: healthy, bloodied at half HP or fewer (the SRD 5.2 Bloodied), critical at a quarter or fewer, and defeated at 0. The engine projects the health; the browser applies no rule of its own. In a fight the strip adds the round, whose turn it is, and a pip for each Action (two after Action Surge), the Bonus action and the Reaction; in and out of fights it shows a pip for each Second Wind, Action Surge and Channel Divinity use, and a line with the character's AC (naming the armour and shield worn) and attacks from its gear as it stands. The room panel shows the room (under its region's name, in a module with regions, #395), the exits, the features with any discovery made, and the visible items, leaving out each empty list (a room with no exits, features or items says so in one line); in a fight the panel stays open, below the Fight section and its initiative table. The room panel also shows each exit's door (shut or open) and a found or sprung trap, and each creature with what it has said. The action bar shows the name of each exit, feature, item, carried potion, door, room, found trap and topic with a **Go**, **Examine**, **Take**, **Drink**, **Force**, **Pick**, **Break**, **Unlock**, **Search**, **Disarm** or **Talk** button, whose accessible name is the full action, such as "Examine Rusted Lantern" or "Force Swollen Door". The history shows each check and saving throw as its own roll group: the die, the ability modifier, the proficiency bonus and the DC, with a Success or Failure tag. The server projects whether the engine would accept each one now; one it would refuse stays in place, disabled, with the reason as visible text linked to the button (a potion at full HP says "Full HP"). After an action, focus stays on the clicked button while it is still enabled, and otherwise moves to the newest conversation-history entry, never to an unrelated action.

**Layout (#432).** The adventure screen has three columns from 1500 px wide, up to 1880 px in all: the character (the status strip, "You carry" and the rests) on the left (owner decision, 14 October 2026), the scene (the room, the fight and the known rooms) and the action bar with the history. Tab order follows character, scene, then actions and history. Narrower, it keeps two, up to 1240 px: the scene with the character's lists under it and the status strip held at the top, and the action bar with the history; there "You carry" and the rests come after the scene on screen but before it in tab order. "You carry" groups what the character carries under Worn and wielded, Consumables (potions, spell scrolls, wands, arrows and bolts), Treasure (gems and art objects) and Other (stowed gear, keys and tools), a line per entry with its icon, name and state ("Worn", "In hand", "Stowed") and its verbs on the right, a disabled verb's reason beside it. An entry's description opens from its name, and shows on hover. Treasure starts collapsed when it holds more than four entries; what the player opens or closes stays so until the next adventure.
